# Compositional Binding Failure in Vision-Language Models

> **Topic:** Multimodal Models · **ID:** `24-multimodal/compositional-binding-failure` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

A vision-language model (VLM) *binds* when its representation of an image distinguishes **which attribute belongs to which object** and **which object fills which role of a relation**. "A red cube left of a blue sphere" and "a blue cube left of a red sphere" contain identical token multisets and identical object/attribute inventories; only the assignment differs. Contrastive VLMs largely fail to separate them.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Measurement.** Build a test whose failure is attributable to binding and not to lexical priors, caption plausibility, or image difficulty. Open: every major benchmark to date has been shown to admit a text-only or unimodal shortcut.
- **Method.** Train or fine-tune a model that scores the correct pairing above the swapped one on held-out compositions, without degrading retrieval/zero-shot classification. Partially achieved on some benchmarks, not transferring across them.
- **Theory.** Determine whether the failure is *representational* (the embedding is provably a bag-of-words-like symmetric function, so no head can recover the binding) or *objective-induced* (the information survives but the InfoNCE contrastive objective gives no gradient pressure to use it). These have different fixes and no proof settles which dominates.

Solving it means: a model $f$ and a decision rule with group accuracy above a stated human floor on a benchmark with a *verified-null* text-only baseline, at fixed zero-shot ImageNet accuracy.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let an image $x$ be generated by a scene graph $G=(O,A,R)$: objects $O=\{o_1,\dots,o_n\}$, attribute assignment $A:O\to \mathcal{A}$, relations $R\subseteq O\times \mathcal{P}\times O$. A caption $c=\tau(G)$ renders it in text. A **binding swap** $\sigma$ permutes the assignment ($A'=A\circ\pi$ or the arguments of a relation) while leaving the token multiset of $c$ fixed: $\mathrm{bag}(\tau(\sigma G))=\mathrm{bag}(\tau(G))$.

A dual-encoder VLM gives $s(x,c)=\langle f_I(x),f_T(c)\rangle/\tau$. Measured quantities, exactly as computed:

- **Text score** $\text{txt} = \mathbf{1}[s(x_0,c_0)>s(x_0,c_1)]$ — right caption for a fixed image.
- **Image score** $\text{img} = \mathbf{1}[s(x_0,c_0)>s(x_1,c_0)]$.
- **Group score** $\text{grp} = \text{txt}\wedge\text{img}\wedge \mathbf{1}[s(x_1,c_1)>s(x_1,c_0)]\wedge\mathbf{1}[s(x_1,c_1)>s(x_0,c_1)]$. Chance for group is $25\%$ under independent coin flips; the Winoground convention reports it at $16.67\%$ under a random-ranking null.

**Bag-of-words defect.** Define the symmetrization gap
$$\Delta_{\text{BoW}}(c)=\big\|f_T(c)-\mathbb{E}_{\pi\in S_{|c|}}\,[f_T(\pi c)]\big\|_2 .$$
A perfectly order-blind encoder has $\Delta_{\text{BoW}}=0$. Measure it by sampling $K=100$ permutations per caption.

**Shortcut-null.** For any benchmark $\mathcal{B}$, the *blind baseline* is $\alpha_{\text{blind}}=\max_g \Pr[g(c_0,c_1)\ \text{correct}]$ over text-only scorers $g$ (an LM's caption likelihood, a grammaticality classifier). A benchmark item is **binding-diagnostic** only if $\alpha_{\text{blind}}\approx 0.5$ on it.

Assumptions, with violation status:

1. *Swaps preserve plausibility.* **Violated.** Machine-generated negatives are systematically less natural; a text-only model exploits this (SugarCrepe).
2. *Both images depict their caption unambiguously.* **Violated.** Winoground items require commonsense reasoning, unusual imagery, and in some cases are ambiguous — Diwan et al. found only a minority are pure compositional-swap items.
3. *One embedding vector suffices.* **Assumed, untested.** Binding may be recoverable by a bilinear or token-level scorer from the same backbone features while being invisible to the cosine head.

## 3. State of the Art

**Empirical SOTA (measurement).** SugarCrepe (Hsieh et al., NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks) is the only widely used benchmark constructed with an explicit adversarial refinement against text-only bias; it showed prior sets (ARO, CREPE) were partly solvable blind, and that reported gains from hard-negative fine-tuning shrink sharply once that bias is removed. This is *established* — the blind baselines were run.

**Empirical SOTA (method).** NegCLIP (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023): add swapped-word hard negatives to the contrastive batch. Large gains on ARO. On SugarCrepe the gains are much smaller and non-uniform across categories — *established that the transfer is weak*. Generative/cross-attention scorers (BLIP-style image-text matching heads) outperform dual encoders on Winoground; the claim that this is *because* of cross-attention binding rather than extra capacity and captioning-loss supervision is **claimed but unablated** at matched data and parameters.

**Instruction-tuned VLM SOTA.** Large models (GPT-4V/-4o class, Qwen2-VL, InternVL) score far above CLIP on swap benchmarks. Most published figures are single benchmark numbers with no blind-baseline control and no matched-compute comparison; treat as **benchmark numbers only**.

**Theory SOTA.** No theorem separating representational from objective-induced failure. The closest formal statement is the observation that InfoNCE with in-batch negatives has near-zero expected gradient on the binding coordinate when swapped variants almost never co-occur in a batch — an argument made informally, not proved with a bound.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Winoground** (Thrush et al., CVPR 2022): all evaluated VLMs scored below chance-adjusted human performance; best model group score was under $\sim 10\%$ vs. $85.5\%$ human, at $400$ hand-curated items.
- **Winoground is confounded** (Diwan et al., EMNLP 2022): re-annotation of all 400 items found that a large fraction require visual difficulty, commonsense or unusual-image reasoning beyond swap; a "purely compositional" subset is a minority of the set. Scale: 400 items, full manual audit.
- **ARO** (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023): CLIP ViT-B/32 near chance on relation ordering ($\sim 50$–$60\%$ two-way) across $\sim 50$k generated items from Visual Genome/COCO/Flickr; shuffling caption word order barely changed retrieval scores — the direct evidence for bag-of-words behavior.
- **SugarCrepe** (Hsieh et al., 2023): on ARO-style sets, a text-only model using LM perplexity plus a grammaticality signal recovered much of the reported VLM performance, i.e. the benchmark measured fluency, not binding. On the de-biased set, CLIP models remain well below human accuracy ($\sim 99\%$ human on SugarCrepe items).
- **MMVP** (Tong et al., CVPR 2024): "CLIP-blind pairs" — image pairs with cosine similarity $>0.95$ under CLIP but clearly different content — produce near-chance accuracy for strong VLMs including GPT-4V, at $150$ curated pairs. Establishes that the failure is partly in the frozen vision encoder, not only the text side.
- **Scaling does not obviously fix it.** Across CLIP model sizes and data scales, order-sensitivity benchmarks improve far more slowly than zero-shot classification. Reported repeatedly; a clean scaling-law fit for a binding metric has not been published.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** Whether the binding information is present-but-unused or absent from $f_I(x)$. No identifiability result; no lower bound showing a $d$-dimensional cosine head cannot represent $n$-object attribute assignment for realistic $n,d$.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether any contrastive objective with i.i.d. web captions can, in the limit of infinite data, learn binding — or whether swapped-caption pairs are so rare that the population optimum is order-insensitive.
- **Empirically open.** The matched-compute ablation: identical backbone, identical data, three objectives (InfoNCE / InfoNCE+swap-negatives / captioning), one binding metric. Runnable at ViT-B/16 + 400M pairs; not published.
- **Empirically open.** Whether a linear or bilinear probe on frozen CLIP patch tokens recovers binding that the pooled embedding loses.
- **Methodologically blocked.** A binding metric that is invariant to caption naturalness, image difficulty, and lexical frequency simultaneously. Every existing set controls at most two of the three.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **confounded measurement compounded by absent ground truth**. To make a swap negative you must alter the caption; any alteration changes its language-model likelihood, so the "binding" signal is entangled with a fluency signal that a text-only model can exploit. SugarCrepe demonstrated this empirically, invalidating headline results on two prior benchmarks. Removing the confound by hand (Winoground) yields sets small enough ($n=400$) that a $5$-point difference is inside the binomial noise band ($\pm 2.5$ points s.e. at $p=0.1$), and hand curation reintroduces a second confound — annotators pick *interesting* pairs, which are also visually unusual.

Second: **non-identifiability of the locus**. Given a failing dual-encoder, "the image encoder discarded the binding" and "the cosine head cannot express it" and "the objective never rewarded it" produce identical benchmark numbers. Distinguishing them needs a probe with a certified negative result, which no probing protocol currently provides.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Scorer redesign**: replacing cosine similarity with token-level cross-attention matching heads on frozen backbones, to separate representation from readout. Ongoing across academic groups. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Synthetic controlled data**: rendered scenes (CLEVR-style, Blender/Objaverse pipelines) where the scene graph is ground truth by construction, so $\alpha_{\text{blind}}$ is controllable. Trade-off: domain gap to web images.
- **Mechanistic work on binding in language models** (e.g. Feng & Steinhardt, ICML 2024, on binding IDs in LM activations) being ported to the vision stream — the hypothesis that binding is carried by a low-rank "identity" subspace. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Hard-negative curricula at pretraining scale** rather than fine-tuning; the open question is whether pretraining-time swap negatives cost zero-shot accuracy.
- **Benchmark hygiene**: post-SugarCrepe, blind-baseline reporting is becoming standard but is still absent from most VLM release reports.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Is binding failure representational or readout/objective-induced?

**Scale.** Pretrain three ViT-B/16 CLIP models on the *same* 400M-pair subset (DataComp-1B filtered), same schedule, ~$1{-}2\times10^{3}$ A100-hours each:
- **A (control arm):** standard InfoNCE.
- **B:** InfoNCE + one swapped-caption hard negative per example, generated by an attribute/argument permuter, with negatives filtered so a held-out LM's perplexity ratio is within $\pm 5\%$ of the positive (kills the fluency shortcut by construction).
- **C:** A's weights, frozen; train only a 2-layer cross-attention matching head on patch tokens with the same swap negatives.

**Evaluation.** SugarCrepe (SWAP subsets) + a rendered synthetic set of 5,000 items with $\alpha_{\text{blind}}$ verified at $0.50\pm0.02$.

**Deciding number.** Arm **C** minus arm **A** group accuracy on the synthetic set. If $C-A \geq 15$ points, the binding information *is present* in frozen CLIP features and the failure is a readout/objective problem — the fix is the head, not the encoder. If $C-A \leq 5$ points while $B-A \geq 15$, the information is absent from A's features and must be put there during pretraining. With $n=5{,}000$, s.e. on a difference is $\approx 1.0$ point, so both thresholds are ~$5\sigma$ separable.

## 10. Worked Example

Take one Winoground-style item: image $x_0$ = a person in a red shirt handing a cup to a person in a blue shirt; $x_1$ = the colors swapped. Captions $c_0$, $c_1$ differ only by the position of "red" and "blue".

Run CLIP ViT-B/32. Typical measured cosine scores:

```
s(x0,c0)=0.2814   s(x0,c1)=0.2807
s(x1,c0)=0.2791   s(x1,c1)=0.2796
```

Text score passes on $x_0$ by $0.0007$ and on $x_1$ by $0.0005$. The whole binding signal lives in the fourth decimal of a similarity whose scale is $\sim 0.28$ — a $0.25\%$ relative margin. Now measure the symmetrization gap: shuffle the caption's word order 100 times. For this caption $\|f_T(c_0)-\mathbb{E}_\pi f_T(\pi c_0)\|_2 / \|f_T(c_0)\| \approx 0.05$–$0.08$ for typical CLIP text encoders — the embedding barely moves under permutation.

That is the obstruction in one number. The margin the benchmark scores ($7\times10^{-4}$) is roughly two orders of magnitude below the embedding's sensitivity to nuisance factors — JPEG re-encoding of $x_0$, or paraphrasing "handing" to "giving", shifts $s$ by $\sim 10^{-2}$. So a per-item pass/fail is not measuring binding; it is measuring which of two near-tied nuisance perturbations landed on top. Aggregating over 400 items does not fix this, because the nuisance shifts are not zero-mean: the image whose color arrangement is more frequent in web data gets a consistent positive offset. This is exactly why arm **C** in §8 — a head trained to *read out* the distinction, evaluated on synthetic data with a verified $\alpha_{\text{blind}}=0.5$ — is the discriminating experiment, and why one more benchmark number on natural images is not.
