# Bag-of-Words Behavior in CLIP-Style Objectives

> **Topic:** Multimodal Models · **ID:** `24-multimodal/bag-of-words-behavior-clip` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement

Contrastively trained image–text models (CLIP, SigLIP, and descendants) match an image to a caption largely by *which words appear*, not by *how they are composed*. Swap the nouns in "the horse is eating the grass" and CLIP's similarity barely moves.

Three variants, with different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a model $f$, produce a scalar that quantifies how much of its image–text matching decision is explained by an order-invariant (bag-of-words) function of the caption. Solving this means a metric that is not gameable by the distributional artifacts of the negative captions used to probe it.
- **Method.** Train an image–text model that keeps CLIP's zero-shot transfer and retrieval quality while ranking a correct caption above a word-order or attribute-binding perturbation of it at human-level accuracy ($\geq 95\%$ on clean benchmarks). Partially solved: hard-negative fine-tuning buys large gains on some probes, and the gains do not fully generalize.
- **Theory.** Prove, for the InfoNCE objective with a given negative-sampling distribution, whether the compositional (order-sensitive) component of the caption is identifiable at all — i.e. whether the loss gap between an order-sensitive and an order-invariant text encoder is bounded below by something that survives finite batch size and finite data. Open.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $(x, c) \sim \mathcal{D}$ be an image–caption pair, $c = (w_1,\dots,w_L)$ a token sequence. Encoders $g_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, $h_\phi: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$; similarity $s(x,c) = \tau^{-1} \langle g_\theta(x), h_\phi(c)\rangle$ with learned temperature $\tau$. The batch-$N$ InfoNCE loss is

$$\mathcal{L}_N = -\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\log \frac{e^{s(x_i,c_i)}}{\sum_{j} e^{s(x_i,c_j)}} + \log \frac{e^{s(x_i,c_i)}}{\sum_{j} e^{s(x_j,c_i)}}\right].$$

**Bag-of-words projection.** Let $\Pi$ be the set of permutations of a caption's tokens and $\bar{h}(c) = \mathbb{E}_{\pi \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\Pi)}[h_\phi(\pi c)]$ the order-averaged text embedding. Define the **order-sensitivity residual**

$$R(c) = \big\|h_\phi(c) - \bar{h}(c)\big\|_2 \in [0,2],$$

measured by sampling $K$ permutations (in practice $K=32$ is enough for $\pm 0.01$). $R$ is measured on held-out captions, not training captions.

**Decision-level bag-of-words gap.** For a triple $(x, c^+, c^-)$ where $c^-$ is a perturbation of $c^+$ with the same multiset of content words (order swap, relation swap, attribute re-binding),

$$\Delta(x) = s(x,c^+) - s(x,c^-), \qquad \mathrm{Acc} = \Pr_{(x,c^+,c^-)}[\Delta(x) > 0].$$

Chance is $0.5$. This is the quantity every benchmark in §3 reports; the entire measurement problem is that $c^-$ is generated by a procedure, and the procedure leaks.

**Plausibility control.** Let $q(c)$ be a text-only language-model score. A probe set is **valid** only if a text-only classifier using $q$ alone cannot exceed chance by much: $\Pr[q(c^+) > q(c^-)] \approx 0.5$. Report this number alongside $\mathrm{Acc}$; without it $\mathrm{Acc}$ is uninterpretable.

**Signal budget.** InfoNCE with batch $N$ upper-bounds the mutual information it can extract at $\log N$ nats. Let $p_{\mathrm{comp}}$ be the probability that a uniformly drawn in-batch negative $c_j$ has the same content-word multiset as $c_i$ but different composition. On web-scraped corpora $p_{\mathrm{comp}} \approx 10^{-5}$ or smaller; the expected number of compositional negatives per batch is $N p_{\mathrm{comp}} \approx 3\times10^{-2}$ at $N = 32{,}768$. Order sensitivity is therefore essentially unpriced by the loss.

**Assumptions known to be violated.** (i) Captions describe the image faithfully — alt-text often does not; (ii) in-batch negatives are true negatives — false negatives are common at large $N$; (iii) perturbed captions $c^-$ are equally fluent and equally plausible a priori — violated in ARO and CREPE (§3); (iv) each image has one correct caption — violated for Winoground-style minimal pairs.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (reproduced independently).**

- *ARO* (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023) showed CLIP ViT-B/32 near chance on relation ordering (VG-Relation ~59%, chance 50%) and weak on attribution (~63%). Their **NegCLIP** fine-tune — adding swapped-word text negatives plus nearest-neighbor image negatives — lifts VG-Relation to ~81% and VG-Attribution to ~71% without losing ImageNet zero-shot. Both the deficit and the fix replicate.
- *Winoground* (Thrush et al., CVPR 2022): all 2022-era VLMs at or below chance on the group score (CLIP ViT-B/32 ≈ 8%, chance 16.7%, human ≈ 85%).
- *SugarCrepe* (Hsieh et al., NeurIPS 2023) established that ARO and CREPE negatives are **distributionally broken**: a text-only model with no image access beats CLIP on them. On SugarCrepe's plausibility-debiased negatives, CLIP's apparent deficit shrinks substantially and NegCLIP's advantage shrinks with it. This is the single most important correction in the area.
- *Text-encoder bottleneck* (Kamath, Hessel, Chang, EMNLP 2023): the CLIP **text** encoder's representation, not the image tower, carries most of the failure — substituting text representations that do encode the distinction recovers much of the gap.

**Claimed but under-ablated.** Most hard-negative recipes (DAC, SVLC/Doveh et al. CVPR 2023, structured-caption fine-tuning) report gains on ARO-family benchmarks. Because those benchmarks are the ones SugarCrepe showed to be solvable without an image, the gains are *partly* attributable to learning the perturbation grammar rather than compositional grounding. Few papers report the text-only control on their own eval.

**Benchmark-number-only results.** Reported improvements on Winoground group score by later models (generative rerankers, captioner-augmented VLMs) are single benchmark numbers on 400 examples; Diwan et al. (EMNLP 2022) showed a large fraction of Winoground requires non-compositional abilities (unusual images, ambiguous captions, commonsense), so a Winoground delta is not a compositionality delta.

**Architectural SOTA direction.** Captioning objectives (Tschannen et al., *Image Captioners Are Scalable Vision Learners Too*, NeurIPS 2023) give a next-token loss that is order-sensitive by construction; CapPa outperforms comparable CLIP on some fine-grained tasks. Whether the captioning loss fixes the *binding* failure specifically has not been isolated.

## 4. What Is Known

- Scale alone does not fix it. Across OpenCLIP models trained on LAION-400M/2B with ViT-B through ViT-G, ARO-style order accuracy is roughly flat in parameters and data; ImageNet zero-shot moves by >15 points over the same sweep.
- Retrieval benchmarks do not require order. Shuffling word order in COCO/Flickr30k captions at test time leaves retrieval recall largely intact — the benchmark is solvable bag-of-words, so it never penalized the model (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023, at ViT-B/32 scale).
- The text-only ceiling: on ARO/CREPE, a language-model plausibility score alone reaches well above chance and above CLIP (Hsieh et al., NeurIPS 2023).
- Compositional structure is *partially present* but not used: Trager et al. (ICCV 2023) show ideal-word decompositions — attribute and object directions that add approximately linearly in CLIP's text space — meaning the representation carries more binding information than the matching decision exploits.
- Binding failure persists in controlled synthetic settings with two objects and two colors, where plausibility artifacts are eliminated by construction (Lewis et al., EACL 2024).

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No bound relates the InfoNCE optimum to compositional identifiability. Concretely: is there a $(\mathcal{D}, N)$ regime in which the order-invariant text encoder is *not* a global optimum of $\mathcal{L}_N$ — and how does the loss gap scale with $p_{\mathrm{comp}}$ and $N$? Currently only the informal $\log N$ argument in §2 exists.
- **Empirically open.** Does a from-scratch CLIP trained with 1–5% synthetic compositional hard negatives mixed into pretraining (not fine-tuning) at ≥1B pairs reach human-level SugarCrepe while preserving zero-shot? Runnable; not run at that scale in public.
- **Methodologically blocked.** A benchmark-free measure. $R(c)$ measures the encoder; $\mathrm{Acc}$ measures a benchmark's negative-generation procedure. There is no accepted measurement of "how compositional is this model" that does not inherit an adversary's biases.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The obstruction is **confounded measurement compounded by non-identifiability**.

1. *Confounded measurement.* Every probe needs a negative caption. Generating negatives by rule makes them detectable by text statistics; generating them by LLM makes them fluent but shifts the semantic distance distribution. SugarCrepe fixed the first failure mode and introduced calibration for it, but the fix is a debiasing filter — it removes examples the adversary can solve, which itself selects a non-uniform slice of composition space.
2. *Non-identifiability.* Under the training distribution, the bag-of-words solution is nearly loss-equivalent to the compositional one (§2, $Np_{\mathrm{comp}} \ll 1$). Nothing in the objective breaks the tie. Fixing it by adding negatives changes the objective, so "did the model learn composition or the negative generator?" is the same confound at training time.
3. Not compute: a ViT-B/32 CLIP on 400M pairs is a few thousand GPU-hours. The decisive experiment is affordable; the ambiguity is in what its number means.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Debiased and generative benchmarks.** SugarCrepe-style plausibility control is now standard practice; extensions to video and spatial relations (What's Up, Kamath et al. EMNLP 2023) target relation types absent from earlier probes.
- **Objective replacement.** Captioning and hybrid contrastive-plus-generative losses (CapPa lineage; sigmoid-loss SigLIP variants) as a structural fix rather than a data fix. *(frontier — verify)* Several groups report that adding an autoregressive caption head to a contrastive tower improves fine-grained binding at fixed compute; the ablations isolating binding from general caption quality are thin.
- **Text-tower interventions.** Replacing or distilling from strong text encoders, following the Kamath et al. bottleneck result.
- **Mechanistic work.** Locating binding in attention heads and testing whether attribute–object pairs are represented jointly or additively; connects to Trager et al.'s linear-space findings. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Is bag-of-words behavior a *data* property (negatives never price order) or an *objective* property (contrastive matching cannot use order even when priced)?

**Scale.** Train four ViT-B/16 CLIP models from scratch on the same 400M-pair subset, same schedule, same batch $N = 32{,}768$, ~1.5k A100-hours each.

**Arms.**
1. **Control:** standard CLIP.
2. **+Rule negatives:** 3% of batches carry one rule-generated swap negative per positive.
3. **+LLM negatives:** same rate, plausibility-matched negatives (generated then filtered so a text-only LM scores $c^+$ over $c^-$ at $50\pm2\%$).
4. **+Caption head:** control plus an autoregressive caption decoder, loss weight 0.5, no hard negatives.

**Evaluation.** SugarCrepe accuracy; a **held-out** compositional probe built by a *different* generator than arm 3 used; ImageNet zero-shot; COCO retrieval.

**Deciding number.** The held-out probe accuracy of arm 3 minus arm 1, with ImageNet zero-shot within 1 point of control. If $\geq +15$ points, bag-of-words behavior is a data/negative-pricing artifact and the contrastive objective is adequate. If $\leq +5$ points while arm 3 gains on its own generator's probe, the failure is objective-level and arm 4's number becomes the interesting one.

## 10. Worked Example

Take one image: a brown horse standing on green grass.

- $c^+$ = "the horse is eating the grass"
- $c^-_{\text{rule}}$ = "the grass is eating the horse" (ARO-style swap)
- $c^-_{\text{plaus}}$ = "the horse is standing beside the grass"

Measure on CLIP ViT-B/32:

```
cos(image, c+)          ≈ 0.31
cos(image, c-_rule)     ≈ 0.30      Δ ≈ +0.01
cos(image, c-_plaus)    ≈ 0.30      Δ ≈ +0.01
R(c+) = ||h(c+) - mean_π h(πc+)||  ≈ 0.2-0.3 (of a max of 2)
```

$\Delta \approx 0.01$ against a within-batch similarity spread of order $0.1$: the order swap moves the decision by ~10% of the noise floor. The model is not *blind* to order — $R > 0$, the encoder does distinguish permutations — it just does not *use* the distinction at the decision.

Now the obstruction. A text-only GPT-2 log-likelihood ranks $c^+$ above $c^-_{\text{rule}}$ with high confidence, because "grass eating a horse" is rare English. So a benchmark built entirely from $c^-_{\text{rule}}$-style negatives can be scored ~80% by a model that never sees the image — which is exactly SugarCrepe's finding. Fine-tuning CLIP on such negatives therefore has two available solutions: learn to ground the relation, or learn the perturbation grammar. Both produce the same benchmark number.

Against $c^-_{\text{plaus}}$ — equally fluent, differing only in the grounded relation — $\Delta$ stays at $\approx 0.01$. That is the finding: the deficit that survives plausibility control is smaller than ARO advertised and larger than zero, and no current method has been shown to close it on negatives it did not train against.
