# Learned versus Fixed Attention Masks for Causality

> **Topic:** Attention Mechanisms · **ID:** `02-attention/learned-vs-fixed-causal-masks` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

Decoder-only transformers use one hard-coded connectivity prior: the lower-triangular causal mask, which lets position $i$ attend to every $j \le i$ and nothing else. This encodes exactly one fact — the autoregressive factorization — and nothing about which earlier tokens actually matter. The question is whether a **learned** mask, one whose support is a trained parameter rather than a constant, beats the fixed triangle.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Method variant (efficiency).** Given a compute budget, does a learned sparse mask $M_\theta$ reach lower validation loss than the dense triangle at matched FLOPs? This is a horse race and is currently the only variant with large-scale evidence.
- **Measurement variant (structure).** If the data is generated by a known dependency graph $G$, does the learned mask's support recover $G$? Solving this needs a recovery metric that is invariant to the many masks that induce the same conditional distribution.
- **Theory variant (identifiability).** Is the generating graph identifiable from the loss-optimal mask at all, or does a positive-measure set of distinct masks attain the same minimum? If the latter, no amount of training data settles the measurement variant.

A solution to the method variant is a reproduced iso-FLOP win. A solution to the measurement variant is a recovery guarantee plus a metric that survives the degeneracies in §6. They are different problems and the literature scores the second by citing the first.

## 2. Formal Setting

Sequence $x_{1:T}$, $x_t \in \mathcal{V}$, drawn from $p^\star$. A mask is $M \in [0,1]^{T \times T}$; attention in head $h$ of layer $\ell$ is

$$A^{(\ell,h)} = \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}} + \log M^{(\ell,h)}\right), \qquad \log 0 \equiv -\infty .$$

The fixed baseline is $M^{\mathrm{causal}}_{ij} = \mathbb{1}[j \le i]$. A learned mask is any $M_\theta$ whose support depends on trained parameters: gate logits $z_{ij}$ with $M_{ij} = \sigma(z_{ij}) \cdot \mathbb{1}[j\le i]$ (hard-concrete / $L_0$ relaxation), a learned span $s_h$ with $M_{ij} = \mathrm{clip}((s_h - (i-j))/R, 0, 1)$, a top-$k$ block selector, or entmax-$\alpha$ with learned $\alpha$ (sparsity emerges from the normalizer rather than a separate mask).

**Measured quantities.**

- **Loss.** $\mathcal{L}(M) = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_t \log p_{\theta,M}(x_t \mid x_{<t})$, reported in bits/byte on a held-out corpus to keep tokenizers comparable.
- **Compute.** $C(M) = \sum_{\ell,h} \|M^{(\ell,h)}\|_0 \cdot (2d_k + 2d_v)$ FLOPs per sequence, plus the mask-selection cost. Measured, not counted: a mask with 10% nonzeros that cannot be tiled into $128\times128$ blocks runs slower than dense on an H100. Report wall-clock alongside $\|M\|_0$.
- **Density.** $\rho = \|M\|_0 / \binom{T+1}{2}$, averaged over heads and inputs.
- **Structural recovery.** With ground-truth parent sets $\mathrm{pa}(t)$, $F_1$ between $\bigcup_{\ell,h}\mathrm{supp}(M^{(\ell,h)}_{t,\cdot})$ (thresholded) and $\mathrm{pa}(t)$.
- **Iso-FLOP gap.** $\Delta = \mathcal{L}(M_\theta) - \mathcal{L}(M^{\mathrm{causal}})$ with $C(M_\theta) = C(M^{\mathrm{causal}})$ enforced by growing width or depth of the sparse arm.

**Assumptions, and which fail.**

1. *Attention support carries the model's information flow.* Violated — residual stream and MLPs move information around a masked edge; deleting an edge at layer $\ell$ leaves a two-hop path through layer $\ell+1$.
2. *The mask is input-independent.* Violated by every competitive method (top-$k$, LSH routing, MoBA), so $\rho$ is a distributional average and worst-case latency is set by the tail.
3. *Ground-truth $G$ exists.* Holds only on synthetic data. On natural language there is no verified parent set, so the measurement variant is untestable outside simulation.
4. *Discrete mask learning has a usable gradient.* Violated; all methods use a relaxation (Gumbel, hard-concrete, straight-through), and the relaxation bias is never separated from the mask's effect.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (ablated, reproduced).**
- *Fixed structured sparsity loses little at moderate scale.* Sparse Transformer (Child et al., 2019), Longformer (Beltagy et al., 2020) and BigBird (Zaheer et al., 2020) show hand-designed strided/local/global patterns retaining full-attention quality on long-context tasks. BigBird additionally proves the pattern is Turing-complete and a universal sequence-function approximator (NeurIPS 2020).
- *Learned spans beat fixed spans on compute.* Adaptive Attention Span (Sukhbaatar et al., ACL 2019) learns a per-head span and reaches character-level enwik8 quality comparable to a fixed 3072-token span with average learned spans in the low hundreds — an order-of-magnitude FLOP reduction, ablated against fixed-span controls at matched depth.
- *Attention sinks are load-bearing.* StreamingLLM (Xiao et al., ICLR 2024) shows that dropping the first few tokens from a sliding-window mask collapses perplexity, and re-adding 4 sink tokens restores it. This is a fixed-mask correction, not a learned one, and it beat every learned scheme available at the time.

**Claimed but not fully ablated.**
- *Trainable sparse attention matches full attention at frontier scale.* Native Sparse Attention (Yuan et al., 2025) and MoBA (Lu et al., 2025) both report learned block selection matching or exceeding full attention at long context on 10B+-parameter models. These are benchmark numbers from single training runs; there is no iso-FLOP-matched dense control at the same token budget, and no seed variance. Treat as existence proofs of viability, not as a measured $\Delta$.
- *Learned masks recover linguistic or causal structure.* Attention-derived structure claims (syntax heads, induction heads) are correlational. Jain & Wallace (NAACL 2019) show attention distributions are not uniquely determined by the prediction; Wiegreffe & Pinter (EMNLP 2019) show the counterargument depends on the exact claim being made. No paper has demonstrated a learned mask recovering a *known* generative graph on natural data.

**Theory SOTA.** Nichani, Damian & Lee (ICML 2024) prove that for a latent-causal-graph in-context task, gradient descent on a two-layer transformer drives the first-layer attention to encode the graph, under a specific data model. This is the strongest positive identifiability result and it is task-specific.

## 4. What Is Known

- Adaptive spans (Sukhbaatar et al. 2019): 12–24 layer character-level models, enwik8/text8; average span drops from a 3072-token maximum to roughly $10^2$ tokens with bits-per-character within noise of the fixed-span control.
- Head pruning (Michel et al., NeurIPS 2019): on trained WMT and BERT models, a large fraction of heads — over 40% in several layers — can be removed at test time with negligible BLEU/accuracy loss. Masks are massively redundant, so mask support is a weak signal about computation.
- StreamingLLM (ICLR 2024): 4 sink tokens plus a sliding window keeps perplexity stable out to $4\times10^6$ tokens on Llama-2-7B; removing them raises perplexity by orders of magnitude.
- Adaptively Sparse Transformers (Correia, Niculae & Martins, EMNLP 2019): learned $\alpha$-entmax yields genuinely zero-weight edges and interpretable head specialization at ~100M-parameter NMT scale, with translation quality on par with softmax.
- Every one of these is at or below ~7B parameters, or at ≤200M for the mask-learning results specifically.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open (the main gap).** No published run compares a learned mask against the fixed causal mask at *matched FLOPs, matched token budget, and multiple seeds* above ~1B parameters. Frontier results (NSA, MoBA) compare against dense attention at matched *parameters*, which advantages the sparse arm by giving it cheaper tokens. The experiment is runnable today on ~$10^5$ GPU-hours.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Structural recovery has no accepted metric. Because information routes through the residual stream, the union of head supports over-counts and any single head under-counts; there is no principled aggregation rule, and $F_1$ against $\mathrm{pa}(t)$ is therefore not comparable across architectures.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether the loss-minimizing mask is identifiable up to a well-characterized equivalence class, for any model class richer than the two-layer construction of Nichani et al. (2024). No proof either way.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Non-identifiability of the mask given the loss.** Fix a trained model and consider the set $\mathcal{M}^\star = \{M : \mathcal{L}(M) \le \mathcal{L}^\star + \epsilon\}$. Head redundancy (Michel et al. 2019) makes $\mathcal{M}^\star$ high-dimensional: dropping an edge in head $h$ is compensated by an edge already present in head $h'$. Multi-layer composition adds a second degeneracy: an edge $i \leftarrow j$ removed at layer $\ell$ is reconstructed as $i \leftarrow k \leftarrow j$ at layers $\ell,\ell+1$ for many intermediate $k$. So the mask that training selects is one point in a large flat set, chosen by initialization and relaxation bias rather than by the data's structure. Any claim "the learned mask found the causal graph" is a claim about which point in $\mathcal{M}^\star$ the optimizer happened to land on.

The second obstruction is the evaluation not measuring what it names: long-context benchmarks that a sparse mask "matches" are dominated by retrieval-shaped tasks whose answers depend on a handful of positions, exactly the regime where any reasonable sparse mask suffices. Matching on those says nothing about the dense-dependency regime.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Trainable block sparsity at scale.** DeepSeek (NSA) and Moonshot (MoBA) ship learned block selection in production long-context models; the open question they leave is the missing iso-FLOP control. *(frontier — verify the exact control arms in any 2026 follow-ups.)*
- **Hardware-aligned mask search.** Work constraining learned masks to block-tileable supports so that measured speedup tracks $\rho$; the gap between theoretical and realized sparsity is the reported bottleneck.
- **Mechanistic edge attribution** (Anthropic, Redwood-lineage groups): edge-level causal tracing / path patching as a substitute for reading masks directly. This is the most credible route around the §6 obstruction, since it measures effect rather than connectivity. *(frontier — verify.)*
- **Synthetic causal-structure benchmarks** extending Nichani et al. (2024) to deeper models and larger graphs.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Scale.** Two 1.3B-parameter decoder-only models, 100B tokens, 32K context, 4 seeds each.

**Arms.**
- *Control:* fixed causal mask, dense attention, standard config.
- *Treatment:* learned per-head block mask ($128\times128$ blocks, hard-concrete gates, $L_0$ penalty tuned so $\rho \approx 0.15$), with width increased so that **measured** training FLOPs match the control to within 2%.

**Data.** 95% natural corpus, 5% a held-out synthetic mixture generated from known sparse dependency graphs over $T=32{,}768$, so structural recovery is checkable in the same run.

**Deciding number.** The iso-FLOP validation gap $\Delta = \mathcal{L}(M_\theta) - \mathcal{L}(M^{\mathrm{causal}})$ in bits/byte on the natural held-out set, with a seed-variance error bar. Decision rule: $\Delta < -0.005$ bits/byte at $p<0.05$ across 4 seeds means learned masks beat the triangle at scale; $|\Delta| < 0.005$ means the triangle is not the bottleneck and the field should stop reporting mask quality as a quality result. Secondary readout: parent-recovery $F_1$ on the synthetic slice, reported per-head *and* as head-union, with the spread between the two stated — that spread is the size of the measurement problem.

## 10. Worked Example

Generate sequences of length $T = 64$ from a sparse structural equation model: each $x_t$ depends on exactly two earlier positions, $\mathrm{pa}(t) = \{t-1, \pi(t)\}$ with $\pi(t)$ a fixed random index in $[1, t-2]$. Vocabulary 64; $x_t = (x_{t-1} + x_{\pi(t)}) \bmod 64$ with 5% label noise.

Counting:
- Fixed causal mask: $\binom{65}{2} - 64 = 2016$ edges.
- True graph: $2 \times 62 = 124$ edges. Density $\rho^\star = 124/2016 = 6.2\%$.
- Entropy floor from the 5% noise: $H = 0.05\log_2 63 + H_b(0.05) \approx 0.586$ bits/token.

Train a 2-layer, 4-head, $d=128$ transformer with hard-concrete gates on every edge, $L_0$ coefficient swept over $\{10^{-4}, \ldots, 10^{-1}\}$, 8 seeds. Expected outcome, and the point of the example:

| $L_0$ coeff | $\rho$ | val. bits/token | parent $F_1$ (head-union) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10^{-4}$ | 0.71 | 0.59 | 0.16 |
| $10^{-3}$ | 0.23 | 0.59 | 0.41 |
| $10^{-2}$ | 0.071 | 0.60 | 0.55 |
| $10^{-1}$ | 0.019 | 0.94 | 0.38 |

Loss is flat at the entropy floor across a 10× range of density — every mask in that range is loss-equivalent — while $F_1$ moves from 0.16 to 0.55 and back down. And across the 8 seeds at $10^{-2}$, the recovered edge sets agree with each other on only about half their union, even though all 8 hit the same loss to within 0.005 bits.

That is the obstruction in one table. The loss cannot rank the masks, so the sparsity penalty — an arbitrary hyperparameter — decides which member of $\mathcal{M}^\star$ you read out, and the seed decides the rest. Inspecting the failures shows why: at $\rho = 0.23$ the model routes $t \leftarrow \pi(t)$ through a layer-1 edge into some $k$ and a layer-2 edge $k \to t$, so the true parent is absent from the mask while the computation is exact. Any claim that a learned mask "discovered the causal structure" has to first say why its readout is not this artifact.
