# Optimal Ratio of Instruction to Pretraining Data in Mid-Training

> **Topic:** Data Pipelines & Curation · **ID:** `06-data-pipeline/midtraining-instruction-pretraining-ratio` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

Mid-training (also "annealing", "cooldown", "decay phase") is the final 1–10% of a pretraining run, executed at decaying learning rate over a re-weighted corpus. Most frontier recipes inject instruction-formatted data — prompt/response pairs, chain-of-thought traces, synthetic QA — into that phase rather than reserving it all for post-training.

**The decision.** Given a mid-training budget of $T_m$ tokens, choose the fraction $\alpha \in [0,1]$ of those tokens drawn from an instruction pool $\mathcal{D}_{\text{inst}}$ versus $1-\alpha$ from a curated pretraining pool $\mathcal{D}_{\text{pre}}$, to maximise the quality of the *final* model after the full downstream pipeline (SFT + preference optimisation).

Three variants, differing in difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Is there a stable, reproducible $\alpha^\star$ at fixed $(N, T, T_m)$, or is the loss surface in $\alpha$ flat within evaluation noise? Nobody has published a dense sweep with seed replicates.
- **Method.** Can $\alpha^\star$ be predicted from cheap proxy runs, the way DoReMi/RegMix/Data Mixing Laws predict pretraining mixtures — without running the post-training stage that defines the objective?
- **Theory.** Is there a model of representation drift under decaying LR that explains *why* instruction data at $\eta \to 0$ behaves differently from the same data at $\eta$ constant, and predicts scaling of $\alpha^\star$ in $N$?

Solved means: a published rule $\alpha^\star(N, T_m, \text{pool composition})$ that transfers to a held-out model family and beats the incumbent heuristic (roughly $\alpha \approx 0.1$–$0.3$, hand-tuned) on a pre-registered eval suite after post-training.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $\theta_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ be parameters after $t$ tokens, $T$ the total pretraining budget, and $T_m$ the mid-training suffix, so the phase runs on $t \in (T - T_m, T]$. The LR schedule is warmup-stable-decay: $\eta_t = \eta_{\max}$ for $t \le T - T_m$, then $\eta_t = \eta_{\max}\, s\!\left(\frac{t-(T-T_m)}{T_m}\right)$ with $s$ decreasing, $s(1) \approx 0$ (Hu et al. 2024; Hägele et al. 2024).

Mid-training samples from
$$P_\alpha = \alpha\, P_{\text{inst}} + (1-\alpha)\, P_{\text{pre}},$$
a token-level mixture. **Measured as:** $\alpha$ is the fraction of *training tokens* (not documents, not examples) whose source document came from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{inst}}$, counted after packing and including any template/special tokens. Reported "data percentages" in tech reports are frequently document- or example-fractions; the two differ by a factor equal to the length ratio, often $3$–$10\times$ since instruction records are short.

Loss masking is a second, usually unreported degree of freedom. Let $m \in \{0,1\}$ indicate whether prompt tokens are masked out. The effective instruction gradient share is
$$\tilde\alpha = \frac{\alpha \cdot (1 - m\,\rho)}{\alpha(1-m\rho) + (1-\alpha)},$$
with $\rho$ the mean prompt-token fraction of instruction records ($\rho \approx 0.3$–$0.6$ for typical SFT pools). $\tilde\alpha$, not $\alpha$, is the quantity a mechanism should depend on; almost no paper reports it.

The objective is defined *after* post-training. Let $\mathcal{A}$ be a fixed post-training operator (SFT on a fixed set, then DPO/RLVR with fixed hyperparameters) and $U$ a scalar eval aggregate over a suite $\mathcal{E}$:
$$\alpha^\star(N, T, T_m) = \arg\max_{\alpha} \; \mathbb{E}_{\text{seed}}\big[\, U\big(\mathcal{A}(\theta_T(\alpha))\big) \,\big].$$

Key assumptions, and their status:

1. **$\mathcal{A}$ is $\alpha$-independent.** Violated: the optimal SFT LR and epoch count shift with how instruction-adapted the base is, so a fixed $\mathcal{A}$ measures $\alpha^\star$ under a possibly mis-tuned downstream stage.
2. **$U$ is decision-relevant.** Violated in the usual way — $\mathcal{E}$ is multiple-choice and closed-form heavy (MMLU, GSM8K), while the claimed benefit of early instruction exposure is generalisation and format robustness.
3. **Mixture is exchangeable in order.** Violated: curriculum within the decay phase (instruction-late vs. uniform) changes results, and is a separate uncontrolled variable.
4. **No contamination.** Frequently violated — synthetic instruction pools are generated by teachers trained on the same benchmarks, so $U$ partly measures leakage.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (ablated in-paper, with a control arm).**

- Llama 3 (Grattafiori et al., 2024) ran an annealing ablation: annealing on high-quality code/math data lifted an 8B model by **+24.0% on GSM8K and +6.4% on MATH**, with **negligible gain at 405B**. This is the strongest published evidence that the mid-training data effect is scale-dependent.
- Domain upsampling at end of training (Blakeney et al., COLM 2024): a 7B model trained on 2.3T tokens, with the last ~10–20% upsampled toward high-quality domains, matched or beat the full-length baseline on aggregate eval — an ablation with a matched-compute control.
- MiniCPM (Hu et al., 2024) reported that introducing SFT-style data *during* the WSD decay phase beats introducing the same data only after pretraining ends.

**Claimed but unablated.** OLMo 2's Dolmino Mix 1124 mid-training stage (OLMo Team, 2025) is documented as a mixture including instruction and math data over ~50B tokens, with large downstream gains — but the mixture weight on instruction data is not swept, so its contribution is not separable from the math/web re-weighting. Phi-4 (Abdin et al., 2024), Nemotron and Qwen reports describe mid-training instruction injection as recipe, not experiment.

**Benchmark-number-only.** Nearly every claim of the form "mid-training instruction data improves the final post-trained model" exists as a single benchmark table comparing two whole recipes that differ in several variables at once.

**Adjacent method SOTA that does not cover this case.** DoReMi (Xie et al., NeurIPS 2023), Data Mixing Laws (Ye et al., ICLR 2025), RegMix (Liu et al., ICLR 2025) and Aioli (Chen et al., ICLR 2025) predict pretraining domain weights from small proxies against a *pretraining loss* target. None targets a post-post-training objective, and all assume a constant-LR regime, so they do not transfer to the decay phase where the effect lives.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Instruction data before/during pretraining aids knowledge acquisition.** Pre-instruction-tuning (Jiang et al., ACL 2024) showed that training on QA before documents raises later closed-book QA accuracy substantially over the document-then-QA order, at Llama-2 7B scale.
- **Instruction Pre-Training** (Cheng et al., EMNLP 2024) converted raw corpora into instruction-response pairs and reported a 500M model trained this way matching a 1B baseline, and gains from continued pretraining of Llama-3-8B — the clearest evidence that instruction formatting has pretraining-phase value, though the ratio is fixed, not swept.
- **Decay-phase data matters more per token than stable-phase data.** Hägele et al. (NeurIPS 2024) established the cooldown phase's outsized effect on final loss for a fixed budget, at up to 360M–1.5B scale.
- **Too much prior fine-tuning-like signal can hurt.** "Catastrophic overtraining" (Springer et al., 2025) shows extended pretraining can degrade post-fine-tuning performance — evidence that the base-model/post-training interaction is non-monotone, which is exactly the interaction $\alpha$ modulates.
- **Scale of every reproduced result:** $\le$ 8B parameters for controlled ablations. The one 405B data point (Llama 3) went the other way.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open (dominant).** No published dense sweep of $\alpha$ — say $\{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4\}$ — at a single fixed $(N, T, T_m)$ with $\ge 2$ seeds and a *fixed downstream post-training arm*. The experiment is runnable today for $N \le 3$B; nobody has published it.
- **Empirically open.** Whether $\alpha^\star$ shrinks with $N$, as the Llama 3 8B-vs-405B contrast hints, or whether that contrast is an artifact of eval saturation at 405B.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Whether mid-training instruction data confers anything beyond what post-training supplies. There is no accepted metric that separates "capability acquired" from "format acquired": the eval suite that would distinguish them (held-out task families, no template overlap) does not exist as a standard artifact.
- **Theoretically open.** No proof or model relating decaying-$\eta$ dynamics to which distribution the final weights are pulled toward. Loss-landscape arguments about small-LR basin selection are informal.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **confounded measurement through a non-commuting downstream stage**. The quantity of interest is $U(\mathcal{A}(\theta_T(\alpha)))$, but $\mathcal{A}$ itself has free hyperparameters whose optima move with $\alpha$. Holding $\mathcal{A}$ fixed biases toward the $\alpha$ that happens to suit the chosen SFT LR; re-tuning $\mathcal{A}$ per arm multiplies cost by the tuning grid and makes the comparison a comparison of two tuning budgets. This is non-identifiability, not just noise: base-model instruction-adaptation and SFT strength are partly substitutes, so $(\alpha, \eta_{\text{SFT}})$ has a ridge, and single-slice sweeps report the ridge's local slope as if it were an optimum.

Two aggravating factors. (a) The effect size sits near eval noise: seed-to-seed spread on GSM8K at 1B scale is several points, comparable to plausible $\alpha$ effects. (b) A clean run costs a full pretraining suffix *plus* a full post-training pipeline per arm — six arms times two seeds is twelve post-trained models, which is why industrial labs pick $\alpha$ once and ship.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Open recipe groups** — AI2 (OLMo/Tülu line), HuggingFace (SmolLM / FineWeb-Edu line), and EleutherAI publish mid-training mixtures in enough detail to be replicated; the sweep itself remains unpublished.
- **Mixture-optimisation methods extending past pretraining loss** to downstream targets, following RegMix/Aioli. *(frontier — verify)* Whether any of these has been fitted with a post-SFT target rather than a validation-loss target is not confidently established.
- **Reasoning-trace mid-training.** Since 2025 the instruction pool of interest is increasingly long chain-of-thought / RL-distilled traces rather than short SFT records, which changes $\rho$ and likely $\alpha^\star$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Base-model/post-training interaction**, following catastrophic-overtraining work (CMU/Stanford) — the closest thing to a mechanism for why $\alpha^\star$ should be interior rather than at $\alpha=1$.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Scale.** $N = 1.4$B dense transformer, $T = 300$B tokens (a standard open recipe), $T_m = 30$B (10%), cosine-to-zero decay over the phase. Six arms $\alpha \in \{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40\}$, 2 seeds each. All arms share the identical $\theta_{T-T_m}$ checkpoint — fork once, so pretraining cost is paid once. Marginal cost: $12 \times 30$B $= 360$B tokens $\approx 3.0\times10^{21}$ FLOPs, roughly 8–10 H100-days at realistic MFU.

**Control arm.** $\alpha = 0$: identical decay phase on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{pre}}$ only, with the *same token count* (compute-matched, not data-matched) and the same instruction pool moved entirely into SFT. This is the incumbent claim being tested — that mid-training instruction data buys nothing post-training cannot.

**Downstream.** Each of the 12 checkpoints gets the same SFT set, with $\eta_{\text{SFT}}$ tuned over a 3-point grid *per arm* — this is the part usually skipped, and it is what breaks the $(\alpha,\eta_{\text{SFT}})$ ridge.

**Deciding number.** $\Delta = \max_\alpha \bar U(\alpha) - \bar U(0)$, where $U$ is the mean over a pre-registered post-SFT suite (IFEval, GSM8K, MMLU, plus a held-out task family with no template overlap with the instruction pool), and the seed standard error $\hat\sigma$ is computed from the 2 replicates. If $\Delta < 2\hat\sigma$, the ratio is flat and the community should stop tuning it. If $\Delta > 2\hat\sigma$, report $\alpha^\star$ and repeat the fork at $N = 7$B to test the scale-dependence implied by Llama 3.

## 10. Worked Example

Take the reported headline setting: 1.4B model, 30B-token decay phase, instruction pool of 1.2M records averaging 620 tokens (prompt 240, response 380).

**Setting $\alpha$ at the document level, as reports do.** "10% instruction data" read as 10% of *examples* against a pretraining pool whose documents average 1,050 tokens gives a token share of
$$\alpha_{\text{tok}} = \frac{0.10 \times 620}{0.10\times620 + 0.90\times1050} = \frac{62}{62+945} = 0.062.$$
Already a $1.6\times$ gap from the intended 0.10.

**Now apply prompt masking.** With $m=1$ and $\rho = 240/620 = 0.387$, the gradient-carrying instruction share is
$$\tilde\alpha = \frac{0.062 \times 0.613}{0.062\times0.613 + 0.938} = 0.039.$$
The nominal 10% is a 3.9% gradient share. A lab that reports $\alpha = 0.10$ unmasked and a lab that reports $\alpha = 0.10$ masked differ by $2.6\times$ in the only quantity a mechanism could depend on.

**What that does to the decision.** Suppose the true response is a shallow interior optimum: $U(\tilde\alpha) = U_0 + 4.0\,\tilde\alpha - 30\,\tilde\alpha^2$ points, peaking at $\tilde\alpha^\star = 0.067$ with a peak height of $0.13$ points over $\tilde\alpha = 0$. Across the whole plausible range $\tilde\alpha \in [0, 0.13]$ the spread is $0.13$ points. Measured seed-to-seed standard deviation on an eval aggregate at 1.4B is roughly $0.5$–$1.0$ points. Detecting a $0.13$-point effect at $2\hat\sigma$ with $\hat\sigma_{\text{seed}} = 0.7$ needs about $n = (2\times0.7/0.13)^2 \approx 116$ seeds per arm.

**The obstruction, made visible.** Under a shallow-optimum hypothesis the experiment in §8 is underpowered by two orders of magnitude — and every published claim of an optimal ratio, run at $n=1$, is reporting seed noise. Under a steep-optimum hypothesis ($\Delta \approx 3$ points, plausible given Llama 3's +24% GSM8K at 8B), $n=2$ suffices. The two hypotheses are not currently distinguishable from published data, because no one reports $\tilde\alpha$ or seed variance. That is why the deciding statistic in §8 is $\Delta/\hat\sigma$ and not $\alpha^\star$ itself: the first thing to establish is whether the curve has a detectable shape at all.
