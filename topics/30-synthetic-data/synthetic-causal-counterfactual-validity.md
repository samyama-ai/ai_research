# Counterfactual Validity of Synthetic Causal Datasets

> **Topic:** Synthetic Data · **ID:** `30-synthetic-data/synthetic-causal-counterfactual-validity` · **Status:** methodologically-blocked

## 1. Problem Statement

Causal-inference methods are benchmarked on synthetic or semi-synthetic data because the counterfactual outcome is unobservable in real data. The generator supplies both potential outcomes, so PEHE and ATE error can be computed exactly. The problem: **the generator's counterfactuals are an artifact of modelling choices that the real-data fit does not constrain.** A ranking of estimators on such a benchmark may reflect the generator's arbitrary layer-3 choices rather than any property of the estimators.

Three variants, of different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a synthetic dataset $\mathcal{D}$ generated by an SCM $\mathcal{M}$ and a real reference dataset $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{real}}$, define a statistic that certifies $\mathcal{M}$'s counterfactual quantities as faithful to the real process. *This is the blocked variant.* No consistent estimator can exist for a quantity that the real data does not identify.
- **Method.** Build a generator whose *identified* quantities match real data and whose *unidentified* counterfactual quantities are reported as bounds, so a benchmark scores estimators against an interval rather than a point.
- **Theory.** Characterise the class of estimator-ranking functionals that are invariant across all SCMs consistent with a given observational (and interventional) distribution. Which benchmark verdicts survive the whole equivalence class?

## 2. Formal Setting

An SCM is $\mathcal{M} = \langle \mathbf{U}, \mathbf{V}, \mathcal{F}, P(\mathbf{U})\rangle$ with exogenous $\mathbf{U}$, endogenous $\mathbf{V} = (X, Y, \mathbf{Z})$, and $V_i \leftarrow f_i(\mathrm{pa}_i, U_i)$. It induces the three layers of Pearl's hierarchy:

$$\mathcal{L}_1: P(\mathbf{v}), \qquad \mathcal{L}_2: P(\mathbf{y} \mid do(\mathbf{x})), \qquad \mathcal{L}_3: P(\mathbf{y}_{\mathbf{x}}, \mathbf{y}'_{\mathbf{x}'}).$$

Individual treatment effect $\tau(z) = \mathbb{E}[Y_1 - Y_0 \mid \mathbf{Z}=z]$ is $\mathcal{L}_2$ under ignorability. Cross-world quantities — probability of necessity $\mathrm{PN} = P(Y_0 = 0 \mid X=1, Y=1)$, probability of necessity and sufficiency $\mathrm{PNS} = P(Y_1=1, Y_0=0)$ — are strictly $\mathcal{L}_3$.

**How the benchmark measures.** A generator is fit to real $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{real}}$, then simulates $n$ units with both outcomes. Reported error:

$$\widehat{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{PEHE}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\hat\tau(z_i) - \tau^{\mathcal{M}}(z_i)\big)^2, \qquad \tau^{\mathcal{M}}(z_i) = f_Y(1, z_i, u_i) - f_Y(0, z_i, u_i).$$

The ground truth $\tau^{\mathcal{M}}$ is read off the generator's own $f_Y$ and its sampled $u_i$ — never off the real data.

**Validity target.** Let $\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{real}})$ be all SCMs whose induced $\mathcal{L}_1$ (and $\mathcal{L}_2$, if an RCT arm exists) match the real distribution. Define the **counterfactual ambiguity** of a functional $\psi$:

$$\Delta_\psi = \sup_{\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}' \in \mathbb{M}} \big|\psi(\mathcal{M}) - \psi(\mathcal{M}')\big|.$$

A benchmark is *counterfactually valid for $\psi$* iff $\Delta_\psi = 0$, i.e. $\psi$ is point-identified. A benchmark **ranking** is valid iff the induced order over estimators is constant on $\mathbb{M}$.

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**

| Assumption | Status in practice |
|---|---|
| Fitted generator matches real $\mathcal{L}_1$ | Approximately; unmeasured on tails |
| No unmeasured confounding in the *real* process | Usually false (IHDP, Twins, Jobs) |
| Additive/independent noise $Y \leftarrow g(X,Z) + \varepsilon$, $\varepsilon \perp X$ | Imposed by fiat; not testable from $\mathcal{L}_1$ |
| Monotonicity ($Y_0 \le Y_1$) | Imposed silently by many generators |
| $\tau^{\mathcal{M}}$ is the estimand of interest | Holds only if the target is $\mathcal{L}_2$, not $\mathcal{L}_3$ |

The additive-noise and monotonicity assumptions do the entire job of pinning down $\tau^{\mathcal{M}}$ at the unit level. Neither leaves a testable footprint in $\mathcal{L}_1$ or $\mathcal{L}_2$.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established.**
- Layer collapse is impossible: Bareinboim, Correa, Ibeling and Icard (*On Pearl's Hierarchy and the Foundations of Causal Inference*, ACM, 2022) prove the Causal Hierarchy Theorem — $\mathcal{L}_2$ is a.e. underdetermined by $\mathcal{L}_1$, and $\mathcal{L}_3$ by $\mathcal{L}_2$. This is the formal statement that $\Delta_\psi > 0$ for cross-world $\psi$.
- Tian and Pearl (*Probabilities of causation: bounds and identification*, AMAI, 2000) give sharp bounds on PNS/PN from $\mathcal{L}_1 + \mathcal{L}_2$. These bounds are generally non-degenerate.
- Counterfactual identifiability *does* hold under restrictions: Nasr-Esfahany, Alizadeh and Kiciman (*Counterfactual Identifiability of Bijective Causal Models*, ICML 2023) show that if $f_Y(x,\cdot)$ is bijective in $U_Y$ for each $x$, layer 3 is recovered from $\mathcal{L}_2$. Additive noise is a special case — which is exactly why standard generators appear to work, and exactly why the appearance is circular.

**Claimed but unablated.** Realism-first generators — RealCause (Neal, Huang, Raghupathi, 2020), Credence (Parikh, Varjao, Xu, Tchetgen Tchetgen, *Validating Causal Inference Methods*, ICML 2022) — fit flexible conditional models to real data and then simulate. Both improve $\mathcal{L}_1$ fidelity, and Credence lets the user *specify* the confounding and effect functions. Neither claims, nor can claim, that the resulting unit-level counterfactuals match the real process; there is no ablation showing estimator rankings are stable across the generator's unidentified choices.

**Benchmark-number-only.** IHDP (Hill, JCGS 2011) response surfaces A/B, ACIC 2016/2017/2018, Twins, and the Jobs/LaLonde construction are the reported ground truth in almost every CATE paper. Their $\tau^{\mathcal{M}}$ values are numbers produced by a hand-written formula, not measurements.

**Empirical alternative.** Gentzel, Garant and Jensen (NeurIPS 2019) and Gentzel, Pruthi and Jensen (ICML 2021) argue for evaluating on real interventional data — this is genuinely $\mathcal{L}_2$-valid, and correspondingly says nothing about $\mathcal{L}_3$.

## 4. What Is Known

- **IHDP is small and its variance dominates.** 747 units (139 treated, 608 control), 25 covariates, 1000 simulation replicates. Curth, Svensson, Weatherall and van der Schaar (*Really Doing Great at Estimating CATE?*, NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks) show reported $\sqrt{\epsilon_{\mathrm{PEHE}}}$ differences between methods are frequently smaller than the across-replicate spread, and that method rankings flip under variants of the response surface that are equally consistent with the real covariates.
- **ACIC 2016** (Dorie, Hill, Shalit, Scott, Cervone, *Statistical Science* 2019): 77 DGP settings × 100 replicates over 4802 real units and 58 covariates. Even here every DGP is hand-specified; the competition measured robustness to *known* DGP variation, not to unidentified counterfactual structure.
- **Bounds are wide in realistic regimes.** With $P(Y=1\mid do(X=1))=0.8$, $P(Y=1\mid do(X=0))=0.4$, Tian–Pearl gives $\mathrm{PNS} \in [0.4, 0.6]$ — a 50% relative spread with *both* layers fully known.
- **Bijectivity buys identifiability, and the field mostly assumes it.** ICML 2023 result above; almost all deep SCM generators (Pawlowski, Castro, Glocker, NeurIPS 2020) use normalizing flows or additive noise, which are bijective by construction.
- **Continuous outcomes are not saved by smoothness alone.** Melnychuk, Frauen and Feuerriegel (NeurIPS 2023) show partial counterfactual identification for continuous outcomes is vacuous without a curvature restriction, and quantify how the bound tightens with it.
- **Axiomatic checks exist but are necessary, not sufficient.** Monteiro et al. (ICLR 2023) measure composition and reversibility soundness of counterfactual image models; a generator can satisfy both and still have the wrong cross-world coupling.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked (the core).** There is no defined measurement of "this synthetic dataset's counterfactuals match reality," because the target is not a functional of any observable distribution. Absent an assumption that is itself untestable, $\Delta_\psi$ is the honest answer, and no amount of real data shrinks it to zero.
- **Theoretically open.** Characterise the *ranking-invariant* class: for which loss functionals $L(\hat\tau, \mathcal{M})$ is $\arg\min$ over a fixed estimator set constant on $\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{real}})$? A partial-identification analogue of PEHE (score against the identified interval) has no established minimax theory.
- **Empirically open.** Nobody has measured how much benchmark rankings actually move across the unidentified degrees of freedom. Constructing many SCMs that agree on $\mathcal{L}_1$ and $\mathcal{L}_2$ but differ on the noise coupling, then re-running a standard estimator suite on each, is entirely runnable at IHDP/ACIC scale on a single GPU-day. It has not been run.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Non-identifiability plus absent ground truth, compounded by an evaluation that does not measure what it names.** PEHE is reported as "individual treatment effect error." At the individual level $\tau(z_i)$ depends on the cross-world coupling of $(Y_0, Y_1)$, which no observational or experimental dataset constrains. So the "ground truth" column in every semi-synthetic benchmark is a *definition*, chosen by the generator's author, not a *measurement*. The circularity: an estimator that implicitly assumes additive noise scores well on a generator built with additive noise, and the benchmark reports this as generalisation. Scaling compute, data, or replicates does not help — the gap is in the identification, not the estimation.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Restricted-class identifiability.** Bijective/monotone SCMs (Nasr-Esfahany, Kiciman, Alizadeh — MIT/Microsoft Research), extending to multi-valued and continuous treatments. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Partial counterfactual identification with neural bounds.** Frauen, Melnychuk, Feuerriegel (LMU Munich) — sharp bounds via curvature or sensitivity models, moving toward interval-valued benchmarks.
- **Neural causal models as generators.** Xia, Pan, Bareinboim (Columbia, ICLR 2023) — NCMs used for counterfactual identification and estimation; the same machinery can emit a *set* of counterfactually-distinct models fitting the same data.
- **Realism-first semi-synthetic pipelines.** Credence, RealCause, IBM's benchmarking framework (Shimoni et al.); active work on RCT-anchored generators where $\mathcal{L}_2$ is pinned by real experimental arms.
- **Interventional-data evaluation.** Jensen's group (UMass) continuing the case for real interventional benchmarks.
- **LLM causal reasoning benchmarks** (CLadder, Jin et al., NeurIPS 2023) inherit the identical problem: their layer-3 answers are generated by a symbolic engine's assumptions.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Do CATE benchmark rankings survive the counterfactually-unidentified degrees of freedom?

**Construction.** Take ACIC 2016 covariates ($n = 4802$, $p = 58$). Fit one generator $\mathcal{M}_0$ with additive Gaussian noise. Then construct $K = 50$ sibling SCMs $\{\mathcal{M}_k\}$ by applying, for each $k$, a distinct copula-based re-coupling of $(U_{Y_0}, U_{Y_1})$ with Spearman correlation drawn on a grid in $[-1, 1]$, holding $f_Y(x,\cdot)$ and $P(U)$ marginals fixed. **By construction all $K+1$ models induce identical $\mathcal{L}_1$ and identical $\mathcal{L}_2$** — the observational and interventional distributions are untouched. Only the cross-world coupling, and hence unit-level $\tau$, changes.

**Arms.** Estimator suite: S-learner, T-learner, X-learner, causal forest, TARNet, CFR-Wass, BART. 100 replicates per sibling. **Control arm:** the same 7 estimators on $\mathcal{M}_0$ with 100 replicates — i.e. the standard benchmark protocol, which reports one ranking.

**Deciding number.** Kendall's $\tau_b$ between the estimator ranking on $\mathcal{M}_0$ and the ranking on each $\mathcal{M}_k$, reported as the minimum over $k$.

- $\min_k \tau_b \geq 0.9$: benchmark rankings are effectively coupling-invariant; current practice is defensible for ranking, and the page's status weakens to *partially-solved*.
- $\min_k \tau_b \leq 0.5$: a majority of published CATE ordering claims are artifacts of an arbitrary noise coupling. Interval-valued scoring becomes mandatory.

Cost: roughly 5,100 dataset-fits × 7 estimators; under one GPU-day.

## 10. Worked Example

Binary treatment $X$, binary outcome $Y$, one covariate stratum. A hypothetical RCT gives, exactly:

$$P(Y{=}1 \mid do(X{=}1)) = 0.80, \qquad P(Y{=}1 \mid do(X{=}0)) = 0.40, \qquad \mathrm{ATE} = 0.40.$$

Both $\mathcal{L}_1$ and $\mathcal{L}_2$ are fully known. Now build two generators.

**$\mathcal{M}_A$ (monotone / additive-noise style).** Draw $U \sim \mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ once per unit; set $Y_0 = \mathbb{1}[U < 0.40]$, $Y_1 = \mathbb{1}[U < 0.80]$. Response-type shares: always-responder 0.40, helped 0.40, never-responder 0.20, hurt 0.00.

**$\mathcal{M}_B$ (anti-coupled).** Set $Y_1 = \mathbb{1}[U < 0.80]$, $Y_0 = \mathbb{1}[U > 0.60]$. Shares: always 0.20, helped 0.60, never 0.00, hurt 0.20.

Check: both give $P(Y_1{=}1) = 0.80$, $P(Y_0{=}1) = 0.40$. **Identical layer 1, identical layer 2.**

Now the unit-level ground truth:

| Quantity | $\mathcal{M}_A$ | $\mathcal{M}_B$ |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{PNS} = P(Y_1{=}1, Y_0{=}0)$ | 0.40 | 0.60 |
| $P(\text{harmed})$ | 0.00 | 0.20 |
| $\mathrm{Var}[\tau_i]$ | $0.4\cdot0.6 = 0.24$ | $0.6\cdot0.4 + 0.2 \cdot \ldots = 0.44$ |
| Fraction with $\tau_i = 0$ | 0.60 | 0.20 |

Both fall inside the Tian–Pearl bound $\mathrm{PNS} \in [\max(0, 0.4), \min(0.8, 0.6)] = [0.40, 0.60]$ — and they sit on its two endpoints. Nothing in any finite or infinite dataset from this process distinguishes them.

**The obstruction, made visible.** A CATE estimator that shrinks toward zero effect scores near-perfectly on $\mathcal{M}_A$ (60% of units truly have $\tau_i = 0$) and poorly on $\mathcal{M}_B$ (20%). An estimator that predicts a constant 0.4 for everyone has $\epsilon_{\mathrm{PEHE}} = 0.24$ on $\mathcal{M}_A$ and $0.44$ on $\mathcal{M}_B$ — an 83% difference in the reported error, and a reversal against the shrinkage estimator, produced by a coupling choice that the benchmark author made in one line of simulation code and never reported. Every published PEHE number on IHDP, Twins and ACIC carries this same unreported line.
