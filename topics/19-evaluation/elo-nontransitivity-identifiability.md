# Identifiability of Elo Ratings Under Non-Transitive Model Preferences

> **Topic:** Evaluation & Benchmarking · **ID:** `19-evaluation/elo-nontransitivity-identifiability` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

Leaderboards such as Chatbot Arena reduce a matrix of pairwise human preferences between language models to a single scalar per model (an Elo or Bradley–Terry rating). The reduction is exact only if preferences are *transitive* in the strong-stochastic sense. Real preferences are not: model $A$ can beat $B$, $B$ beat $C$, and $C$ beat $A$, because raters, prompts, and stylistic matchups differ.

**The problem.** Given pairwise outcomes generated by a possibly non-transitive preference process, determine whether the fitted scalar rating vector is *identified* — that is, whether it is a function of the preference process alone, or also of the **match schedule** (which pairs were compared, how often).

Three variants, of different difficulty:

- **Theory.** Under what conditions on the cyclic component of the preference matrix is the Bradley–Terry projection invariant to the sampling design? Give a bound on rating displacement in terms of cyclic energy.
- **Measurement.** Define and estimate the transitive/cyclic split of an *observed* leaderboard, separating genuine non-transitivity from sampling noise, rater heterogeneity, and prompt-distribution drift.
- **Method.** Produce a rating that is either schedule-invariant, or reports a validity interval outside which the scalar summary should not be used.

Solving it means: a procedure that, given an arena battle log, returns either a certificate that scalar ratings are schedule-stable to within $\pm\varepsilon$ Elo, or the specific rank pairs that are schedule-artifacts.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $\mathcal{M}=\{1,\dots,n\}$ be models. The preference process is a matrix $P \in [0,1]^{n\times n}$ with $p_{ij}+p_{ji}=1$; $p_{ij}$ is the probability a rater prefers $i$ over $j$, **measured** as the empirical win rate over battles between $i$ and $j$ in the deployed prompt and rater distribution. Ties are either split ($\tfrac12$ each) or dropped; the choice changes estimates and is rarely ablated.

Define the log-odds matrix
$$S_{ij} = \log \frac{p_{ij}}{1-p_{ij}}, \qquad S = -S^{\top}.$$
$S$ is skew-symmetric, so it lives in the edge space of the comparison graph $G=(\mathcal{M},E)$. The **schedule** is the weight $w_{ij}\ge 0$ = number of battles observed for pair $(i,j)$, normalized to $\sum w_{ij}=1$. This is chosen by the arena's sampling policy, not by nature.

Bradley–Terry fits $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^n$ with $\hat p_{ij}=\sigma(\theta_i-\theta_j)$. The MLE is asymptotically the weighted least-squares projection
$$\hat\theta(w) \;=\; \arg\min_{\theta:\,\mathbf{1}^\top\theta=0} \;\sum_{(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,\bigl(S_{ij}-(\theta_i-\theta_j)\bigr)^2 .$$
Elo points are $R_i = \frac{400}{\ln 10}\,\theta_i + 1000 \approx 173.7\,\theta_i + 1000$.

This is the **combinatorial Hodge decomposition** (Jiang, Lim, Yao & Ye, 2011): $S = \operatorname{grad}\theta \;\oplus\; S^{\text{cyc}}$, orthogonal *under the inner product $\langle a,b\rangle_w=\sum w_{ij}a_{ij}b_{ij}$*. Define the **cyclic energy fraction**
$$\kappa(w) \;=\; \frac{\|S^{\text{cyc}}\|_w^2}{\|S\|_w^2} \in [0,1].$$

Assumptions, and their status in practice:

- *Strong stochastic transitivity* ($p_{ij}\ge\tfrac12, p_{jk}\ge\tfrac12 \Rightarrow p_{ik}\ge\max$): **violated**; measured cycles exist in arena logs.
- *Bradley–Terry parametric form* ($\kappa=0$): **violated** whenever cycles exist.
- *Connected comparison graph* (Ford, 1957): required for existence and uniqueness of the MLE; usually holds in arenas but fails for cold-start models.
- *i.i.d. raters and a fixed prompt distribution*: **violated**; raters self-select, prompts drift, and providers can submit multiple private variants.
- *Schedule independent of outcomes*: **violated**; arenas up-sample new and close-in-rating pairs.

The identifiability question is precisely: **is $\hat\theta(w)$ constant in $w$?** If $\kappa=0$, yes (up to the additive shift, on a connected graph). If $\kappa>0$, $\hat\theta$ depends on $w$, and the leaderboard is a property of the schedule as much as of the models.

## 3. State of the Art

**Theory SOTA (established).**
- Ford (1957): BT MLE exists and is unique iff the comparison digraph is strongly connected. Zermelo (1929) gave the original algorithm.
- Jiang, Lim, Yao & Ye (*Math. Programming*, 2011): HodgeRank gives the exact orthogonal gradient/cyclic split, with the projection defined *relative to* $w$.
- Balduzzi, Tuyls, Pérolat & Graepel, *Re-evaluating Evaluation* (NeurIPS 2018): Elo is provably unable to represent cyclic games; proposes Nash averaging and $m$Elo$_{2k}$, a rating augmented with a rank-$2k$ skew-symmetric term.
- Bertrand, Czarnecki & Gidel, *On the Limitations of Elo: Real-World Games are Transitive, not Additive* (AISTATS 2023): formalizes when Elo's additive form fails even on games that are transitive but not additive.
- Bong & Rinaldo (ICML 2022): sharp $\ell_\infty$ existence/consistency conditions for the BTL MLE.

**Empirical SOTA (established).** Chatbot Arena (Chiang et al., ICML 2024) is the reference system: BT with bootstrap confidence intervals, published battle logs.

**Claimed but unablated.** Arena-style leaderboards report bootstrap CIs that condition on the realized schedule — they quantify sampling noise at fixed $w$, not the sensitivity of $\hat\theta$ to $w$. No published leaderboard reports $\kappa$. Statements that observed cycles are "rare enough to ignore" appear as benchmark commentary, not as ablations.

**Benchmark-number-only results.** Reported rating gaps of 5–20 Elo between adjacent frontier models are single numbers from one schedule; their schedule-invariance has not been tested.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Non-transitivity is measurable in preference data.** Chen & Joachims (WSDM 2016) fit the Blade–Chest model to matchup datasets and beat BT on held-out accuracy, at $n\approx 10$–$10^3$ competitors — direct evidence that a non-transitive term carries predictive signal.
- **Real games are "spinning tops."** Czarnecki et al. (NeurIPS 2020) show across 14 real games that the cyclic dimension is large for mid-skill agents and collapses near the top — so cycles concentrate among *near-equal* competitors, exactly where leaderboard rank decisions are made.
- **Elo is order-sensitive and hyperparameter-sensitive at LLM scale.** Boubdir, Kim, Ermis, Hooker & Fadaee, *Elo Uncovered* (2023), show that permuting match order and varying the $K$-factor changes LLM rankings; online Elo is not the same estimator as batch BT.
- **Schedule is adversarially exploitable.** Singh et al., *The Leaderboard Illusion* (2025), analyze roughly 2M Chatbot Arena battles across ~240 models and ~40 providers and find large asymmetries in sampling rate and private-variant testing — a direct demonstration that $w$ is under provider influence.
- **Scale of the public data.** The Arena ICML 2024 paper reports over 240K votes across ~50 models; later dumps exceed 1M. Adjacent frontier models differ by $\sim$10 Elo with $\sim$±5–10 point bootstrap CIs.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No published bound of the form $\sup_{w,w'}\|\hat\theta(w)-\hat\theta(w')\|_\infty \le f(\kappa, \lambda_2(L_w))$, where $\lambda_2$ is the graph-Laplacian spectral gap. The Hodge machinery gives the decomposition but not a schedule-perturbation bound for the *rank order*. No characterization of which $S$ admit schedule-invariant rank orders despite $\kappa>0$.
- **Empirically open.** $\kappa$ has never been reported for a public LLM arena log, at any scale. The experiment needs only existing data.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Separating *genuine* non-transitivity from rater heterogeneity is not well-posed with current logging: a population of transitive raters with different preferences aggregates to a non-transitive $P$ (Condorcet). Arena logs do not carry stable rater IDs, so the mixture cannot be decomposed. Whether the "non-transitivity" is in the models or in the aggregation is currently unmeasurable.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The obstruction is **non-identifiability with respect to a nuisance design variable that is not held fixed and is not neutral**. When $\kappa>0$ the map from preferences to ratings has a null direction: the cyclic component projects onto the gradient component with a coefficient set by $w$. Because arenas choose $w$ adaptively (up-sampling close matchups and new entrants) and providers influence $w$ (Singh et al., 2025), the nuisance variable is correlated with the thing being estimated. Bootstrap CIs cannot see this — they resample outcomes at fixed $w$, so they estimate variance *conditional on* the confound.

Compounding it: there is no ground-truth ranking to check against. "Which model is better" is defined by the preference process itself, so the error cannot be measured externally, only via invariance tests.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Style and confound control.** LMArena's style-controlled leaderboard regresses out response length and markdown, which removes one known driver of matchup-specific (hence cyclic) structure. It does not address the schedule dependence.
- **Low-rank non-transitive ratings.** $m$Elo$_{2k}$ (DeepMind lineage) and Blade–Chest models remain the main parametric alternatives; no leaderboard has adopted them.
- **Social-choice aggregation.** Lanctot et al., *Evaluating Agents using Social Choice Theory* (2023), argue for voting rules over ratings; Nash averaging (Balduzzi et al.) is invariant to redundant competitors but not to schedule in general. *(frontier — verify: adoption in any production LLM leaderboard.)*
- **Adaptive sampling for arenas.** Active pair-selection to shrink CIs is being deployed; it *increases* schedule dependence, and the interaction with $\kappa$ appears unstudied. *(frontier — verify.)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Scale.** The public Chatbot Arena battle dump: $\ge 10^6$ votes, $\ge 200$ models. No model training, no GPU; hours of CPU.

**Procedure.** Restrict to the top 30 models by battle count. Estimate $\hat p_{ij}$ and its variance per pair. Then construct two *re-weighted* arms by resampling battles **within each pair** (so every pair's empirical win rate is preserved exactly in expectation, and only $w$ changes):
- Arm U: uniform $w_{ij}$ over all observed pairs.
- Arm A: $w_{ij}\propto$ the realized arena schedule (heavily skewed toward frontier and new models).

Fit batch BT on each. Report $\Delta_i = R_i^{U}-R_i^{A}$ (after mean-centering), the max rank displacement, and $\kappa(w)$ for both weightings.

**Control arm.** Repeat the identical pipeline on synthetic battles drawn from a *fitted transitive* BT model with the same per-pair counts. There $\kappa=0$ by construction, so any observed $\Delta$ is pure estimation noise; this calibrates the null.

**Deciding number.** The **median $|\Delta_i|$ over the top 30 models**, in Elo points, compared with the control arm's median and with the published bootstrap CI half-width (currently $\approx 5$–$10$ points). If real-data median $|\Delta|$ exceeds $3\times$ the control median and exceeds the CI half-width, scalar Elo is schedule-dependent at the resolution at which leaderboard ranks are reported, and every adjacent-rank claim within that band is unsupported. If it is below the control median, the non-transitivity is empirically negligible at frontier scale and the problem downgrades to theory-only.

## 10. Worked Example

Three models, a pure 3-cycle: $p_{AB}=p_{BC}=p_{CA}=0.7$. Log-odds $c=\log(0.7/0.3)=0.847$, so $S_{AB}=S_{BC}=S_{CA}=+0.847$.

**Schedule U (uniform), $w_{AB}=w_{BC}=w_{CA}$.** By symmetry the least-squares fit is $\theta_A=\theta_B=\theta_C=0$. All three models tie at 1000 Elo. Here $\kappa=1$: 100% of the signal is cyclic and discarded.

**Schedule A (skewed), $w_{AB}=w_{BC}=5$, $w_{CA}=1$** — the kind of skew an arena produces by up-sampling two of the three matchups. With $x=\theta_A-\theta_B$, $y=\theta_B-\theta_C$, minimize
$$5(c-x)^2+5(c-y)^2+(c+x+y)^2 .$$
Stationarity gives $12x+2y=8c$ and $2x+12y=8c$, so $x=y=\tfrac{8}{14}c=0.484$. Centered: $\theta=(+0.484,\,0,\,-0.484)$.

In Elo points ($\times173.7$): $A=1084$, $B=1000$, $C=916$.

**The obstruction, visible.** Nothing about the models changed. Every pairwise win rate is identical in both arms — including the fact that $C$ beats $A$ 70% of the time. Only the number of battles per pair changed. Yet the leaderboard moved from a three-way tie to a **168-point** gap that ranks $A$ above $C$, contradicting their head-to-head record. A bootstrap over outcomes at fixed $w$ would report tight CIs on both answers and flag no problem, because it never varies the quantity that produced the difference. That is non-identifiability, not noise: 168 Elo is an order of magnitude larger than the $\sim$10-point gaps frontier leaderboards currently ask readers to believe.
