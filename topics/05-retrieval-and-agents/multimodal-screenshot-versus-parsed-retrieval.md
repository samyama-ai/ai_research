# Multimodal Document Retrieval over Screenshots versus Parsed Text

> **Topic:** Retrieval & Agentic Systems · **ID:** `05-retrieval-and-agents/multimodal-screenshot-versus-parsed-retrieval` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

**Input.** A corpus of visually rich documents (PDFs, slides, scanned forms, web pages) and a natural-language query $q$.

**Two pipelines.**
- *Parsed*: run OCR/layout parsing to produce text $T(d)$, chunk it, embed the chunks, retrieve.
- *Screenshot*: render each page to an image $I(d)$, embed the pixels with a vision-language model (VLM), retrieve. No parse step.

**Output.** A ranked list of pages; the objective is retrieval quality (nDCG@5, Recall@k) and downstream answer accuracy after a generator reads the retrieved pages.

**Decision predicate.** Does the screenshot pipeline beat the parsed pipeline *because it avoids parse loss*, or because current screenshot systems happen to use bigger backbones, late-interaction scoring, and richer training data? The claim under test is a **main effect of modality**, not a system-vs-system win.

Three variants, different difficulty:
- **Measurement.** Define a capacity-matched, byte-matched comparison. This is the blocked part.
- **Method.** Build a retriever that dominates on quality *and* index cost. Empirically open.
- **Theory.** Characterize which information is provably unrecoverable by a parse-then-embed pipeline. Open, and barely attacked.

## 2. Formal Setting

Corpus $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1,\dots,d_N\}$ of pages. Two lossy channels:

$$T:\mathcal{D}\to\Sigma^*,\qquad I:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}$$

$T$ is a parser (Tesseract, Nougat, Docling, olmOCR); $I$ is deterministic rasterization at DPI $\rho$.

**Encoders.** $E_\theta^{\text{txt}}$ and $E_\phi^{\text{img}}$ map to $m_d$ vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{h}$ ($m_d=1$ for bi-encoders; $m_d\approx 1030$ for ColPali-style patch embeddings). Scoring is MaxSim late interaction:

$$s(q,d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_q}\max_{j\le m_d}\langle E_q(q)_i,\; E(d)_j\rangle,$$

which reduces to cosine similarity when $n_q=m_d=1$.

**Quality.** With graded relevance $r(q,d)$, $\mathrm{nDCG@}k$ as usual; the estimator is a mean over $|\mathcal{Q}|$ queries with a paired bootstrap CI, since arms share queries.

**Cost, as actually measured.**
- Index bytes per page $B = m_d \cdot h \cdot b/8$, $b$ = bits per component after quantization.
- Index latency $L_{\text{idx}}$ = wall-clock seconds/page on one A100 (or CPU-seconds for the parser, which is often the dominant term and is routinely omitted from reported numbers).
- Query latency $L_q$ at fixed recall on a fixed ANN backend.

**Effect of interest.** With backbone $\theta$, scoring architecture $A$, and training mixture $M$ held fixed:

$$\Delta_{\text{mod}} = \mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{nDCG@}5(I) - \mathrm{nDCG@}5(T)\big]\ \Big|\ \theta, A, M, B.$$

Published comparisons estimate $\Delta_{\text{sys}}$, which varies $\theta, A, M, B$ jointly. $\Delta_{\text{sys}}\ne\Delta_{\text{mod}}$.

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**
1. *Page is the retrieval unit.* Violated: answers span pages; text pipelines chunk on semantics, screenshot pipelines cannot.
2. *$T$ and $I$ carry the same source content.* Violated: parsers drop figures, reading order in multi-column layouts, and merged table cells.
3. *Relevance labels are modality-neutral.* Violated: ViDoRe-style labels were generated by prompting a VLM over page images, so the label distribution is aligned with the visual arm.
4. *Equal training budget.* Violated: screenshot retrievers are typically initialized from VLMs pretrained on far more document-image supervision than the text baselines' encoders saw.

## 3. State of the Art

**Systems/empirical SOTA.**
- **ColPali** (Faysse et al., ICLR 2025) — PaliGemma-3B + ColBERT late interaction over 1024 image patches, 128-d each. On ViDoRe v1 it reports ~81 average nDCG@5 versus ~66 for a strong parse-then-embed baseline (Unstructured + captioning + text embedder), and page-indexing latency of roughly 0.4 s/page against ~7 s/page for the parse pipeline. *Established*: the end-to-end system win on ViDoRe. *Unablated*: which factor produced it. Backbone, scoring, training data and index size all differ across the arms.
- **DSE** (Ma, Lin et al., 2024) — "Document Screenshot Embedding": single-vector screenshot retrieval from a VLM, showing that the win survives without late interaction. This is the closest thing to a scoring-architecture ablation in the literature, but the text arm still uses a different backbone.
- **ColQwen2 / ColNomic and successors** (2025) — Qwen2-VL backbones with dynamic resolution; top of the ViDoRe leaderboard. Leaderboard numbers only; no factorial ablation.
- **VisRAG** (Yu et al., 2024) and **M3DocRAG** (Cho et al., 2024) extend the claim to end-to-end generation, reporting double-digit relative gains over text RAG on visually rich sets.

**Text-side SOTA that the comparisons usually skip.** Modern parsers (Docling, olmOCR, Marker, Nougat) plus a strong hybrid BM25+dense retriever with reranking. Most screenshot papers baseline against a weaker parser than the current best.

**Theory SOTA.** Essentially none. There is no formal statement of what a parse channel destroys, no capacity bound relating $m_d\cdot h$ to achievable recall on document images.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Parse quality propagates to RAG accuracy.** OHRBench ("OCR Hinders RAG", Zhang et al., 2024) measures a monotone degradation of end-to-end QA accuracy as OCR noise rises, across ~1k PDF pages and 8+ OCR engines. Established, reproduced across engines.
- **Late interaction helps document images a lot.** ColPali's own ablation to a single pooled vector costs several nDCG@5 points on ViDoRe at 3B scale. Established within one paper; not independently reproduced with the backbone held fixed.
- **Index cost is the real gap.** ColPali-style pages cost ~1030 × 128 bytes ≈ **132 KB/page at int8**, versus ~1.5 KB/page for a 768-d fp16 single-vector text chunk — roughly **80×**. Measured directly from the architecture, not disputed.
- **Screenshot retrievers underperform on text-dense, layout-simple corpora.** ViDoRe v2 and the MTEB visual-document splits (2025) show the margin over text baselines shrinking toward zero on clean, single-column, text-heavy documents. Scale: thousands of queries, tens of thousands of pages.
- **DocVQA-family results** (Mathew et al., WACV 2021; InfographicVQA, WACV 2022) establish that answers to a substantial minority of realistic document questions depend on chart or figure content that no text parser emits.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open.** $\Delta_{\text{mod}}$ itself. Nobody has run the 2×2 factorial — same VLM backbone, same training mixture, same scoring head, one arm fed rendered pixels and the other fed that page's parse as text — at a scale (≥$10^5$ pages, ≥$10^3$ queries) where the CI is tighter than the effect. The experiment is runnable today on 8 A100s.
- **Empirically open.** Whether the screenshot advantage survives byte-matching. Nobody has reported ColPali-class quality at $B\le 8$ KB/page against a text arm at the same $B$.
- **Methodologically blocked.** A modality-neutral relevance label. ViDoRe queries were synthesized by a VLM reading page images; a query that no parser could ever serve is trivially won by the visual arm, and there is no accepted procedure for sampling queries independently of either channel.
- **Theoretically open.** Any bound of the form "for document class $\mathcal{C}$, no parse-then-embed pipeline achieves recall $>1-\epsilon$". Not attempted.
- **Empirically open.** Hybrids. Whether $\alpha\, s_{\text{img}} + (1-\alpha)\, s_{\text{txt}}$ dominates both arms, and at what $\alpha$ — plausible, widely assumed, not systematically measured.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**The obstruction is confounded measurement compounded by label non-neutrality.**

Every published head-to-head varies at least four factors at once: backbone family and parameter count, scoring architecture (single-vector vs MaxSim), training mixture and volume, and index footprint. With one measurement per cell, the modality main effect is **non-identifiable** — a 15-point ViDoRe gap is consistent with a 15-point modality effect, a 15-point late-interaction effect, or a 15-point pretraining-data effect.

The label problem is worse, because it cannot be fixed by more compute. Benchmark queries generated by a VLM prompted on page images inherit that model's notion of what is on the page. The benchmark then *names* itself document retrieval while *measuring* agreement with a visual annotator. Fixing this requires queries sampled from real user logs against the same corpus, which no public visual-document benchmark currently has at scale.

Compute is a secondary obstruction: the factorial needs 8 training runs of a 3B VLM plus 8 index builds over $10^5$–$10^6$ pages, roughly $10^3$–$10^4$ A100-hours — affordable for a lab, not for most groups, which is why the ablation keeps not happening.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Index compression for multi-vector visual retrieval.** Token pooling, hierarchical clustering, PLAID-style residual compression applied to patch embeddings; reported 3–10× footprint cuts at ~1 point nDCG@5 loss (ILLUIN / Hugging Face, 2025). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Better parsers narrowing the gap.** olmOCR (AI2, 2025), Docling (IBM), and VLM-based parsers that emit figure descriptions; the open question is whether a strong parser plus a strong text retriever recovers most of ColPali's margin.
- **Benchmark hardening.** ViDoRe v2 (2025) and MMLongBench-Doc (Ma et al., NeurIPS 2024 D&B) explicitly target the label-leakage and saturation problems in v1.
- **Hybrid and cascaded retrieval.** Cheap text first-stage, screenshot reranker second-stage — the obvious cost-optimal design, mostly folklore in production systems rather than published. *(frontier — verify)*
- Active groups: ILLUIN Technology + CentraleSupélec (ColPali line), Waterloo (DSE, Jimmy Lin's group), UNC (M3DocRAG), Tsinghua/OpenBMB (VisRAG), AI2 (olmOCR).

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**The factorial that identifies $\Delta_{\text{mod}}$.**

- **Scale.** Corpus: 500k pages (ViDoRe v1+v2 pools plus a held-out 400k-page slice of arXiv/EDGAR/GovDocs). Queries: 3,000 — 1,500 human-written by annotators who saw the page image, 1,500 written by annotators who saw only the parse. Reporting both label sources is the point.
- **Design.** One backbone throughout: Qwen2-VL-2B. Arms: $\{$pixels, parse-as-text$\}$ × $\{$single-vector, MaxSim$\}$ × $\{B\approx 1.5$ KB/page (pooled), $B\approx 132$ KB/page (full)$\}$ = 8 cells. Identical contrastive training mixture, identical steps, 3 seeds. The parse arm uses olmOCR output fed to the *same* VLM's text pathway — this is the control arm that no published comparison has.
- **Deciding number.** The modality main effect on nDCG@5 at matched $B$ and matched scoring, with a paired bootstrap 95% CI over queries.
  - $\Delta_{\text{mod}} > 5$ points with CI excluding zero on **both** label sources → pixels carry retrieval-relevant information the parse destroys; screenshot retrieval is justified on its merits.
  - $|\Delta_{\text{mod}}| < 2$ points → the ViDoRe gap is architecture and data, not modality, and the field should spend its compute on late interaction and parsers instead.
  - $\Delta_{\text{mod}} > 5$ on image-authored queries but $< 2$ on parse-authored queries → the benchmark, not the pipeline, is producing the result.

Cost estimate: ~24 GPU-days on 8×A100 for training, ~6 for indexing. Two months for one PhD student.

## 10. Worked Example

One page: a 10-K financial statement, two-column, with a merged-header table reporting segment revenue.

**Query.** "What was cloud segment revenue in fiscal 2023?" Ground truth: `$8,412M`, in a cell whose column header spans two sub-columns.

**Parse arm.** Tesseract linearizes the table row-major and drops the span, emitting `Cloud 8,412 7,109 2023 2022` — the year alignment is destroyed. A 768-d embedding of that chunk scores 0.71 cosine against the query; a distractor chunk from the prior-year filing scores 0.74. **The correct page ranks 2nd.** Substituting olmOCR, which emits Markdown tables with spans preserved, moves the correct page to rank 1. So the parse arm's failure here is a *parser* failure, not a modality failure.

**Screenshot arm.** ColPali encodes the page as 1030 patches. MaxSim fires on the patches covering the `8,412` cell and its column header, since spatial adjacency is preserved in the patch grid. Rank 1, score margin 2.3 over the distractor.

**The cost accounting.** Storage for 500k pages: parse arm at 1.5 KB/page = **0.75 GB**; screenshot arm at 132 KB/page (int8) = **66 GB**. At an in-memory ANN budget of 64 GB, the screenshot arm does not fit and must spill to disk, changing $L_q$ from ~15 ms to ~120 ms.

**Where the obstruction becomes visible.** Both arms produce rank 1 once the parser is good. The screenshot arm costs 88× the bytes and 8× the query latency to get there. And the published gap that motivated the screenshot arm was measured against Tesseract-class parsing, on queries a VLM wrote while looking at the page. Neither the quality claim nor the cost claim is settled by any experiment yet run — which is the whole content of this entry.
