# Optimal Beam Reranking Objective for Reasoning

> **Topic:** Reasoning & Inference-Time Compute · **ID:** `17-reasoning/beam-reranking-objective-for-reasoning` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

Step-level beam search over chain-of-thought is now a standard test-time-compute method: sample $k$ continuations of each live prefix, score every prefix with a verifier, keep the top $b$, repeat. The **scoring rule** — what number a partial reasoning prefix is ranked by — is chosen by convention, not derivation. Published systems use the last step's process-reward-model (PRM) score, the minimum over steps, the product over steps, the mean, a length-normalized sum, or a learned value head. These give different beams and different final accuracy, and no one has shown which is right.

Three variants, different difficulty:

- **Theory.** Given a generator $\pi$, a verifier $\hat r$ with known error, and a budget of $N$ sampled steps, what ranking function on partial prefixes maximizes the probability that the returned trajectory is correct? Open.
- **Method.** Build a reranker that beats *all* of {last, min, mean, product, sum} aggregations, at matched compute, across ≥3 model scales and ≥3 benchmarks. Not achieved.
- **Measurement.** Attribute an accuracy delta to the objective rather than to the verifier's calibration, beam width, step segmentation, or diversity. Currently confounded.

Solved means: a ranking rule with a stated optimality property under stated verifier-error assumptions, plus an ablation isolating it from beam width and verifier quality.

## 2. Formal Setting

A problem $x$; a reasoning trajectory is a sequence of steps $s_{1:T}$, $s_t \in \Sigma^*$, generated by $\pi(\cdot \mid x, s_{1:t-1})$. Terminal correctness is a binary $y(x, s_{1:T}) \in \{0,1\}$, measured by exact-match on the extracted final answer (this is the only ground truth actually available).

**Value.** The quantity search wants is
$$V^{\pi}(s_{1:t}) \;=\; \Pr_{s_{t+1:T}\sim\pi}\!\left[y(x,s_{1:T}) = 1 \mid x, s_{1:t}\right],$$
measured empirically by Monte Carlo rollout: $\hat V = \frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^m y(x, s_{1:t} \oplus \rho_j)$ with $m$ completions $\rho_j$ (Math-Shepherd uses $m \in [4,16]$; the estimator has standard error $\ge 0.5/\sqrt{m}$, so $m=8$ gives $\pm 0.18$ — larger than most reported gaps).

**Reranking objective.** A PRM emits per-step scores $\hat r_t \in [0,1]$. The beam ranks by an aggregator $A$:
$$\mathrm{score}(s_{1:t}) = A(\hat r_1,\dots,\hat r_t), \quad A \in \Big\{\hat r_t,\ \min_i \hat r_i,\ \textstyle\prod_i \hat r_i,\ \frac{1}{t}\sum_i \hat r_i,\ \sum_i \hat r_i\Big\}.$$
Beam search with width $b$, branching $k$, depth $T$ costs $N = bkT$ generated steps plus $bkT$ verifier calls; compute is reported in generated tokens, and a fair comparison holds $N$ fixed across arms.

**Advantage form.** Setlur et al. define step-level progress $A^{\mu}(s_{1:t}) = V^{\mu}(s_{1:t}) - V^{\mu}(s_{1:t-1})$ under a *prover* policy $\mu \ne \pi$, and rank by accumulated advantage rather than value.

**Assumptions, and which fail.**
1. *Steps are well defined.* Violated — segmentation is by "\n\n" or by a heuristic; step boundaries are not intrinsic, and re-segmentation changes $\min$ and $\prod$ aggregates but not $\hat r_T$.
2. *$\hat r$ is calibrated: $\hat r_t \approx V^\pi(s_{1:t})$.* Violated — PRMs are trained on rollouts from a fixed base policy and are systematically overconfident on out-of-distribution prefixes.
3. *Off-policy match.* Violated in the way that matters: beam search induces a new policy $\pi_b \ne \pi$ (top-$b$ filtered), so the quantity to rank by is $V^{\pi_b}$, while the verifier estimates $V^\pi$. The target moves when you use the estimator.
4. *Errors are independent across steps.* Violated — an early mistake makes later steps wrong in correlated ways, which is exactly the regime where $\prod$ and $\min$ diverge.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (with ablations).**
- Lightman et al., *Let's Verify Step by Step* (ICLR 2024): PRM reranking over 1,860 samples reaches **78.2%** on a 500-problem MATH subset vs **72.4%** for an outcome RM and **69.6%** for majority vote, same generator. The aggregation used is the product of step correctness probabilities (equivalently, a min-like statistic); the paper reports product and min give near-identical results — the only clean aggregator ablation in the literature.
- Wang et al., *Math-Shepherd* (ACL 2024): automatic per-step labels from MC rollouts, no human annotation; verification gains on GSM8K/MATH for Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-67B-scale generators.
- Snell et al., *Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally* (2024): beam search beats best-of-$N$ at small budgets and on hard problems, and *loses* to best-of-$N$ at large budgets; compute-optimal allocation gives up to $\sim4\times$ efficiency over best-of-$N$. PaLM 2-S\* on MATH.

**Claimed but unablated / benchmark-number-only.**
- Diverse Verifier Tree Search (Beeching, Tunstall, Rush; HuggingFace 2024) reports Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct matching or exceeding Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on MATH-500 under search. This is a blog benchmark number with no aggregator ablation.
- Xie et al., *Self-Evaluation Guided Beam Search* (NeurIPS 2023) uses an LLM self-evaluation confidence multiplied into the generation likelihood; the combination weight is tuned, not derived.
- Setlur et al., *Rewarding Progress* (ICLR 2025) reports ~8% absolute accuracy gain and 1.5–5$\times$ compute efficiency from advantage-based PRMs over outcome-value PRMs, on Gemma-2B/9B-scale generators. The strongest evidence that the objective — not just verifier quality — matters; not independently reproduced at frontier scale.

**Theory SOTA.** There is no optimality theorem for step-level reranking. The nearest results are for best-of-$N$: Gao, Schulman, Hilton (ICML 2023) give the KL cost $\mathrm{KL}(\text{BoN}\|\pi) = \log n - \frac{n-1}{n}$ and the overoptimization law $R_{\text{gold}}(d) = d(\alpha - \beta \log d)$, $d=\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}$ — i.e. reward increases then falls as search pressure grows.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Verification is the bottleneck, not generation.** Brown et al., *Large Language Monkeys* (2024): Llama-3-8B-Instruct coverage (any-sample-correct) on MATH rises from ~15.9% at one sample to **79.8%** at 10,000 samples, while verifier-selected accuracy plateaus far below. The gap is the reranking problem.
- **Imperfect verifiers cap scaling.** Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan (2024) show that with a false-positive rate bounded away from zero, resampling accuracy is non-monotone in $N$ and can fall below single-sample performance — search selects for verifier error.
- **Aggregation choice is worth single-digit points.** Snell et al. report last-step PRM aggregation performing best in their setup; Lightman et al. find product $\approx$ min. These two findings are not consistent with each other across setups, and no paper resolves the discrepancy.
- **Beam-width and objective interact.** Snell et al.: at $N=256$ on MATH, beam search underperforms best-of-$N$ on easy problems; the crossover moves with problem difficulty bin. Any aggregator comparison at a single $(b,N)$ is uninformative.
- **Effect sizes are small relative to noise.** MATH-500 has $n=500$; a 3-point difference has a binomial standard error of ~2.0 points, so most published aggregator comparisons are within noise of each other.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No characterization of the optimal ranking function under a noisy verifier and finite beam budget. Even the ranking-by-$V^{\pi_b}$ fixed point (rank by the value of the policy the ranking induces) has no existence or uniqueness result.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether *any* Markov aggregator over per-step scores can be optimal, or whether the optimal statistic must depend on the sibling set (the other beams at depth $t$) — i.e. whether reranking is intrinsically comparative rather than pointwise.
- **Empirically open.** The full cross of {5 aggregators} × {3 beam widths} × {3 model scales} × {3 benchmarks} at matched token budget has never been run. It is a few thousand GPU-hours, not a research program.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Attributing gain to the objective requires holding verifier calibration fixed across aggregators — but $\min$, $\prod$ and mean have different implicit calibration targets, so "the same PRM" is not the same estimator under each. No accepted recalibration protocol exists.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **non-identifiability from outcome labels under a moving target policy**. PRM training labels come from rollouts under the base policy $\pi$; search consumes those scores to build $\pi_b$, whose value function is what should have been estimated. Any two aggregators that induce the same top-$b$ ordering on the training distribution are indistinguishable in the data, yet diverge on the OOD prefixes that search deliberately concentrates on. This is self-inflicted distribution shift: the better the ranking, the further from $\pi$ the beam sits, and the less the verifier's training signal applies.

Compounding it: step segmentation is a free parameter that changes $\min$/$\prod$/sum but not last-step scoring, so the aggregator comparison is not even well posed without fixing a segmenter. And MATH-500-scale benchmarks cannot resolve 2–3 point differences.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Advantage/progress-based PRMs** — CMU and Google DeepMind (Setlur, Kumar and collaborators). Ranking by progress under a separate prover policy, which partially sidesteps the value/policy mismatch.
- **Generative verifiers** — Zhang et al. (ICLR 2025), GenRM: verification as next-token prediction with CoT, letting verifier compute scale alongside generator compute. Changes the cost model of reranking, not the aggregator question. *(frontier — verify current scale)*
- **Search-free RL as a substitute** — DeepSeek-R1-style outcome-RL training folds search into the policy, weakening the case for elaborate test-time reranking on math; whether this holds for agentic/long-horizon tasks is unresolved. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Open tooling** — HuggingFace `search-and-learn` and Qwen PRM releases make the missing ablation cheap to run. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Scale.** Generators: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. Verifier: one fixed open PRM (e.g. Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B) for all arms. Benchmarks: MATH-500, AIME-2024/2025 pooled, GPQA-Diamond. Budget: token-matched at $N \in \{16, 64, 256\}$ equivalent full solutions, 5 seeds. Fix segmentation to a single rule ("\n\n") for every arm.

**Arms.** Aggregators {last, min, product, mean, sum} × beam widths $b \in \{4, 16\}$.

**Control arms (two, both required).** (i) Best-of-$N$ with the same PRM and last-step scoring — the no-search control. (ii) Majority vote with no verifier — the no-verifier control. Without (ii), an aggregator ranking is uninterpretable, since Lightman-style gains partly reproduce with self-consistency.

**Deciding number.** The **maximum pairwise accuracy gap between aggregators, at fixed $(b, N)$, averaged over the three generators, with 95% CIs from the 5 seeds**. If that gap is $<2$ points and its CI covers zero at every budget, the objective does not matter and the field should stop tuning it. If any aggregator wins by $\ge 5$ points with a CI excluding zero at $\ge 2$ of 3 scales, the ordering is real and worth a theory.

**Secondary readout.** Verifier ECE (expected calibration error) measured on the beam-selected prefixes vs on base-policy prefixes — this quantifies the distribution shift in §6 directly.

## 10. Worked Example

One MATH problem, beam width $b=2$, branching $k=2$, four steps. The PRM gives per-step scores for two surviving prefixes at depth 3:

| Prefix | $\hat r_1$ | $\hat r_2$ | $\hat r_3$ | last | min | product | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0.95 | 0.40 | 0.92 | **0.92** | 0.40 | 0.350 | 0.757 |
| B | 0.80 | 0.78 | 0.75 | 0.75 | **0.75** | **0.468** | **0.777** |

Last-step scoring keeps A; min, product and mean all keep B. The two rules disagree on a single-beam decision, so at $b=1$ they produce entirely different trajectories.

Now measure which is right. Roll out $m=8$ completions from each prefix under the generator and score by exact match: A gets 5/8, B gets 3/8. So $\hat V(A) = 0.625$, $\hat V(B) = 0.375$ — last-step wins here. But the standard error of each estimate is $\sqrt{0.625\cdot0.375/8} = 0.17$; the difference $0.25 \pm 0.24$ (pooled SE) barely clears zero. Reaching $\pm 0.05$ needs $m \approx 200$ rollouts *per prefix*, and a width-4 depth-8 beam has 32 such decisions per problem — about 6,400 rollouts to adjudicate one problem's worth of ranking.

The obstruction is visible in the arithmetic. Step 2 of A scores 0.40 — the PRM flags a step it finds unusual — yet A's actual continuation value is higher. The PRM was trained on base-policy rollouts where a 0.40 step usually preceded failure; A's step 2 is an unusual-but-valid substitution, exactly the OOD region beam search steers into. `min` punishes it because $\min$ is maximally sensitive to a single miscalibrated score; `last` ignores it because the recovery is visible by step 3. Neither rule is more principled — they differ only in which verifier error mode they happen to be robust to, and which mode dominates is a property of the PRM's training distribution, not of reasoning. That is why the answer is not derivable from the aggregators alone, and why the §8 experiment must hold the verifier fixed and vary nothing else.
