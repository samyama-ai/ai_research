# Bradley-Terry Misspecification and Intransitive Preferences

> **Topic:** Alignment & Preference Learning · **ID:** `04-alignment/bradley-terry-misspecification-intransitivity` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement

RLHF and DPO both assume human pairwise preferences are generated by a Bradley-Terry (BT) model: a scalar reward $r(x,y)$ exists such that $P(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma(r(x,y_1) - r(x,y_2))$. This forces **stochastic transitivity** and a total order over responses. Human preference data is aggregated across annotators with different values, is context-dependent, and shows documented cycles. The question is what breaks when the assumption fails, and by how much.

Three variants, different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a preference dataset $D$, estimate how far the true preference kernel is from the BT family, separating genuine intransitivity from annotator noise and annotator heterogeneity. *Methodologically contested.*
- **Method.** Build an alignment objective that is well defined without BT — minimax/Nash winner, von Neumann winner. *Largely solved in principle since 2023-2024; the empirical gain is unresolved.*
- **Theory.** Bound the loss in policy value from fitting BT to a non-BT kernel, as a function of a misspecification radius. *Open in the form that would matter.*

Solving it means: a reproducible misspecification statistic on real data, plus a demonstration that the non-BT objective beats the BT objective by more than seed noise on a preference-independent outcome.

## 2. Formal Setting

Prompt $x \sim \rho$, responses $y \in \mathcal{Y}$. The ground object is the **preference kernel**
$$p(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) \in [0,1], \qquad p(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) + p(y_2 \succ y_1 \mid x) = 1 .$$

*As measured:* $\hat p_{ij} = k_{ij}/n_{ij}$, where $n_{ij}$ is the number of votes collected on pair $(i,j)$ and $k_{ij}$ the wins for $i$. Ties are either dropped or split $0.5$; the choice moves arena rankings and is rarely ablated.

**BT submodel.** $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{BT}} = \{p : \exists r,\; p_{ij} = \sigma(r_i - r_j)\}$. Misspecification radius, measured as a held-out log-loss gap in nats per vote:
$$\Delta_{\mathrm{BT}} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{ij} n_{ij} H(\hat p_{ij})}_{\text{unconstrained}} \;-\; \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{ij} n_{ij}\,\mathrm{CE}(\hat p_{ij}, \sigma(\hat r_i - \hat r_j))}_{\text{BT fit}} ,$$
sign-flipped so $\Delta_{\mathrm{BT}} \ge 0$ measures how much BT gives up. $H$ is binary entropy in nats.

**Intransitivity.** A triple $(i,j,k)$ is cyclic if $\hat p_{ij}, \hat p_{jk}, \hat p_{ki} > 1/2$. Report the **excess** cycle rate: observed cycle fraction minus the fraction under a parametric bootstrap from the fitted BT model at matched $n_{ij}$. Raw cycle counts are meaningless — near-equal models cycle by sampling noise alone.

**Nash / minimax winner.** With $\Pi$ the policy set and $P(\pi,\pi') = \mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi, y'\sim\pi'}[p(y\succ y')]$, the preference game is symmetric zero-sum and always has an equilibrium in mixed strategies:
$$\pi^\star \in \arg\max_{\pi \in \Delta(\Pi)} \min_{\pi' \in \Delta(\Pi)} P(\pi, \pi') , \qquad P(\pi^\star,\pi^\star)=\tfrac12 .$$
Under BT, $\pi^\star$ is the point mass on $\arg\max_i r_i$; without BT it can require support on several responses.

**Assumptions known violated in practice.** (i) Transitivity — violated in human choice under attribute tradeoffs (Tversky 1969). (ii) Annotator homogeneity — a single $p$ pooled over raters is a *social choice aggregate*; Condorcet cycles arise from transitive individuals. (iii) Independence of irrelevant alternatives — violated by position bias, length bias and list context. (iv) Stationarity — arena data drifts as prompt mix and model pool change.

## 3. State of the Art

**Theory SOTA (established).** Zhu, Jordan, Jiao (ICML 2023) give finite-sample $\tilde O(\sqrt{d/n})$ guarantees for pessimistic MLE under linear BT *with coverage* — correctly specified. Azar et al. (AISTATS 2024, IPO) show BT's logit link is the failure point when preferences are near-deterministic: $\sigma^{-1}(p)\to\infty$, so the KL penalty in DPO stops binding and the optimum degenerates. Their $\Psi$PO/IPO objective works directly on $p$ and needs no BT. Dudík et al. (COLT 2015) established the von Neumann winner as the right solution concept for general (possibly intransitive) dueling bandits.

**Method SOTA (established, mechanism-level).** Nash-MD (Munos et al., ICML 2024), SPO (Swamy et al., ICML 2024), DNO (Rosset et al., 2024), SPPO (Wu et al., 2024) all optimize the game rather than a fitted reward, with convergence guarantees to (approximate) equilibria under stated oracle access. SPO's contribution is sharp: because the preference game is symmetric, *single-player* no-regret self-play suffices — no adversary training loop, no reward model.

**Claimed but unablated.** The headline evidence for these methods is AlpacaEval 2.0 / MT-Bench / Arena-Hard numbers against DPO baselines (e.g. SPPO reporting large length-controlled AlpacaEval 2.0 gains from Mistral-7B-Instruct). These are benchmark numbers only. They do not isolate intransitivity: the same methods also change the data distribution (on-policy, iterative) and the loss geometry. No published ablation holds data collection fixed, injects a controlled amount of intransitivity, and shows the gap scales with it.

**Measurement SOTA.** Chatbot Arena (Chiang et al., ICML 2024) fits BT with bootstrap CIs at $>10^6$ votes; Bertrand, Czarnecki, Gidel (AISTATS 2023) show formally that Elo/BT cannot represent cyclic structure and propose a disc-decomposition alternative.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Human intransitivity is real but modest at the individual level.** Tversky (1969) found systematic, replicable intransitivity in lab gamble choices — but only in a minority of subjects (roughly 6 of 18 pre-screened), under adversarially designed attribute tradeoffs. May (1954), $n=62$ students choosing marriage partners on three attributes, reported intransitive triads in about 27% of subjects.
- **Aggregation manufactures cycles.** Condorcet: three transitive rater groups in equal proportion yield a $2/3$-cycle over three items. This is a theorem, not an empirical finding, and it applies directly to pooled RLHF data.
- **Annotator agreement is low.** Inter-annotator agreement on RLHF-style helpfulness comparisons is commonly reported in the 60-75% range (e.g. InstructGPT, Ouyang et al. 2022, reports ~72-77% agreement among trained labelers). Any kernel with $p$ near $0.5$ is where cycles live.
- **BT fits arena data well on average.** At Chatbot Arena scale ($>10^6$ votes, $\sim$100 models), BT is used as the production ranking model and its bootstrap CIs are stable; published held-out log-loss gaps versus an unconstrained pairwise model are not routinely reported.
- **Equilibrium existence is unconditional.** The symmetric preference game always has a Nash equilibrium with value $1/2$; the minimax winner is well defined for *any* kernel, unlike the BT argmax.
- **BT's failure mode under near-deterministic preferences is proved,** not just observed (Azar et al. 2024).

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open.** The size of $\Delta_{\mathrm{BT}}$ and the excess cycle rate on real, annotator-identified preference data at scale. The data exists (arena logs, HH-RLHF, UltraFeedback); the per-annotator decomposition has not been published at $10^5$-$10^6$ votes.
- **Empirically open.** Whether any measured gain of Nash/self-play methods over DPO is *attributable* to intransitivity rather than to on-policy data. Runnable today at 7B; nobody has run the controlled version.
- **Theoretically open.** A tight bound on regret of the BT-argmax policy against the minimax winner as a function of a misspecification radius $\epsilon = \inf_{q \in \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{BT}}} \|p - q\|_\infty$. Worst-case constructions exist; a matching upper bound with realistic structural assumptions does not.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Separating "intransitive preference" from "heterogeneous transitive population" from "noisy annotator" using pooled pairwise votes alone. Without repeated triads per annotator, the three are not identifiable (see §10).

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Non-identifiability is the specific obstruction.** Pooled pairwise data of the form (prompt, $y_1$, $y_2$, winner) is a sufficient statistic for the aggregate kernel $p$ and for nothing else. A cyclic $p$ is generated identically by (a) genuinely intransitive raters, (b) a mixture of transitive raters with different orderings, and (c) a transitive kernel plus context-dependent bias. These have opposite implications: (a) and (b) call for a mixed-strategy or per-population target; (c) calls for debiasing, not a new objective. Distinguishing them requires per-annotator IDs on *closed triads* — which arena-style random pairing almost never produces, because the probability that one annotator sees all three pairs of a triple is vanishing under uniform pairing over $\sim$100 models.

Secondary: the evaluation does not measure what it names. AlpacaEval and Arena-Hard score against a single fixed judge — a *transitive* comparator by construction. A method that correctly learns a mixed equilibrium can score no better on such a benchmark than a method that learns the judge's argmax.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Game-theoretic alignment.** Google DeepMind (Nash-MD lineage), CMU/Microsoft Research (SPO, DNO), UCLA (SPPO). Direction: cheaper equilibrium computation, regularized Nash with KL anchors, theory for KL-regularized NLHF (Ye et al.).
- **Preference heterogeneity and pluralism.** Distributional and multi-objective reward models; social-choice framings of RLHF that treat the annotator pool as an electorate rather than a noisy oracle *(frontier — verify current state)*.
- **Evaluation repair.** Length-controlled AlpacaEval, style-controlled arena rankings, and Nash-averaging-style evaluation (Balduzzi et al., NeurIPS 2018) as replacements for Elo.
- **Measurement.** Instrumented annotation collection designed to close triads per annotator *(frontier — verify; not, to my knowledge, published at scale)*.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Two arms, one number each.**

*Arm 1 — measurement.* Collect $2\times10^5$ pairwise votes over a fixed pool of 12 models on a fixed prompt set, with **triad-closed assignment**: each annotator is served all three pairs of a randomly chosen model triple, in randomized order, with $\ge 30$ triples per annotator. Cost estimate: ~$60$k at $\$0.30$/vote, or a fraction of that with an LLM-judge replicate arm.
**Control:** parametric bootstrap resampling under the fitted BT model at identical $n_{ij}$ and identical annotator assignment.
**Deciding number:** the *within-annotator* excess cycle rate. If it is $\le 1$ percentage point above the BT bootstrap, individual intransitivity is not the problem and all observed pooled cycles are aggregation or noise — the field should stop citing intransitivity as motivation. If it exceeds 5 points, BT is misspecified at the individual level and mixed-strategy targets are justified.

*Arm 2 — method attribution.* Fix one 7B base model and one frozen preference oracle. Train four policies on **identical prompt/response pairs**: (i) DPO, (ii) IPO, (iii) SPPO, (iv) DPO on a re-labeled dataset where labels are forced transitive by resolving cycles to the BT fit. Three seeds each.
**Control:** arm (iv) — the transitivity-forced dataset — is the arm that isolates intransitivity from every other difference.
**Deciding number:** the win rate of (iii) against (iv), evaluated by round-robin against the full model pool (not a single judge), reported as the minimax value $\min_{\pi'} P(\pi,\pi')$. A gap $\ge 3$ points with non-overlapping seed ranges attributes the benefit to intransitivity; a gap under 1 point attributes it to on-policy data.

## 10. Worked Example

Three models $A,B,C$; measured kernel $\hat p_{AB} = \hat p_{BC} = \hat p_{CA} = 0.60$.

**Step 1 — BT cannot fit it.** The cycle is symmetric, so the MLE is $r_A=r_B=r_C$ and every fitted $p$ is $0.50$. Per-vote cross-entropy under BT is $\ln 2 = 0.6931$ nats; the unconstrained model achieves $H(0.6)=0.6730$ nats. So $\Delta_{\mathrm{BT}} = 0.0201$ nats/vote.

**Step 2 — how much data detects it.** Likelihood-ratio statistic with $n$ votes per pair: $2 \cdot 3n \cdot 0.0201 = 0.121n$, against $\chi^2_1$ (3 free pairwise parameters vs. 2 in BT). Rejection at $0.05$ needs $0.121n > 3.84$, i.e. $n \ge 32$ votes per pair — 96 votes total. A weaker cycle at $0.53$ needs $n \ge 368$ per pair. **Detection is cheap.**

**Step 3 — where it actually breaks.** Two generative stories fit these numbers exactly:

| Story | Composition | Aggregate kernel |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Intransitive raters | all raters cycle with strength 0.6 | 0.60 / 0.60 / 0.60 |
| (b) Transitive electorate | 60% order $A\!\succ\!B\!\succ\!C$, 20% $B\!\succ\!C\!\succ\!A$, 20% $C\!\succ\!A\!\succ\!B$ | 0.60 / 0.80 / 0.40 |

Story (b) as written is distinguishable — but re-weight to thirds with within-group noise and you reproduce (a)'s numbers to within sampling error. Pooled votes give you three numbers; the space of rater-population mixtures consistent with them is a continuum. **That is the obstruction: the test in Step 2 is powerful and cheap, and it tells you nothing about which story you are in.** Only per-annotator closed triads break the tie.

**Step 4 — the cost of guessing wrong.** Under story (a) the minimax winner is uniform over $\{A,B,C\}$ with value $0.50$; any deterministic choice loses 60/40 to one competitor — 10 points of regret. Under story (b) the right answer is a *pluralistic* system serving different populations, and deploying the uniform mixture serves 60% of users their third choice 33% of the time. Same data, same $\Delta_{\mathrm{BT}}$, incompatible deployments.
