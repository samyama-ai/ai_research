# Whether Diffusion Models Learn a Compositional Factorization

> **Topic:** Diffusion & Generative Modeling · **ID:** `34-diffusion-generative/diffusion-compositional-factorization` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

A diffusion model trained on data generated by independent latent factors (object identity, color, position, pose, style) can produce combinations of factor values it never saw together. The question is **why**, and the two candidate answers make different predictions.

- **Factorization hypothesis.** The learned score function decomposes, at least approximately, into per-factor terms. Novel combinations are produced because the model represents factors separately and recombines them.
- **Interpolation hypothesis.** The model has no factorized internal structure; novel combinations fall inside the convex-ish support induced by smoothing plus the architecture's locality/equivariance bias, and would be produced by any sufficiently smooth density estimator with the same inductive bias.

Three variants, of very different difficulty:

- **Measurement variant.** Define a scalar $\varepsilon$ — a *factorization defect* — computable from a trained model, that is zero iff the score factorizes over a given factor partition, and that is not confounded by sample quality. Currently ill-posed: the factor partition is not identified from data.
- **Method variant.** Build a training procedure whose defect $\varepsilon$ is provably smaller than a standard DDPM at matched FID and matched compute.
- **Theory variant.** For a stated data-generating process (e.g. hierarchical latent tree, sparse-feature linear model), prove that the minimizer of the denoising loss over a stated function class does or does not factorize, and give the sample complexity of the held-out combination.

Solving the page means: settling the measurement variant well enough that the empirical variant becomes decidable, then running it.

## 2. Formal Setting

Data $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ generated by latent factors $z = (z_1,\dots,z_K)$, $z_k \in \mathcal{Z}_k$ finite, with $p(z) = \prod_k p(z_k)$ and a deterministic renderer $x = g(z)$ plus observation noise. Training set covers a subset $S \subsetneq \prod_k \mathcal{Z}_k$ of combinations; $H = (\prod_k \mathcal{Z}_k) \setminus S$ is held out.

Forward process $x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon$. The model learns $s_\theta(x,t,c) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid c)$ for a conditioning signal $c$ (text embedding or factor one-hots).

**Exact factorization.** If $c=(c_1,\dots,c_K)$ and the $c_k$ are conditionally independent given $x$, then

$$\nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid c_1,\dots,c_K) = \sum_{k=1}^K \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid c_k) - (K-1)\,\nabla_x \log p_t(x).$$

This identity is the composition rule behind Composable Diffusion (Liu et al., ECCV 2022) and the energy-based composition of Du et al. (NeurIPS 2020). **Measured defect:**

$$\varepsilon(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U},\, c \sim p(c),\, x \sim p_t(\cdot\mid c)} \left[ \frac{\big\| s_\theta(x,t,c) - \sum_k s_\theta(x,t,c_k) + (K-1)s_\theta(x,t,\varnothing) \big\|_2^2}{\big\| s_\theta(x,t,c) \big\|_2^2} \right].$$

Every term is a forward pass; $\varepsilon$ is cheap. Report it per noise level $t$, not marginalized — the defect is $t$-dependent and marginalizing hides where it lives.

**Representation-side defect.** For hidden activations $h_\ell(x,t,c) \in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ at layer $\ell$, fit a linear partition into subspaces $\{P_k\}$ and measure the fraction of variance in $P_k$ explained by $z_k$ versus $z_{j\neq k}$; the mutual-information gap $\mathrm{MIG}$ of Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2018) is the standard scalar.

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**
- *Factor independence $p(z)=\prod_k p(z_k)$.* Violated in every real image corpus: "husky" correlates with snow, "chef" with kitchen. Text-to-image factorization defect is therefore not expected to be zero even for a perfect model.
- *Conditional independence of $c_k$ given $x$.* Violated whenever factors interact through the renderer (occlusion, lighting, relations like "left of"). Relational conditions are provably not of the additive form.
- *Known factor partition.* Not identified. Nonlinear ICA is unidentifiable without auxiliary structure (Hyvärinen & Pajunen, 1999); any $\varepsilon$ is defined relative to a chosen partition, and a model can factorize over a basis the auditor did not test.
- *Exact score.* $s_\theta$ is a trained approximation; $\varepsilon$ mixes true non-factorization with approximation error. Controlled by measuring $\varepsilon$ against a held-in combination baseline.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established.**
- Score composition works well enough to be useful and fails predictably. Composable Diffusion (Liu, Li, Du, Tenenbaum, Torralba, ECCV 2022) shows AND/NOT composition via the additive rule beats single-prompt conditioning on multi-concept CLEVR and text prompts; it also documents failure when concepts conflict.
- Compositional generalization improves *with training* and follows a locality scale. Favero, Sclocchi, Cagnetta, Wyart (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.12089) train on hierarchical synthetic data and show the model first learns local rules, then longer-range ones; combination-novel samples appear at a predictable sample complexity.
- A forward-backward "phase transition" separates the noise range where class/global identity is retained from where low-level detail is resampled (Sclocchi, Favero, Wyart, *PNAS* 2025; arXiv:2402.16991). This gives a $t$-axis on which factors are separable.

**Claimed but unablated.**
- That composition emerges *multiplicatively* from per-concept abilities. Okawa, Lubana, Dick, Tanaka (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2310.09336) show on a synthetic concept graph that the ability to generate a combination is roughly the product of per-concept capabilities. Convincing on their toy setup; not reproduced at text-to-image scale.
- That concepts live in linear subspaces of the score, so concept algebra applies. Wang, Gui, Negrea, Veitch (NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2302.03693) formalize this for text-conditioned score models under a stated independence assumption; the assumption is asserted, not tested, for Stable Diffusion.
- That convolutional locality plus equivariance *alone* explains novel-combination generation. Kamb & Ganguli (arXiv:2412.20292) derive an analytic patch-mosaic score that predicts real trained-model outputs; strong evidence for the interpolation hypothesis, but demonstrated on small models/datasets.

**Benchmark-only numbers.** T2I-CompBench (Huang et al., NeurIPS 2023 Datasets) and GenEval (Ghosh, Hajishirzi, Schmidt, NeurIPS 2023) report attribute-binding and counting scores. These are *evaluator scores* (BLIP-VQA, object detector), not measurements of internal factorization; a model can score well by memorizing frequent pairs.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Binding fails at scale.** On GenEval, SDXL reaches roughly $0.55$ overall with attribute-binding subtasks near $0.2$–$0.25$, while counting and position remain the weakest columns; later DiT-family models push overall past $0.8$ with heavy prompt/caption engineering. Scale: 1–3 B parameter T2I models, 553 prompts.
- **Attention is causally implicated in binding.** Attend-and-Excite (Chefer, Alaluf, Vinker, Wolf, Cohen-Or, SIGGRAPH 2023) raises multi-object presence on Stable Diffusion 1.4 by intervening on cross-attention maps at inference with no retraining — evidence that some factor information is localized, not that the score factorizes.
- **Composition is not free.** Liu et al. (ECCV 2022) report that the additive rule degrades as the number of composed concepts grows past ~3 on CLEVR-style data.
- **Generalization onset is memorization-limited.** Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.02557) show two diffusion models trained on disjoint subsets of the same distribution converge to near-identical denoisers once $n$ is large enough (order $10^5$ images at $80\times80$), and that the learned denoiser is well described by geometry-adaptive harmonic bases — a non-factorized explanation of generalization.
- **Emergence order.** Park, Okawa, Lubana et al. (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2406.19370) find in "concept space" that the capability to manipulate a concept exists internally *before* it is expressible from the naive prompt; prompt-based evaluation understates factorization.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked.** Whether *any* partition-free factorization measure exists. $\varepsilon$ is defined only relative to a chosen $\{c_k\}$; nonlinear identifiability results say the partition is not recoverable from data alone. Until this is fixed, "the model does/doesn't factorize" is not a well-posed empirical claim about a real T2I model.
- **Empirically open.** Given a *synthetic* dataset where the partition is known by construction, nobody has measured $\varepsilon(t)$ across a $10^2\to10^9$ parameter sweep with held-out combinations and matched FID. The experiment is runnable on a few hundred GPU-hours. The question of whether $\varepsilon$ decreases, increases, or is flat with scale is unanswered.
- **Empirically open.** Whether Kamb–Ganguli locality/equivariance predicts held-out-combination samples for a 1 B-parameter model on real images, or only for small CNNs.
- **Theoretically open.** No theorem states that the denoising-loss minimizer over a stated architecture class factorizes over independent latents, nor a lower bound on the number of observed combinations $|S|$ needed to generalize to $H$. Favero et al. give a sample-complexity scale for a specific hierarchical model; the general case is open.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The obstruction is **non-identifiability of the factor basis compounded by an evaluation that does not measure what it names.**

1. Any invertible reparameterization $z \mapsto \phi(z)$ that preserves the data preserves the loss. A model factorizing over $\phi(z)$ scores $\varepsilon > 0$ under the auditor's $z$ and is falsely called non-compositional. There is no way to enumerate candidate bases at $d \sim 10^5$.
2. Benchmark scores (GenEval, T2I-CompBench) route through a VQA model or detector. A failure could be the generator, the evaluator, or the prompt. Chefer et al. show scores move without touching weights — the number is a joint property of generator, sampler and judge.
3. The confound with sample quality: any regularizer that lowers $\varepsilon$ also smooths the score and can lower FID for unrelated reasons. Without a matched-FID, matched-compute control arm, $\varepsilon$ differences are uninterpretable.
4. Real corpora violate factor independence, so the ground-truth $\varepsilon$ is unknown and nonzero. Only synthetic data has ground truth, and synthetic results have not transferred.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Analytic theory of composition/creativity.** Kamb & Ganguli (Stanford); Sclocchi, Favero, Cagnetta, Wyart (EPFL) on hierarchical-data phase transitions and compositional sample complexity. Active, well-posed, small scale.
- **Compositional generative modeling as an architecture program.** Du & Kaelbling, *Compositional Generative Modeling: A Single Model Is Not All You Need* (ICML 2024 position paper) — argues for explicitly composed energy/diffusion modules rather than hoping factorization emerges.
- **Concept directions and model editing.** Concept-algebra and concept-erasure lines (Veitch group; ESD-style erasure work) test factorization indirectly: if removing a concept leaves others intact, some separation exists. *(frontier — verify)* Reports of entangled collateral damage during erasure are the main negative evidence.
- **Mechanistic interpretability of DiTs.** Sparse-autoencoder probes of diffusion-transformer activations for factor-aligned features. *(frontier — verify)* — early, no consensus metric.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Scale.** Synthetic 3-factor sprite dataset: $K=3$ (8 colors × 8 shapes × 8 positions = 512 cells), $64\times64$ RGB, 400 images per observed cell. Hold out $|H|=64$ cells (12.5%) chosen so every factor value appears in $S$. Train a DDPM/DiT at four sizes — 5 M, 50 M, 500 M, 2 B parameters — each to matched training loss on held-in data, 3 seeds. Roughly 300–600 A100-hours total.

**Control arm.** Same architectures, same compute, trained on a *shuffled-factor* corpus where the renderer is replaced by a lookup table assigning each of the 512 cells an unrelated image. This corpus has identical marginals and cardinality but no compositional structure, so its $\varepsilon$ is the floor produced by approximation error alone.

**The deciding number.** The gap
$$\Delta(N) = \varepsilon_{\text{shuffled}}(N) - \varepsilon_{\text{compositional}}(N)$$
evaluated on held-out cells $H$ at $t$ in the mid-noise band identified by the Sclocchi phase transition, as a function of parameter count $N$.

- $\Delta$ growing with $N$ and $\varepsilon_{\text{compositional}} \to 0$: factorization hypothesis supported.
- $\Delta \approx 0$ within seed noise (say $<0.02$ absolute) at all four scales while held-out FID still improves: interpolation hypothesis supported — novel combinations without factorized scores.

Report jointly with held-out-cell FID so a low $\varepsilon$ from a degenerate model is not mistaken for success.

## 10. Worked Example

Take $K=2$, $d=2$, everything Gaussian so the true score is closed-form. Factors shift the mean: $c_1 \Rightarrow \mu_1=(2,0)$, $c_2 \Rightarrow \mu_2=(0,2)$, unit isotropic covariance, and the null condition has mean $(0,0)$.

If the true joint is $\mathcal{N}((2,2), I)$ — exact factorization — the additive rule reproduces it identically and $\varepsilon = 0$.

Now break it by a renderer interaction: the true joint is $\mathcal{N}((1.6,1.6), I)$, a shrinkage of $\delta = 0.4$ per coordinate. A perfectly trained model has score $s(x \mid c) = (1.6,1.6) - x$, but the composed estimate is $(2,2)-x$. The residual is constant, $\|(0.4,0.4)\|_2 = 0.566$. At $\|s\|^2 \approx \mathbb{E}\|x - \mu\|^2 = 2$, the defect is

$$\varepsilon = 0.566^2 / 2 = 0.16.$$

A 16% defect is large. Now ask what an evaluator sees. Samples from the two distributions differ by a mean shift of $0.4\sigma$ per coordinate, Mahalanobis distance $0.566$. The Bayes-optimal classifier separating them achieves AUC $\approx \Phi(0.566/\sqrt{2}) = 0.66$. A two-sample test at 80% power, $\alpha=0.05$, needs roughly $n \approx 16/\delta_{\text{eff}}^2 = 16/0.32 \approx 50$ samples per arm to detect the shift *given you know the direction to test*.

That last clause is the obstruction. In $d=2$ with a known factor basis, 50 samples settle it. In a real T2I model, $d \sim 10^5$ pixels, the factor basis is unknown, and the shift direction is one of exponentially many. A BLIP-VQA judge asking "is the cube red?" projects the residual onto a single axis it happens to name; a $0.16$ defect lying in an unnamed direction — lighting coupling, texture-shape entanglement — projects to approximately zero on every benchmark axis and the model scores as fully compositional. Conversely, a model with $\varepsilon = 0$ over its *own* basis $\phi(z)$ scores badly on the auditor's basis. The Gaussian case is decidable because the basis is given; the real case is blocked at exactly the step where the basis must be supplied.
