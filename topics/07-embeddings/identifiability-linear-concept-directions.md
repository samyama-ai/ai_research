# Provable Identifiability of Linear Concept Directions

> **Topic:** Embeddings & Representations · **ID:** `07-embeddings/identifiability-linear-concept-directions` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

A trained model maps inputs to activations $h(x)\in\mathbb{R}^d$. The linear representation hypothesis says human-interpretable concepts (sentiment, truth, gender, "is a capital city") correspond to directions $v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}$ such that the concept can be read off by $\langle v,h\rangle$ and written by $h\mapsto h+\alpha v$. The problem: **give conditions under which $v$ is uniquely determined by observable data, and an estimator that provably recovers it.**

Three variants, of very different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a concept label set, estimate a direction. Solved many ways (probes, difference-in-means, SAE latents) — but the estimators disagree with each other and no criterion says which is right.
- **Method.** Give an algorithm whose output is stable across seeds, architectures, and probe families. Empirically open.
- **Theory.** State assumptions on the data-generating process and the observation map under which the concept direction is identifiable up to sign and scale, and prove the estimator is consistent. This is the open problem.

Solving it means: a theorem of the form *"under assumptions A1–A4, any two estimators consistent with the observed distribution recover the same direction up to $\pm$ scale,"* plus a check that A1–A4 hold, or measurably fail, in a real language model.

## 2. Formal Setting

Latent concepts $z\in\mathbb{R}^k$ with distribution $p(z)$. Representation generated by

$$h = A z + \varepsilon,\qquad A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},\ \varepsilon\perp z,\ \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon]=0.$$

Column $a_j$ is the concept direction for $z_j$. Identifiability target: recover $A$ up to right-multiplication by a signed permutation, $A' = A\Pi D$, $\Pi$ a permutation, $D$ diagonal with $|D_{jj}|>0$.

**Quantities as measured.**

- *Probe direction.* $\hat v_{\text{lr}}=\arg\min_v \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \log(1+e^{-y_i\langle v,h_i\rangle}) + \lambda\|v\|^2$, on $n$ labelled activations at a fixed layer, normalised to unit norm.
- *Difference-in-means.* $\hat v_{\text{dim}} \propto \mathbb{E}[h\mid y=1]-\mathbb{E}[h\mid y=0]$, optionally whitened: $\Sigma^{-1}\hat v_{\text{dim}}$ with $\Sigma=\mathrm{Cov}(h)$.
- *Dictionary/SAE column.* Train $\hat h = b + \sum_j f_j(h)\, d_j$ with $f$ sparse (L1 or TopK); the concept direction is the decoder column $d_j$ of the latent whose activation best matches the label, scored by F1 or AUC.
- *Causal score.* Steering effect $\Delta(\alpha,v)=\mathbb{E}[\,\ell_c(h+\alpha v) - \ell_c(h)\,]$ where $\ell_c$ is the model's own logit for the concept, and off-target leakage $\Delta(\alpha,v)$ measured on unrelated concepts $c'$.
- *Cross-seed agreement.* $\mathrm{MCS} = \frac{1}{k}\sum_j \max_{j'} |\langle d_j^{(1)}, d_{j'}^{(2)}\rangle|$ over two runs differing only in seed.

**Assumptions, and their status.**

- **A1 Linearity** — $h$ is affine in $z$. Violated: Engels et al. (2025) show days-of-week and months occupy irreducible 2D circular manifolds.
- **A2 Independence of latents** — required by ICA-style identifiability. Violated badly: concepts in natural corpora are correlated (gender–occupation, sentiment–topic).
- **A3 Sparsity** — each $h$ uses $s\ll k$ latents. Plausible for token-level features, false for always-on properties (language, register, position).
- **A4 Overcompleteness with incoherence** — $\max_{i\neq j}|\langle a_i,a_j\rangle|\le \mu$. Superposition arguments (Elhage et al. 2022) predict $k\gg d$, so $\mu$ cannot be small; the required incoherence bounds are unverified at real $d$.
- **A5 Environment/auxiliary variation** — nonlinear ICA identifiability needs an observed $u$ (time, class, context) making $z\mid u$ vary. No canonical $u$ exists for LLM activations.

## 3. State of the Art

**Theory SOTA — established.**
- Nonlinear ICA is unidentifiable without side information (Hyvärinen & Pajunen, *Neural Networks* 1999): infinitely many independent-component decompositions exist.
- With auxiliary variable $u$ and conditionally exponential-family latents, identifiability up to permutation and componentwise transform holds (Khemakhem et al., iVAE, AISTATS 2020).
- Models trained with a contrastive/conditional objective are identifiable *up to a linear map* — the representation is unique only modulo $GL(k)$ (Roeder, Metz & Kingma, ICML 2021). This is exactly the gap: linear identifiability does not pin down individual directions.
- Sparse dictionary learning is identifiable for complete dictionaries with $s=O(\sqrt{d})$ nonzeros (Spielman, Wang & Wright, COLT 2012) and for overcomplete incoherent dictionaries at $s\le \tilde O(\sqrt{d}/\mu)$ (Arora, Ge & Moitra, COLT 2014). These are the strongest results that apply to SAEs — and their hypotheses have never been checked on LLM activations.

**Empirical SOTA — claimed but unablated.**
- Sparse autoencoders at scale: 34M latents on Claude 3 Sonnet (Templeton et al., 2024); 16M-latent TopK SAE on GPT-4 (Gao et al., 2024). Both report interpretability and steering demos. Neither establishes that the recovered directions are *the* directions; both are benchmark-number results.
- Park et al. (ICML 2024) derive a "causal inner product" — whitening by the unembedding covariance — under which unembedding and embedding concept representations align. Established as a construction and validated on curated concept pairs; not established as recovering ground-truth latents.
- Difference-in-means outperforms trained probes for *steering* on truth-related concepts in LLaMA-scale models (Marks & Tegmark, 2023). Reproduced; explanation contested.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Seeds disagree.** Locatello et al. (ICML 2019) trained 12,800 disentanglement models: unsupervised disentanglement is impossible without inductive bias, and random seed explains more variance in disentanglement scores than the choice of method or hyperparameters. The result is about VAEs but the identifiability obstruction is the same one.
- **SAEs inherit that instability.** Paulo & Belrose (2025) report that SAEs trained on identical data with different seeds learn substantially different latent sets — a large fraction of latents have no high-cosine partner in the sibling run. Measured on Pythia-scale models.
- **Probe accuracy is nearly saturated and nearly useless as a discriminator.** On standard concept-probing sets, linear probes at mid-layers of 1B–70B models reach 90–99% accuracy for many concepts; directions from different estimators that all achieve >95% can have pairwise cosine well below 1.
- **Linear analogies have a proven generative explanation.** Arora et al. (TACL 2016) prove that under a log-linear random-walk model, PMI-based word vectors satisfy analogy relations — the one case where "concept = direction" is a theorem, not an observation. It assumes isotropy, which contextual embeddings violate.
- **Some concepts are provably not one-dimensional.** Engels et al. (ICLR 2025) isolate multi-dimensional, irreducible features (weekday, month) in GPT-2 and Mistral 7B.
- **Steering works and leaks.** Steering vectors reliably shift target behaviour and reliably move correlated attributes; magnitude of leakage is reported per-paper, not standardised.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No theorem gives identifiability of individual concept directions for the regime that actually holds in LLMs: overcomplete ($k\gg d$), correlated latents, no auxiliary environment variable, partially non-sparse. Existing results each break exactly one of A2–A5. Nobody has proved impossibility for this regime either — the open question has both directions unresolved.
- **Empirically open.** Whether a *synthetic-ground-truth* transformer — trained on data with known, controlled latent factors at $\geq$100M parameters — yields SAE/probe directions that recover the planted $A$ up to signed permutation. The experiment is runnable today; it has been run only at toy scale (2–10 features, MLP-only models).
- **Methodologically blocked.** "The concept direction" has no operational definition independent of an estimator. There is no accepted ground truth for a natural-language concept in a pretrained model, so recovery error cannot be computed — only inter-estimator agreement, which is compatible with all estimators being wrong the same way.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The obstruction is **non-identifiability compounded by absent ground truth**.

Non-identifiability: if $h=Az$ and $z$ has correlated components, then $h = (AM^{-1})(Mz)$ for any invertible $M$ preserving the observed covariance. Second-order statistics fix $A$ only up to rotation; higher-order statistics fix it only if latents are independent and non-Gaussian (A2), which is false. Sparsity can substitute for independence — that is the SAE bet — but the sparsity theorems require $s\le\tilde O(\sqrt{d}/\mu)$ and incoherence that superposition rules out.

Absent ground truth: the standard evaluation is probe accuracy or an interpretability score on the recovered latent. Both are invariant to the rotation ambiguity. **The evaluation does not measure the thing it names**: a direction that is a fixed mixture of "gender" and "occupation" scores 99% on a gender probe and reads as "gender" to an auto-interpreter, while being causally wrong.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Causal representation learning applied to foundation models.** Rajendran, Buchholz, Aragam, Schölkopf & Ravikumar show concepts are identifiable given atomic interventions or concept-conditional data — trading the unsupervised setting for an interventional one. The open direction is what the minimal intervention set is for a real LLM. *(frontier — verify current status)*
- **Structured / matryoshka / cross-layer SAEs**, aimed at making the decomposition less seed-dependent (Anthropic, EleutherAI, Goodfire). Stability improvements are reported; identifiability guarantees are not. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Benchmarks with partial ground truth.** SAEBench (Karvonen et al., 2025) and the principled-evaluation protocol of Makelov, Lange & Nanda (ICLR 2024), which uses the IOI task in GPT-2-small where the causal variables are known.
- **Non-linear feature geometry** (Tegmark group, MIT): circular and manifold features, and what identifiability means when the feature is not a direction.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question:** does any current estimator recover planted concept directions when the planted latents are correlated?

**Scale.** Train two 160M-parameter transformers on a synthetic corpus generated from $k=512$ known binary latent concepts with a controlled correlation matrix $R$, sweeping $\rho\in\{0,0.2,0.4,0.6\}$ for a designated concept pair, 10B tokens, two seeds each. The generating process is a templated grammar, so $A$ is not planted directly in activations — it must be learned — but the ground-truth latent value of every concept on every token is known.

**Arms.** (i) TopK SAE, $k_{\text{latents}}=32{,}768$; (ii) logistic probe per concept; (iii) difference-in-means, whitened; (iv) **control arm:** the same estimators run on the $\rho=0$ corpus, where independence makes the problem identifiable in principle. A second control: a random rotation of the true $A$ within the span, which must score *worse* than the estimators or the metric is vacuous.

**Deciding number.** Mean absolute cosine between the estimated direction and the ground-truth planted direction, $\overline{|\langle \hat v_j, a_j\rangle|}$, over the 512 concepts, at $\rho=0.6$.

- $\ge 0.90$ and within 0.05 of the $\rho=0$ control → correlation is not the binding obstruction; the identifiability question is about sparsity and overcompleteness alone.
- $\le 0.75$, i.e. a drop of $>0.15$ from the control, while probe accuracy stays $>0.95$ in both → confirms that current estimators return correlation-contaminated directions and that accuracy-based evaluation cannot detect it. That result alone reclassifies most published steering-vector claims as unvalidated.

## 10. Worked Example

Two orthonormal planted directions in $\mathbb{R}^d$: $a_1$ = "gender", $a_2$ = "stereotypically-female occupation". Latents $z_1,z_2\in\{-1,+1\}$, correlated with $\Pr[z_1z_2=1]=0.8$, so $\rho=\mathbb{E}[z_1z_2]=0.6$. Activations $h=a_1z_1+a_2z_2+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I)$, $\sigma=0.1$.

Difference-in-means on gender labels:

$$\mathbb{E}[h\mid z_1{=}1]-\mathbb{E}[h\mid z_1{=}{-}1] = 2a_1 + 2\rho a_2 \;\Rightarrow\; \hat v = \frac{a_1+0.6\,a_2}{\sqrt{1.36}} = 0.857\,a_1 + 0.514\,a_2 .$$

The estimate sits $31.0^\circ$ off the true gender direction. Whitening does not fix it: $\mathrm{Cov}(h)=I + \rho(a_1a_2^\top+a_2a_1^\top)+\sigma^2I$ is not isotropic in the $\{a_1,a_2\}$ plane, and $\Sigma^{-1}\hat v$ rotates the estimate toward $a_1$ only if $\rho$ is known — which is what we were trying to recover. A logistic probe converges to the same plane and, at $\sigma=0.1$, both $\hat v$ and $a_1$ give **100% held-out gender accuracy**. The evaluation cannot see the error.

The causal consequence: steering $h\mathrel{+}=\alpha\hat v$ moves the $z_1$ coordinate by $0.857\alpha$ and the $z_2$ coordinate by $0.514\alpha$ — a 60% leak into occupation, reported in papers as "gender steering also changes profession, as expected."

Now the identifiability point. An SAE with L1 sparsity could break the tie — but only if the latents are sparse. Here both concepts are active on every token ($z_j\in\{\pm1\}$, never zero), so the sparsity prior gives no signal, and the L1-optimal 2-latent decomposition of this data is the rotated pair $\{\hat v, \hat v^\perp\}$, not $\{a_1,a_2\}$. The obstruction is not estimation noise: at $n\to\infty$ and $\sigma\to0$ every estimator converges, and they converge to the wrong basis. Only an intervention — data where $z_1$ is set independently of $z_2$ — restores identifiability.
