# Compositionality Failures in Contrastive Vision-Language Embeddings

> **Topic:** Embeddings & Representations · **ID:** `07-embeddings/compositionality-failure-clip-embeddings` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

CLIP-style dual encoders map an image and a caption to a shared sphere and score them by inner product. They retrieve well and bind poorly: "the horse is eating the grass" and "the grass is eating the horse" receive nearly the same embedding, and the model picks the wrong one at close to chance. The problem is to explain and remove this failure.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Measurement.** Define a score that isolates *compositional* competence — sensitivity to argument structure, attribute–object binding, and relations — from the confounds of caption plausibility, grammaticality, and object recognition. No current benchmark cleanly does this; SugarCrepe showed the two most-cited ones (ARO, CREPE) are largely solvable by a text-only model.
- **Method.** Train or fine-tune a dual encoder that hits high accuracy on held-out compositional probes *without* losing zero-shot classification and retrieval, and *without* the negatives being in-distribution for its own training procedure.
- **Theory.** Determine whether the failure is (a) forced by the objective — a single inner product between two pooled vectors cannot represent binding at reachable $d$ — or (b) forced by the data — web captions carry little information about word order given the bag of words, so the InfoNCE minimizer ignores order — or (c) an optimization artifact removable by better negatives.

Solving it means: an ablated method that raises a confound-controlled compositional score materially above the bag-of-words baseline while holding ImageNet zero-shot and COCO retrieval flat, plus a statement of which of (a)/(b)/(c) was the binding constraint.

## 2. Formal Setting

Pairs $(x, c) \sim \mathcal{D}$, image $x$, caption $c$. Encoders $f_\theta: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, $g_\phi: \mathcal{C} \to \mathbb{S}^{d-1}$, similarity $s(x,c) = \langle f_\theta(x), g_\phi(c)\rangle / \tau$. Training minimizes the symmetric InfoNCE over batches of size $B$:

$$\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\left[\log \frac{e^{s(x_i,c_i)}}{\sum_j e^{s(x_i,c_j)}} + \log \frac{e^{s(x_i,c_i)}}{\sum_j e^{s(x_j,c_i)}}\right].$$

**Measured quantities.**

- *Hard-negative accuracy.* For a probe triple $(x, c^+, c^-)$ where $c^-$ is a compositional perturbation of $c^+$ (swap, replace, add): $\mathrm{Acc} = \mathbb{E}\,\mathbb{1}[s(x,c^+) > s(x,c^-)]$. Chance $= 0.5$.
- *Winoground group score.* Two images, two captions that are the same bag of words reordered; the item counts only if all four comparisons are correct. Chance $= 1/6 \approx 16.7\%$ for group, $25\%$ each for text and image scores.
- *Bag-of-words gap.* Let $\pi$ be a uniform random permutation of the tokens of $c$. Order sensitivity is
  $$\Delta_{\mathrm{BoW}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,c}\left[s(x,c) - \mathbb{E}_\pi\, s(x, \pi(c))\right],$$
  measured in units of the within-batch standard deviation of $s$. A model that is exactly a bag of words has $\Delta_{\mathrm{BoW}} = 0$.
- *Plausibility confound.* A text-only scorer $h$ (an LM log-likelihood, or a trained implausibility classifier) applied to the same triples: $\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{blind}} = \mathbb{E}\,\mathbb{1}[h(c^+) > h(c^-)]$. Any benchmark with high $\mathrm{Acc}_{\text{blind}}$ measures fluency, not grounding.
- *Retention.* ImageNet-1k zero-shot top-1 and COCO 5k text→image R@1, reported alongside every compositional number.

**Assumptions, and which fail.**

1. *The negative differs from the positive only in compositional structure.* Violated in ARO and CREPE: the swap often makes the caption non-fluent or absurd, so a blind model wins (Hsieh et al., 2023).
2. *Each Winoground pair isolates compositionality.* Violated: Diwan et al. (2022) find a large fraction of the 400 pairs additionally require non-compositional reasoning, unusual images, or commonsense.
3. *The caption describes the image.* Violated at web scale; LAION/alt-text captions are frequently keyword lists with no argument structure — which is exactly the data regime that makes order uninformative.
4. *Sphere + inner product suffices.* Assumed by the architecture, unproven; a single pooled vector must encode a variable-length binding structure in $d = 512$–$1024$.

## 3. State of the Art

**Empirical SOTA (established).**
- *SugarCrepe* (Hsieh et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B) is the current confound-controlled benchmark: hard negatives generated by an LM and filtered by adversarial refinement against blind detectors. It is the only widely used probe where the blind baseline is near chance by construction.
- *NegCLIP* (Yuksekgonul et al., ICLR 2023) — fine-tune CLIP with word-order-shuffled caption negatives plus nearest-neighbour image negatives. Large gains on ARO. On SugarCrepe the gain is real but much smaller, concentrated in the SWAP category, and clearly benefits from the train/test match between its own negative generator and ARO's.
- *DAC* (Doveh et al., NeurIPS 2023) and *SVLC* (Doveh et al., CVPR 2023) — LLM-rewritten dense captions and rule-based negatives; reported gains on ARO/VL-Checklist.

**Claimed but unablated.** Nearly every "compositional CLIP" result reports gains on a benchmark whose negatives were generated by a procedure resembling the method's own training negatives. The control that is almost never run: hold the negative *generator* out, i.e. train on swap-type negatives and evaluate only on replace/add types produced by a different pipeline, with retrieval and zero-shot held fixed. Reported numbers on ARO in particular should be treated as benchmark artifacts until re-measured on SugarCrepe or SugarCrepe++.

**Theory SOTA.** Nakada et al. (AISTATS 2023) show that multimodal contrastive learning with linear encoders has a closed-form solution related to SVD/CCA of the cross-modal covariance — an explicitly order-blind object. Trager et al. (ICCV 2023) show CLIP's text embeddings support approximate *linear* composition of attribute directions, which is a partial positive result and also a diagnosis: linear composition is unordered addition, so it cannot distinguish "red cube, blue sphere" from "blue cube, red sphere". No theorem states the capacity of a $d$-dimensional dual encoder for $k$-way binding.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Winoground is near-unsolved.** In the original evaluation (Thrush et al., CVPR 2022), all ~15 tested models scored below chance on group score; the best was under $\sim 10\%$ against $16.7\%$ chance, at ViT-L/14 scale. Later large models improved but the group score remains far below human ($\sim 85\%$).
- **Retrieval does not require order.** Yuksekgonul et al. (ICLR 2023) show COCO/Flickr30k retrieval is nearly unchanged when caption word order is destroyed — so the training and evaluation objective both tolerate a bag of words. This is the single most load-bearing empirical fact in the area.
- **Two popular benchmarks are hackable.** SugarCrepe reports that blind, text-only models (an LM likelihood and a trained plausibility model) outperform strong CLIP models on ARO and CREPE negatives. Any pre-2023 compositionality claim measured only on those sets is uninterpretable.
- **Winoground is partly mis-specified.** Diwan et al. (EMNLP 2022) categorize the 400 pairs and find only a minority are unambiguously "swap the arguments" cases; the rest add visual difficulty or commonsense.
- **Binding fails in humans-like ways.** Campbell et al. (NeurIPS 2024) show VLM errors on multi-object scenes track capacity-limited binding, degrading with the number of objects — a set-size effect, not a random error pattern.
- **Scale does not fix it.** Across model sizes and pretraining data scales, SugarCrepe SWAP accuracy improves much more slowly than ImageNet zero-shot; the gap between REPLACE (easy) and SWAP (hard) persists at every scale reported.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** Is there a $d$ and an encoder pair such that a single inner product separates all $k$-object binding permutations of a fixed vocabulary, and what is the minimal $d(k)$? No lower bound and no construction exists. Also open: whether the InfoNCE population minimizer on realistic caption distributions is provably order-invariant, or merely order-insensitive because the mutual information $I(\text{order}; x \mid \text{bag})$ is small.
- **Empirically open.** Does training on data where order *is* informative — synthetic or rendered scenes with controlled attribute–object assignments, at $\ge 100$M pairs — produce a dual encoder with a large $\Delta_{\mathrm{BoW}}$ that transfers to natural images? Runnable today; not run at that scale.
- **Methodologically blocked.** There is no accepted measurement that separates binding failure from recognition failure. If a model cannot tell a red cube from a blue cube in the image at all, its binding score is meaningless — yet no standard probe conditions on per-attribute recognition being correct.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **confounded measurement compounded by train/test leakage of the negative generator**. A compositional probe must produce a negative caption that is (i) grammatical, (ii) equally plausible a priori, (iii) false of the image, and (iv) different from the positive only in argument structure. Automating (i)–(iii) requires an LM, and the same LM class is what everyone uses to make training negatives — so gains are indistinguishable from fitting the generator. SugarCrepe's adversarial filtering fixes (ii) for the *current* blind detectors, not for future ones.

Second obstruction: **absent ground truth on the image side**. Attribute–object binding in a natural photo has no annotation; there is no dataset with per-object attribute labels at contrastive-pretraining scale, so the conditional measurement in §5 cannot be computed on real data without new annotation.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Better-controlled benchmarks.** SugarCrepe++ (2024) adds lexically varied positives to separate lexical from semantic sensitivity. Continued adversarial filtering against stronger blind detectors *(frontier — verify whether current filters survive 2026-era LMs)*.
- **Synthetic controlled pretraining.** Rendered-scene corpora with programmatic attribute–object ground truth used as a pretraining mixture component; groups working on procedurally generated VL data *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Architectural escape from the single dot product.** Late-interaction / token-level scoring (ColBERT-style multi-vector similarity applied to VL), and generative rescoring with a captioner or VLM as a second stage. Both trade retrieval-index efficiency for binding.
- **Theory of multimodal contrastive learning.** Extensions of the linear/SVD analyses toward nonlinear encoders and identifiability of latent factors from paired data.
- **Binding as a cognitive-science question.** Princeton-affiliated work (Campbell, Griffiths, Cohen and colleagues) framing VLM binding errors against human capacity limits.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Is the failure caused by the *data* (order is uninformative in web captions) or by the *objective/architecture* (a single dot product cannot bind)?

**Design.** Pretrain CLIP ViT-B/16 from scratch, $d = 512$, on 100M image–text pairs, three arms, identical compute (≈ 32 A100-days each), identical hyperparameters:

1. **Control:** 100M web pairs (LAION-style).
2. **Data arm:** 90M web pairs + 10M rendered multi-object scenes with programmatic captions in which *only* argument order and attribute assignment disambiguate the image (e.g. "a red cube left of a blue sphere" vs. the swapped caption, both rendered as real images in the corpus).
3. **Architecture arm:** control data, but a late-interaction scorer $s(x,c) = \frac{1}{|c|}\sum_{t \in c}\max_{p \in x}\langle f_\theta(x)_p, g_\phi(c)_t\rangle$ over patch and token embeddings.

**Held-out evaluation.** SugarCrepe SWAP-OBJ and SWAP-ATT (negatives from a generator used in none of the arms), plus $\Delta_{\mathrm{BoW}}$ on COCO, plus ImageNet zero-shot top-1 and COCO 5k R@1 as retention guards.

**The deciding number.** SugarCrepe SWAP-ATT accuracy, with ImageNet zero-shot within 1.0 point of the control. If arm 2 exceeds the control by $\ge 10$ points and arm 3 does not, the constraint is data. If arm 3 exceeds and arm 2 does not, the constraint is the single dot product. If neither clears $+10$ points, the failure is in the objective itself and negative-mining fine-tuning is cosmetic.

## 10. Worked Example

Take one Winoground-style item: image $A$ shows a mug on a book; image $B$ shows a book on a mug. Captions $c_A = $ "a mug on a book", $c_B = $ "a book on a mug". Both captions are the identical multiset of tokens $\{a, a, mug, on, book\}$.

Suppose the text encoder were exactly permutation-invariant. Then $g(c_A) = g(c_B)$ and all four similarities collapse to two values, $\langle f(A), v\rangle$ and $\langle f(B), v\rangle$ for a single $v$. The text score (given image $A$, prefer $c_A$) is then a coin flip; the group score is $0$ regardless of how good the image encoder is. This is the ceiling argument: **image quality cannot rescue a bag-of-words text tower on this item.**

Real CLIP is not exactly invariant, but nearly. A typical measurement on ViT-B/32 with COCO captions: cosine similarities of matched pairs cluster around $0.30$ with within-batch standard deviation $\approx 0.03$; shuffling caption word order moves the mean by roughly $0.005$–$0.01$, i.e. $\Delta_{\mathrm{BoW}} \approx 0.2$–$0.3$ standard deviations. Whether the correct caption wins is then decided by a margin about a quarter the size of the ordinary noise between distractors, which is exactly the observed near-chance behaviour.

Now the obstruction. Try to fix this by fine-tuning on shuffled-caption negatives. Accuracy on ARO's relation split jumps by tens of points. But SugarCrepe reports that a *blind* text-only plausibility model already beats CLIP on those same ARO items — so the jump is consistent with the model having learned "shuffled text is unlikely text", a property of the caption alone. The measurement cannot distinguish "learned to bind" from "learned to detect the negative generator." Until an evaluation is built whose negatives are provably out-of-family for the training negatives and whose per-attribute recognition is separately verified on the image, the headline numbers in this area do not measure the thing they name.
