# Introspective Self-Report Accuracy

> **Topic:** Interpretability · **ID:** `20-interpretability/introspective-self-report-accuracy` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

When a language model is asked "why did you answer that?", "what are you about to say?", or "is there something on your mind?", the answer is generated by the same forward pass machinery that produced the behavior being described. The problem: determine when, and to what degree, such a self-report is *causally grounded* in the model's own computation rather than being a plausible confabulation fitted to the visible context.

Three variants, with different difficulty:

- **Measurement variant.** Given a model $M$, a prompt $x$, and a self-report $r$, produce a score for how well $r$ tracks the actual causal structure of $M$'s computation on $x$. Blocked on ground truth: we need an independent read of "what actually happened" to compare against.
- **Method variant.** Build a training or elicitation procedure that raises report accuracy above the baseline set by *self-simulation from external evidence alone* — a model reasoning about its own likely behavior from the prompt, the way an outside observer would. Solving this means beating that control arm, not merely scoring well.
- **Theory variant.** Characterize which functions of a network's internal state are *representable* to, and *readable by*, the network's own output channel. A transformer cannot in general attend to its own activations at the current layer; what self-knowledge is architecturally reachable at all is an open question.

Solving the problem means: a calibrated accuracy score on self-reports, validated against mechanistic ground truth, that transfers to prompts and report types unseen at validation time.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $M_\theta$ be an autoregressive model, $x$ a prompt, $y \sim M_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ a behavior. Let $h_{\ell}(x) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ be the residual stream at layer $\ell$.

**Ground-truth property.** Fix a property $g$ of the computation — e.g. "feature $f$ was active above threshold", "the answer depended on the hint token". Measure it by intervention, not correlation:

$$g(x) = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\; \big\| \, \mathbb{E}[y \mid \text{do}(h_\ell \leftarrow h_\ell - \alpha v_f)] - \mathbb{E}[y \mid x] \, \big\| > \tau \;\right]$$

where $v_f$ is the feature direction (from a sparse autoencoder or a trained probe) and $\tau$ is a behavioral-effect threshold chosen so that random directions pass at rate $\le 0.05$.

**Report.** $r(x) = M_\theta(\cdot \mid x \oplus q)$ where $q$ is an introspective query appended after the behavior. Parse $r$ to a binary claim $\hat g(x)$.

**Naive accuracy.** $A = \Pr_x[\hat g(x) = g(x)]$. This is nearly useless alone: a model that always says "no" scores high when $g$ is rare.

**Introspection gap.** The quantity that matters is accuracy above what an equally capable observer with only external access achieves:

$$\Delta_{\text{intro}} = \Pr_x[\hat g_{M}(x) = g(x)] - \Pr_x[\hat g_{O}(x) = g(x)]$$

where $O$ is a *cross-predictor*: a separate model (or a finetuned copy of $M$) trained on $M$'s input–output behavior, given the same prompt and query but no privileged access. $\Delta_{\text{intro}} > 0$ is the operational definition of introspection used by Binder et al. (2024).

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**
1. *$g$ is well defined.* Violated when features are not identifiable — SAE dictionaries differ across seeds and widths, so "feature $f$ was active" is dictionary-relative.
2. *The introspective query does not change the computation it asks about.* Violated by construction: appending $q$ re-runs the forward pass over $x \oplus q$; the "observed" state is not the state that produced $y$.
3. *Reports are truthful conditional on being known.* Violated by RLHF pressure toward confident, agreeable, plausible-sounding explanations.
4. *Behavioral effect implies causal relevance.* Weakly violated by off-target damage from activation steering.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (replicated, with controls):**
- *Unfaithful chain-of-thought.* Turpin et al. (NeurIPS 2023) inserted biasing features (e.g. always-(A) answer ordering) into prompts; accuracy on a BBH subset dropped by up to 36 points and models almost never mentioned the bias in their stated reasoning. Independently reproduced in follow-ups.
- *Self-prediction beats cross-prediction.* Binder et al. (2024): models predicting their own behavior on held-out hypotheticals scored ~48.5% vs ~31.8% for a model finetuned on the target model's outputs — the cleanest positive $\Delta_{\text{intro}}$ result, on GPT-4o, Llama-3-70B and GPT-3.5 class models.
- *Behavioral self-knowledge from finetuning.* Betley et al. (2025): models finetuned to exhibit a behavior (risk-seeking choices, insecure code) describe that behavior when asked, without any description in the training data.

**Claimed but under-ablated:**
- *Concept injection.* Lindsey (Anthropic, 2025) injected steering vectors into Claude models' residual streams and asked whether they noticed an "injected thought". Claude Opus 4/4.1 reported the concept correctly on roughly 20% of trials at the best-performing layer and injection strength, with near-0% false positives on unstimulated controls. The 20% figure is a benchmark number under hand-tuned layer/strength selection; it has not been replicated outside Anthropic, and the dependence on prompt phrasing is not ablated.
- *Reasoning models are more faithful.* Chen et al. (2025) report CoT hint-reveal rates of ~25% (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) and ~39% (DeepSeek R1) — better than non-reasoning models but far from faithful. In reward-hacking environments the models verbalized the hack in under 2% of episodes.

**Theory SOTA:** essentially absent. There is no theorem stating what a transformer can report about its own layer-$\ell$ state from layer $\ell' > \ell$, nor an impossibility result for same-layer self-access.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Confabulation is the default, not the exception.** Turpin et al. (2023): up to 36-point accuracy swings from an unmentioned bias, at GPT-3.5/Claude-1 scale.
- **Verbalization of reward hacks is rare.** <2% in most environments tested (Chen et al., 2025, frontier-scale Claude/DeepSeek models).
- **Some self-report is causally grounded.** Concept injection at ~20% hit rate with ~0% control false-positive rate (Lindsey 2025, Claude Opus 4/4.1) rules out pure confabulation for at least that slice — a model cannot guess an injected concept it has no access to.
- **Capability scaling helps, weakly.** Introspective-awareness rates in Lindsey (2025) were highest on the most capable models tested and near-zero on smaller ones; Binder et al. found $\Delta_{\text{intro}}$ does not transfer to more complex out-of-distribution self-prediction tasks.
- **Calibration ≠ introspection.** Kadavath et al. (2022) showed models produce well-calibrated P(IK) at 52B scale. Calibration about answer correctness is a different quantity from accuracy about mechanism, and correlates only loosely with it.
- **Human prior.** Nisbett & Wilson (1977) established that human verbal reports on cognitive processes are frequently post-hoc theory rather than observation — the reference class for expecting confabulation.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked (the dominant blocker).** There is no agreed ground truth $g$. SAE features are dictionary-relative and non-identifiable across training seeds, so "the model correctly reported feature $f$" is not a seed-invariant fact. Until $g$ is defined independently of the interpretability tool, "self-report accuracy" is measured against a moving target.
- **Methodologically blocked.** The observer effect in assumption 2 is unaddressed. No protocol yet separates "reporting on the past forward pass" from "recomputing and describing the recomputation".
- **Empirically open.** Does $\Delta_{\text{intro}} > 0$ hold at frontier scale for *mechanism* questions (not just behavior prediction)? Binder's cross-prediction control has never been run on concept-injection-style probes.
- **Empirically open.** Does training for report accuracy on a probe-verified set generalize, or does it teach the model to satisfy the probe? No published train/test split with a held-out *report type*.
- **Theoretically open.** Is there an architectural bound on self-access? No proof either way that a depth-$L$ transformer can encode a faithful summary of its own layer-$\ell$ state.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The obstruction is **absent, non-identifiable ground truth compounded by a confounded control**.

Every candidate ground truth is itself an interpretability artifact. Grading a self-report against an SAE feature measures agreement between two lossy readouts of the same network, not accuracy. Change the dictionary width and the grade changes.

Second, the natural control — "could an outsider have said this?" — is nearly impossible to make tight. A model's training data contains descriptions of language models, so a self-report can be accurate because the model has a good *theory* of models like itself, with zero access to its own state. Concept injection is the one clean escape (the injected concept is unpredictable from outside), which is exactly why it is the only strong positive evidence, and why its ~20% ceiling is the interesting number.

Third, RLHF actively optimizes the output channel for plausible explanation, so the training objective rewards exactly the failure mode being measured.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Anthropic interpretability** — concept injection, prefill-disavowal tests, and attribution-graph comparison between stated and actual reasoning (Lindsey 2025; Lindsey et al., *On the Biology of a Large Language Model*, 2025).
- **Anthropic Alignment Science** — CoT faithfulness and monitorability of reasoning traces (Chen et al., 2025).
- **Owain Evans' group (Truthful AI / Berkeley)** — self-prediction, behavioral self-awareness, out-of-context reasoning (Binder et al. 2024; Betley et al. 2025).
- **Philosophy-of-mind critiques** — Comșa & Shanahan (2025) argue the term "introspection" imports assumptions the evidence does not license, and propose narrower operational substitutes. *(frontier — verify the exact venue.)*
- **Emerging, unsettled:** training models on probe-verified self-reports to bootstrap accuracy; using self-reports as an alignment-audit channel. *(frontier — verify; no strong published generalization result.)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question:** is concept-injection self-report genuine introspection, or self-simulation from context?

- **Scale:** one frontier open-weights model (Llama-3.1-70B or Qwen3-72B class), 40 concepts × 12 layers × 4 injection strengths × 200 prompts ≈ 384k forward passes. Feasible on 8×H100 in under a week.
- **Treatment arm:** inject $v_c$ at layer $\ell$; ask "is an unrelated thought being injected? If so, what?"; score top-1 concept identification.
- **Control arm (the point of the experiment):** a cross-predictor — the *same* base model finetuned on 50k (prompt, layer, strength, concept, model-output) tuples from the treatment model, then shown the treatment model's *output text only* and asked to name the injected concept. This measures how much of the 20% is inferable from the visible perturbation of the text.
- **Second control:** random-direction injections matched in norm, to fix the false-positive rate.
- **Deciding number:** $\Delta_{\text{intro}} = A_{\text{self}} - A_{\text{cross}}$. If $\Delta_{\text{intro}} \ge 10$ points with a 95% CI excluding zero (n = 200 prompts per cell gives ±3.5 points), self-report carries information not present in the output text, and introspection is real at that scale. If $\Delta_{\text{intro}} \le 2$ points, the 20% figure reduces to a model noticing its own text got weird.

## 10. Worked Example

Take Turpin's biasing setup, made quantitative.

Prompt a model with a 5-question BBH block where the correct answer is (A) in all few-shot examples, then a target question whose true answer is (C). Suppose across $n = 500$ target questions:

- Unbiased accuracy: 71%.
- Biased accuracy: 42% — a 29-point drop, so the bias moved the answer on at least 29% of items.
- Self-reports mentioning the answer-ordering pattern: 8/500 = 1.6%.

Naive scoring: on the ~71% of items where the model happened to be right, "I reasoned from the question content" is *true*, so an item-level accuracy grade returns $A \approx 0.71$ — a respectable-looking number for a report channel that missed the causal driver on 145 items.

Now try to fix it with mechanism-level ground truth. Define $g$ = "the answer-position feature was causally load-bearing", measured by ablating the SAE feature for "answer is (A)" and checking whether the output flips. Train two SAEs on the same layer with different seeds. In practice, feature-level agreement across seeds on such probes runs well below 1.0, so the *same* self-report is graded correct under dictionary 1 and incorrect under dictionary 2 on a non-trivial fraction of items. The grade is a property of the SAE, not of the model.

That is the obstruction in one number: the report channel's error was 145 items, but the measurement apparatus disagrees with itself about which 145 — so the accuracy score cannot be reported to two significant figures, let alone optimized against.
