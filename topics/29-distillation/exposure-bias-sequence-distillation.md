# Exposure Bias in Sequence-Level Distillation

> **Topic:** Distillation & Transfer · **ID:** `29-distillation/exposure-bias-sequence-distillation` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement

Sequence-level knowledge distillation (seq-KD, Kim & Rush 2016) trains a student on sequences *generated by a teacher* rather than on human references. The student is still fit by teacher forcing: at training time each prefix is a teacher-produced prefix, at inference time each prefix is the student's own. The claim under examination is that this train/inference prefix mismatch — **exposure bias** — is a principal cause of the residual student–teacher gap, and that on-policy distillation (training on student-sampled prefixes labeled by the teacher) closes it.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Measurement.** Given a student $\pi_\theta$ and a teacher $p_T$, produce an estimator of the loss attributable *specifically* to prefix-distribution mismatch, separable from the loss attributable to the teacher's targets being easier, shorter, or lower-entropy than references. No agreed estimator exists.
- **Method.** Build a distillation procedure whose student, at a fixed inference budget, beats offline seq-KD. Partially solved: on-policy methods (ImitKD, GKD, MiniLLM) beat offline seq-KD on published benchmarks.
- **Theory.** Prove that the offline-distilled student's expected sequence loss grows super-linearly in length $T$ while an on-policy student's grows linearly, *for the distributions autoregressive language models actually induce*. Open. The imitation-learning bounds that give $O(T^2)$ vs $O(T)$ hold, but are worst-case and empirically slack.

A solution is: an estimator that assigns a number to the exposure-bias component, plus an ablation showing that method gains track that number rather than tracking target complexity.

## 2. Formal Setting

Source $x$, target $y = (y_1,\dots,y_T)$ over vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. Teacher $p_T(y\mid x)$, student $\pi_\theta(y\mid x) = \prod_t \pi_\theta(y_t \mid y_{<t}, x)$.

**Offline seq-KD.** Build $\hat{\mathcal{D}} = \{(x, \hat{y})\}$ with $\hat{y} = \arg\max_y p_T(y\mid x)$ (measured: beam search, beam 5, length penalty $\alpha$; the true mode is not computed). Minimize
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{seq-KD}}(\theta) = -\tfrac{1}{|\hat{\mathcal D}|}\sum \log \pi_\theta(\hat y \mid x).$$

**On-policy KD.** With $\mu_\theta$ a sampling policy derived from $\pi_\theta$ (temperature $\tau$, or a mixture $\lambda \pi_\theta + (1-\lambda) p_T$),
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{on}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{x}\,\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\mu_\theta}\sum_{t=1}^{T} D\!\left(p_T(\cdot\mid y_{<t},x)\;\|\;\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid y_{<t},x)\right),$$
$D$ forward KL, reverse KL, or JSD$_\beta$. Measured as a Monte-Carlo average over $k$ samples per prompt ($k=4$ typical), with gradients stopped through the sampling.

**Prefix-distribution mismatch.** Let $d_t^{\pi}$ be the marginal over prefixes of length $t$ under $\pi$. The exposure-bias gap is
$$\Delta_{\text{EB}}(\theta) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{y_{<t}\sim d_t^{\pi_\theta}}\!\left[D(p_T\|\pi_\theta)\right]}_{\text{student's own prefixes}} - \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{y_{<t}\sim d_t^{p_T}}\!\left[D(p_T\|\pi_\theta)\right]}_{\text{teacher-forced prefixes}}.$$
Both terms are directly computable: sample prefixes from each source, run both models, take the KL. $\Delta_{\text{EB}}$ is the quantity that should be reported and almost never is.

**Imitation bound.** With per-step error $\epsilon = \max_t \mathbb{E}_{d_t^{p_T}}[\mathbb{1}(\pi_\theta \neq p_T)]$, behavior cloning gives $J(\pi_\theta) \le J(p_T) + T^2\epsilon$; DAgger-style on-policy training gives $O(T\epsilon)$ (Ross et al. 2011).

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**
1. *The teacher's beam output approximates its mode.* Violated: Stahlberg & Byrne (EMNLP 2019) find by exact search that the empty string is the global mode for 51.8% of WMT15 En→De inputs; beam search is a beneficial search *error*.
2. *Targets are drawn from $p_T$.* Violated: $\arg\max$ targets are a degenerate point mass, far lower-entropy than $p_T$ samples.
3. *The teacher is realizable by the student.* Violated by construction — capacity gap is the point of distillation.
4. *Errors do not self-correct.* Violated: He et al. (EMNLP 2021) show autoregressive LMs recover from injected prefix distortions, so the quadratic bound's error-absorbing-state assumption fails.

## 3. State of the Art

**Empirical SOTA (established, ablated).**
- Offline seq-KD (Kim & Rush, EMNLP 2016) remains the default for non-autoregressive MT and small-model MT. Reproduced by many groups over a decade.
- GKD (Agarwal et al., ICLR 2024) — on-policy student samples, teacher-labeled, with a JSD$_\beta$ divergence — beats offline seq-KD across summarization, translation and arithmetic, with an ablation isolating *student-generated data* from *choice of divergence*. This ablation is the strongest evidence that prefix distribution matters independently of the loss.
- MiniLLM (Gu et al., ICLR 2024): reverse KL with policy-gradient on student samples; reduces exposure to low-density teacher regions and cuts hallucination on instruction-following.
- ImitKD (Lin et al., EMNLP 2020): DAgger applied to seq-KD; the first clean statement that seq-KD is behavior cloning.

**Claimed but unablated.**
- That on-policy gains *are* exposure-bias reduction. In every paper above, on-policy training simultaneously (a) matches prefix distributions and (b) replaces $\arg\max$ targets with full teacher distributions on those prefixes. No published ablation separates these.
- That $\arg\max$ seq-KD helps NAT *because* it removes multimodality rather than because it shortens and regularizes targets. Zhou et al. (ICLR 2020) measure a complexity proxy but the causal ablation is a benchmark number, not a controlled swap.

**Theory SOTA.** Arora et al. (Findings of ACL 2022) give the tightest error-accumulation analysis for language generation, bounding accumulated divergence by per-step error times an accumulation factor; the bound is non-vacuous only for large per-step error.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Seq-KD works, at MT scale.** Kim & Rush (WMT En→De, 4×1000 LSTM teacher → 2×500 student, ~10× fewer parameters) report the distilled student closing nearly all of the gap to the teacher, with greedy decoding beating the baseline student's beam search.
- **NAT depends on it almost totally.** WMT14 En→De: non-autoregressive students trained on raw bitext score in the low teens BLEU; trained on seq-KD data from the same autoregressive teacher, ~19–20 BLEU. An 6–9 BLEU swing from changing *only the targets*, not the prefix distribution (Gu et al., ICLR 2018; Zhou et al., ICLR 2020). This is the single strongest evidence that the dominant seq-KD effect is target simplification, not exposure.
- **Distilled data is measurably simpler.** Zhou et al. define conditional word-translation entropy $C(d)$ and show distilled corpora have lower $C(d)$; smaller NAT students prefer weaker (lower-$C$) teachers.
- **Errors self-recover.** He et al. (EMNLP 2021), on open-ended generation with GPT-2-scale models, find that a corrupted prefix does not cause monotonically increasing distortion — the model returns toward its own manifold.
- **Scheduled sampling is not a fix.** Huszár (2015) shows the scheduled-sampling objective is not consistent: its optimum need not be the data distribution.
- **On-policy > offline at LLM scale.** GKD reports consistent gains for T5-small/base/large students from a T5-XL teacher on XSum, WMT En→De and GSM8K, over offline seq-KD with the same teacher and budget.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked.** No accepted estimator that reports the exposure-bias share of the student–teacher gap. $\Delta_{\text{EB}}$ above is computable but is not reported in the distillation literature, so cross-paper comparison is impossible. Every headline gain is confounded with target entropy, sequence length, and effective data augmentation.
- **Empirically open.** The controlled 2×2 — {teacher-forced prefixes, student prefixes} × {$\arg\max$ targets, full teacher distribution} — has not been run at fixed token budget on a modern student/teacher pair. It is runnable on a few hundred GPU-hours.
- **Empirically open.** Whether $\Delta_{\text{EB}}$ scales up or down with student size. Folk claim: exposure bias vanishes as students get stronger. No scaling sweep tests it.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether the $T^2$/$T$ separation is achievable for the smooth, self-correcting policies LLMs realize, or whether under a self-recovery / contraction condition offline cloning also achieves $O(T\epsilon)$. No proof either way.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Confounded measurement, not compute.** Every intervention that changes the prefix distribution also changes the targets. Switching from offline seq-KD to on-policy KD simultaneously changes: (i) which prefixes are visited, (ii) the target from a one-hot $\arg\max$ token to a full teacher distribution, (iii) the effective number of distinct sequences seen (student sampling is stochastic and unbounded; the offline set is fixed), and (iv) the length distribution. The gain is real and reproducible; its attribution is non-identified. Compounding this: the ground-truth counterfactual — "what would the student's loss be under its own prefixes if it had been trained on them" — requires the trained student to define the prefix distribution, so the measurement is circular unless run iteratively. And the standard evaluations (BLEU, ROUGE, task accuracy) do not measure prefix-distribution mismatch at all; they are the outcome, not the mechanism.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **On-policy distillation as default for small LLMs.** GKD-family recipes (Google DeepMind) and reverse-KL variants (Tsinghua/CoAI, MiniLLM line) are the working standard for sub-10B students.
- **Speculative / partially-on-policy distillation** — using the student to draft and the teacher to correct, to cut the teacher-inference cost of on-policy KD. *(frontier — verify: several 2024–2025 preprints, ablations against a matched-budget offline arm are thin.)*
- **MBR-distillation.** Distilling from minimum-Bayes-risk decoded teacher outputs (following Eikema & Aziz, COLING 2020; Freitag et al., TACL 2022) rather than beam outputs, which directly attacks assumption 1 of §2 rather than exposure. *(frontier — verify)*
- **RL-from-teacher-reward** blurring the KD/RLHF boundary; exposure bias here is subsumed into on-policy training and stops being separately measured.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**The 2×2 attribution run.**

- **Scale.** Teacher: an 8B instruction-tuned model. Student: 1B, same tokenizer/family. Data: 200k prompts spanning WMT En→De, XSum, GSM8K. Budget: identical student-optimizer token count (2B tokens) across all arms; teacher forward passes capped equally.
- **Arms.**
  1. *Offline / hard*: beam-5 teacher $\arg\max$ targets, teacher-forced prefixes. **This is the control arm** (classic seq-KD).
  2. *Offline / soft*: same teacher-generated sequences, but token targets are the full teacher distribution.
  3. *On-policy / hard*: student-sampled prefixes, teacher's $\arg\max$ token as target.
  4. *On-policy / soft*: student-sampled prefixes, full teacher distribution (GKD).
- **Instrument.** Report $\Delta_{\text{EB}}$ (§2) for every arm, plus target entropy $H(p_T)$ on visited prefixes and mean target length, so the confounds are on the page.
- **Deciding number.** $R = \dfrac{\text{arm 3} - \text{arm 1}}{\text{arm 4} - \text{arm 1}}$ on the held-out task metric. $R > 0.5$ ⇒ the prefix distribution carries most of the gain and exposure bias is the mechanism. $R < 0.2$ ⇒ the gain is target-side (soft labels), and "exposure bias" is a misattribution. Pre-register a $\pm 0.1$ band on $R$ from 3 seeds.

## 10. Worked Example

Take a WMT En→De student with measured per-step top-1 disagreement against its teacher of $\epsilon = 0.01$ on teacher-forced prefixes, and mean target length $T = 30$.

- Behavior-cloning bound: $T^2\epsilon = 900 \times 0.01 = 9$ — expressed as expected sequence-level cost, this exceeds the maximum possible cost of 1. **The bound is vacuous at the length and error rate that actually occur.**
- On-policy bound: $T\epsilon = 0.3$. Also not tight, but at least in range.
- Direct measurement instead: sample 2,000 student prefixes at $t=15$ and 2,000 teacher prefixes at $t=15$, score both under the teacher. A typical result is $\mathbb{E}_{d^{\pi_\theta}}[\mathrm{KL}] = 0.42$ nats vs $\mathbb{E}_{d^{p_T}}[\mathrm{KL}] = 0.35$ nats, so $\Delta_{\text{EB}} \approx 0.07$ nats/token — roughly 17% excess divergence on the student's own prefixes.

Now the obstruction. Switch that student to GKD and suppose it gains $+1.1$ BLEU. Attributing that to the 0.07-nat prefix mismatch requires ruling out the target-side change — and the NAT evidence says target-side effects are large: on the same language pair, changing *only* targets from human references to teacher $\arg\max$ moves a non-autoregressive student by 6–9 BLEU with the prefix distribution held fixed at teacher forcing. A 6–9 BLEU target-side lever and a $+1.1$ BLEU observed gain are not separable by inspection. Until arms 2 and 3 of §8 are run at matched token budget, "exposure bias" names a quantity that is measurable ($\Delta_{\text{EB}} = 0.07$) but has never been shown to predict the gain it is credited with.
