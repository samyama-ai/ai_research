# Sample Complexity of Preference Learning With Bradley-Terry Misspecification

> **Topic:** Reinforcement Learning for LLMs · **ID:** `18-rl-for-llms/bradley-terry-misspecification-sample-complexity` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement

RLHF pipelines fit a scalar reward $r_\theta$ to pairwise human preferences under the Bradley–Terry (BT) link, then optimize a policy against it. BT assumes preferences are generated by a single latent utility and are therefore **transitive** and **stochastically consistent**. Human preference data is neither: it aggregates heterogeneous annotators, is context-dependent, and admits intransitive triads.

The problem: **how does the sample complexity of preference learning degrade as a function of the amount of BT misspecification, and at what dataset size does misspecification bias dominate statistical error?**

Three variants that are routinely conflated:

- **Theory.** Given a misspecification budget $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ (distance from the true preference kernel to the BT family), prove upper and lower bounds on the suboptimality of the policy learned from $n$ comparisons. Is the dependence $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$, $\sqrt{d}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$, or unbounded?
- **Method.** Do general-preference methods (Nash-learning, SPO, IPO) actually beat BT reward modeling at fixed $n$, and by how much?
- **Measurement.** Estimate $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ on a real preference dataset. This is the blocked variant: standard datasets have **one label per pair**, so the true preference probability is never observed.

Solving it means: a bound of the form $\mathrm{SubOpt}(n,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}})$ with a matching lower bound, plus a measured $\hat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ on a public dataset that makes the bound numerically predictive.

## 2. Formal Setting

Prompts $x \sim \rho$, responses $y \in \mathcal{Y}$. The **ground-truth preference kernel** is
$$P^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) \in [0,1],$$
measured as the limiting fraction of the annotator population preferring $y_1$. Data: $\mathcal{D} = \{(x_i, y_i^+, y_i^-)\}_{i=1}^n$, one Bernoulli draw per triple.

BT family with features $\phi(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^d$, $\|\phi\|\le L$:
$$P_\theta(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma\big(\theta^\top(\phi(x,y_1)-\phi(x,y_2))\big),\quad \sigma(z)=(1+e^{-z})^{-1}.$$

**Misspecification** measured in total variation over the sampling distribution $\mu$ of pairs:
$$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}} = \inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\ \mathbb{E}_{(x,y_1,y_2)\sim\mu}\big|P^*(y_1\succ y_2\mid x) - P_\theta(y_1\succ y_2\mid x)\big|.$$

**Intransitivity rate**, measured on sampled triples $(y_1,y_2,y_3)$:
$$\iota = \Pr\big[\,P^*(y_1\!\succ\!y_2)>\tfrac12 \wedge P^*(y_2\!\succ\!y_3)>\tfrac12 \wedge P^*(y_3\!\succ\!y_1)>\tfrac12\,\big].$$
$\iota>0$ implies $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}>0$; the converse does not hold (BT can be violated with zero intransitivity, e.g. wrong link function or annotator mixtures).

Objective. BT pipelines maximize $J_\beta(\pi) = \mathbb{E}[r_\theta] - \beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})$. Under misspecification the correct target is a two-player game value: the **von Neumann winner** $\pi^\star = \arg\max_\pi \min_{\pi'} \mathbb{E}[P^*(y\succ y')]$. Suboptimality is measured as duality gap
$$\mathrm{DG}(\hat\pi)=\max_{\pi'}\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\hat\pi, y'\sim\pi'}\big[P^*(y'\succ y)\big]-\tfrac12 .$$

**Assumptions known to be violated in practice:** (i) single latent utility — violated by annotator heterogeneity, the dominant term in inter-annotator disagreement; (ii) transitivity — violated in human choice since Tversky (1969); (iii) logistic link — never tested against alternatives at scale; (iv) i.i.d. labels — violated, annotators label batches and drift; (v) realizability of $\phi$ — the reward head is a finetuned LLM, so $d$ is effectively unbounded and $\Theta$ is not convex.

## 3. State of the Art

**Theory SOTA (established).** Zhu, Jordan & Jiao (ICML 2023) give MLE guarantees for linear BT under both pairwise and $K$-wise comparisons: under a bounded-parameter assumption the estimate satisfies $\|\hat\theta-\theta^*\|_{\Sigma}=\tilde O(\sqrt{d/n})$, and pessimistic policy optimization yields suboptimality $\tilde O(C_\mu\sqrt{d/n})$ with $C_\mu$ a single-policy concentrability coefficient; a matching $\Omega(\sqrt{d/n})$ lower bound is proved. **All of this is under correct specification.** Zhan et al. (ICLR 2024) extend to general function classes and trajectory-level offline PbRL. Wang, Liu & Jin (NeurIPS 2023) reduce preference-based RL to standard RL with no more than polynomial overhead, again assuming a link function.

**Theory SOTA for misspecification (partial).** Azar et al. (AISTATS 2024, $\Psi$PO/IPO) show the BT+KL objective degenerates when preferences are near-deterministic: the KL regularizer's effective weight vanishes as $P^*\to\{0,1\}$, so the learned policy over-optimizes regardless of $n$. This is the cleanest established statement that misspecification bias does not shrink with data. Munos et al. (ICML 2024, Nash-MD) and Swamy et al. (ICML 2024, SPO) give last-iterate/no-regret convergence to the von Neumann winner **without** any transitivity assumption; Ye et al. (NeurIPS 2024) give online sample-complexity bounds for general preference models with an eluder-type complexity measure. What is missing across all of these: an *interpolating* bound in $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$.

**Empirical SOTA (claimed but largely unablated).** SPO and Direct Nash Optimization (Rosset et al., 2024) report win-rate gains over DPO/BT baselines, but neither ablates *along the misspecification axis* — the datasets' $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ is unknown, so a gain cannot be attributed to handling intransitivity rather than to a better optimizer or more online sampling. RewardBench (Lambert et al., 2024) top reward models score above 90% overall but drop to roughly 60–80% on the "Chat Hard" subset; that is a **benchmark number only** — it does not decompose into statistical error versus link misspecification.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Rate under correct specification:** $\tilde O(\sqrt{d/n})$, tight (Zhu et al., ICML 2023). Measured in a $\Sigma$-semi-norm; reward is identifiable only up to an additive constant per prompt.
- **Human labels are far from deterministic.** Ouyang et al. (2022) report inter-annotator agreement near 73% on InstructGPT comparisons; Bai et al. (2022) report comparable figures on Anthropic HH. Under BT, 73% agreement corresponds to a typical margin $|\theta^\top\Delta\phi|\approx\log(0.73/0.27)\approx 1.0$ — i.e. real data sits in the noisy regime where BT's likelihood is well-conditioned, but also where a single label carries $\approx 0.16$ bits about the pair.
- **Reward over-optimization is real and scales predictably.** Gao, Schulman & Hilton (ICML 2023) fit gold-reward-vs-proxy curves of the form $d(\alpha-\beta d)$ with $d=\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}}$, across reward models from 3M to 3B parameters. Increasing RM size and RM data shifts the curve but does **not** remove the turnover — consistent with a bias floor, though the paper's synthetic gold reward is itself a BT model, so it cannot isolate link misspecification.
- **Intransitivity exists in human choice** (Tversky, *Psychological Review*, 1969) and BT fit quality on LLM arenas is imperfect; Chatbot Arena (Chiang et al., ICML 2024) reports BT-based ratings with acknowledged violations, but the triad violation rate $\iota$ over model pairs is not the same quantity as $\iota$ over *response* pairs, which is what RLHF needs.
- **Aggregation of heterogeneous annotators breaks BT even with individually transitive annotators.** A mixture of two BT models is generally not BT — an elementary, established fact, and the most likely dominant source of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ in practice.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No bound of the form $\mathrm{DG}(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{BT}}) \le \tilde O(C_\mu\sqrt{d/n}) + c\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ with a matching lower bound. Unknown whether the misspecification term is $O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}})$, amplified by $\sqrt{d}$ or by $1/\beta$, or unbounded once $\pi$ leaves the data support. Also open: the minimax sample complexity of learning a von Neumann winner to duality gap $\epsilon$ for a general preference class — known bounds carry complexity measures that are not evaluated for any realistic class.
- **Empirically open.** The BT-versus-general-preference comparison at matched $n$, matched compute, and *measured* $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ has not been run. Also open: does $n^\star$ — the sample size where BT's error curve plateaus — exist below $10^6$ comparisons?
- **Methodologically blocked.** $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ and $\iota$ cannot be estimated from existing public datasets: one label per pair means $P^*$ is unidentified pointwise, and no public RLHF dataset ships $\ge 5$ independent labels per pair on a sample large enough to estimate $\iota$ with useful error bars.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **absent ground truth compounded by non-identifiability**. With one Bernoulli sample per pair, the empirical log-loss of a BT model and of a saturated (unconstrained) preference model differ by an amount that is *entirely* attributable to overfitting; misspecification bias and label noise enter the loss identically. You cannot separate them without repeated labels on the same pair — and repeated labels are the one thing preference-collection budgets are optimized against.

Secondary: the target itself changes under misspecification. BT-trained policies are evaluated by win rate against a reference, which is a *preference* quantity, so a BT policy can win the evaluation while being far from the von Neumann winner — an evaluation that does not measure what it names. And $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ is distribution-dependent: it is defined under $\mu$, but RLHF shifts the policy off $\mu$, so the measured misspecification at collection time under-estimates the misspecification where the optimizer ends up.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Game-theoretic RLHF:** Nash-MD (Google DeepMind), SPO (CMU), Direct Nash Optimization (Microsoft Research), and online iterative variants with general preference models (Ye, Xiong et al.). Direction: replace the reward scalar with a preference oracle and target the von Neumann winner.
- **Beyond-BT reward modeling:** vector-valued and embedding-based preference heads that represent non-transitive preferences (Sun, Shen, Ton and collaborators, 2024–2025); distributional and mixture-of-annotator reward models. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Voting-theoretic aggregation:** Condorcet-style objectives for rating systems, including Soft Condorcet Optimization (DeepMind, 2024). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Measurement work:** multi-annotator re-labeling efforts and annotator-disagreement modeling (Hosking, Blunsom & Bartolo, ICLR 2024 show human preference is confounded by assertiveness and factuality). No public dataset yet reports $\iota$ at response level. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Goal:** find $n^\star$, the sample size at which BT bias overtakes BT statistical error, and test whether it lies below the size of production preference datasets.

**Scale.** One 8B instruction-tuned policy. Collect $N=20{,}000$ prompt–response pairs with **7 independent annotations each** (140k labels; at $0.10 per label, roughly $14k, or replicate with 7 diverse LLM judges as a cheaper proxy arm). This gives $\hat P^*$ per pair with standard error $\le 0.19$, enough to estimate $\iota$ over 5,000 sampled triads and to compute $\hat\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}$ by fitting BT to $\hat P^*$.

**Arms.** Train on $n \in \{5\mathrm{k}, 20\mathrm{k}, 80\mathrm{k}, 320\mathrm{k}\}$ single-label comparisons (subsampled from the same pool, one label per pair, as in normal practice):
1. **BT arm** — standard reward model + PPO/DPO.
2. **Control arm** — identical architecture and compute, but the preference-model arm (SPO or Nash-MD) targeting the von Neumann winner.
3. **Oracle-calibration arm** — BT fit directly to the 7-vote $\hat P^*$ soft labels. This isolates label noise from link misspecification: any residual gap to arm 2 is misspecification, not variance.

**Deciding number.** Plot duality gap $\mathrm{DG}(\hat\pi)$ against $\hat P^*$-derived preferences versus $n$, log–log. **The single decisive number is the slope of the BT arm between $n=80\mathrm{k}$ and $n=320\mathrm{k}$.** If it stays near $-1/2$, BT misspecification is not binding at production scale and the game-theoretic machinery is unjustified for current datasets. If the slope is flatter than $-0.15$ while the control arm holds near $-1/2$, a bias floor exists and $n^\star \le 10^5$ — which would make BT the binding constraint on every current RLHF pipeline.

## 10. Worked Example

Two annotator populations, each internally BT, in equal proportion. Three responses; population A has utilities $(u_1,u_2,u_3)=(2,1,0)$, population B has $(0,2,1)$. Aggregate preference is the average of two logistic curves:

$$P^*(y_i\succ y_j) = \tfrac12\sigma(u^A_i-u^A_j)+\tfrac12\sigma(u^B_i-u^B_j).$$

Compute: $P^*(1\succ2)=\tfrac12\sigma(1)+\tfrac12\sigma(-2)=\tfrac12(0.731)+\tfrac12(0.119)=0.425$. $P^*(2\succ3)=\tfrac12\sigma(1)+\tfrac12\sigma(1)=0.731$. $P^*(1\succ3)=\tfrac12\sigma(2)+\tfrac12\sigma(-1)=\tfrac12(0.881)+\tfrac12(0.269)=0.575$.

So $y_2\succ y_1$, $y_2\succ y_3$, $y_1\succ y_3$ — transitive here, with $y_2$ the Condorcet winner. Now fit BT by maximum likelihood to these three probabilities. BT cannot reproduce them: with $\hat\theta$ giving scores $s_1,s_2,s_3$, matching $P(1\succ2)=0.425$ and $P(2\succ3)=0.731$ forces $s_1-s_3 = (s_1-s_2)+(s_2-s_3) = -0.302 + 1.000 = 0.698$, hence $P_{\mathrm{BT}}(1\succ3)=\sigma(0.698)=0.668$, against the truth $0.575$. Residual $0.093$. The MLE trades error across the three pairs, landing near $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mis}}\approx 0.04$ per pair.

**Where the obstruction becomes visible.** Suppose you collect $n$ single labels per pair. The statistical error on each $\hat P$ is $\approx 0.5/\sqrt{n}$, which equals the $0.04$ bias floor at $n\approx 156$ labels per pair. Beyond that, more data does not improve the BT fit — but *you cannot tell*, because the empirical log-loss of the BT fit converges to $H(P^*) + \mathrm{KL}(P^*\|P_{\hat\theta})$ and $H(P^*)\approx 0.65$ nats dwarfs the $\mathrm{KL}\approx 0.004$ nats of misspecification. A validation-accuracy curve that plateaus at 68% is indistinguishable between "BT is right, humans are noisy" and "BT is wrong by $0.04$ and humans are noisy". Only repeated labels on the *same* pair separate the two — which is exactly the measurement Section 8 buys, and exactly what no public dataset contains.
