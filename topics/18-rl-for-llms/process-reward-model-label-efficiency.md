# Process Reward Model Label Efficiency

> **Topic:** Reinforcement Learning for LLMs · **ID:** `18-rl-for-llms/process-reward-model-label-efficiency` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

A process reward model (PRM) scores intermediate reasoning steps, not just final answers. Training one has historically required step-level supervision: PRM800K used 800K human step labels; Math-Shepherd and OmegaPRM replaced humans with Monte-Carlo rollouts costing billions of generated tokens.

**The problem:** determine how process-supervision quality scales with label budget, and whether the exponent can be improved.

Three variants, with different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a labeling budget $B$ (human annotations, or rollout tokens), what is the best achievable downstream utility $U(B)$ — and is $U$ measured by best-of-$n$ accuracy, by step-error localization, or by RL return? These three disagree, which is itself an open result.
- **Method.** Construct a labeling scheme that reaches a target $U$ with $10\times$ fewer labels than MC rollout supervision. Claimed several times; never ablated against a matched-compute control.
- **Theory.** Is there a sample-complexity separation between outcome supervision and process supervision for credit assignment in a length-$T$ reasoning MDP? No lower bound is known that is specific to the LLM setting.

Solving it means: a published curve $U(B)$ over at least two decades of $B$, with the labeling *compute* held constant across arms, so that "label-efficient" is distinguishable from "spent the compute elsewhere."

## 2. Formal Setting

A problem $x \sim \mathcal{P}$, a solution $y = (s_1,\dots,s_T)$ of steps generated by policy $\pi$. Ground-truth answer checker $\mathbb{1}[\text{correct}(y,x)] \in \{0,1\}$ — measured by symbolic answer match, which is itself noisy at roughly 1–3% on MATH-style parsers.

**Step value.** The quantity most PRMs actually estimate is
$$V^\pi(x, s_{\le t}) = \mathbb{P}_{y' \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x, s_{\le t})}\left[\text{correct}(s_{\le t} \oplus y', x)\right].$$
Measured by $k$ rollouts: $\hat V_k = \frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^k \mathbb{1}[\text{correct}]$, standard error $\sqrt{V(1-V)/k} \le 0.5/\sqrt{k}$. At the common $k=8$, that is $\pm 0.18$ — larger than the gap between many adjacent steps.

**Label budget.** Two incommensurable currencies:
- $B_{\text{human}}$ = step annotations. PRM800K: $8\times10^5$.
- $B_{\text{tok}}$ = rollout tokens $\approx N \cdot T \cdot k \cdot L$ for $N$ solutions, $T$ steps, $k$ rollouts, $L$ tokens/rollout.

Any label-efficiency claim must state which it economizes.

**Utility.** Three estimators, not equivalent:
$$U_{\text{BoN}}(n) = \mathbb{E}_x\big[\text{correct}(\arg\max_{y \in Y_n} r_\theta(x,y))\big], \quad
U_{\text{loc}} = \text{F1 of first-error index}, \quad
U_{\text{RL}} = \mathbb{E}[\text{correct}] \text{ after policy optimization against } r_\theta.$$

**Label efficiency exponent.** Fit $U(B) = U_\infty - cB^{-\alpha}$; the method question is whether $\alpha$ or $c$ is what improves.

**Assumptions known violated in practice.**
- *$V^\pi$ is the right target.* It is not: a step can be locally correct yet lower the completion probability under a weak $\pi$, and vice versa. Setlur et al. (ICLR 2025) show the correct target for search is an *advantage* under a prover policy $\mu \ne \pi$, not $V^\pi$.
- *Steps are well-defined units.* Step segmentation is by newline or "\n\n" heuristics; changing the delimiter changes both labels and F1.
- *Off-policy validity.* PRMs are trained on rollouts from $\pi_0$ and applied to $\pi_t$ after RL updates. Distribution shift is unbounded and unmeasured.
- *False negatives are absent.* MC labeling marks a correct step as bad whenever $k$ rollouts all fail; rate grows with problem difficulty.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (independently reproduced).**
- Process supervision beats outcome supervision for *reranking* at large $n$. Lightman et al. (2023): PRM reaches 78.2% on a 500-problem MATH subset at best-of-1860 vs. ~72.4% for the ORM baseline, GPT-4-scale generator.
- Automatic MC labels work well enough to be useful. Math-Shepherd (Wang et al., ACL 2024) and OmegaPRM (Luo et al., 2024; ~1.5M automatic annotations, Gemini Pro MATH 51% → 69.4%) both replace human labels entirely.

**Claimed but not ablated at matched compute.**
- *Implicit PRM* (Yuan et al., ICML 2025): a PRM obtained free from outcome labels via a DPO-style parameterization, reported to beat Math-Shepherd with under $1/38$ of the training data. The comparison is on data *count*, not on labeling compute or generator quality.
- *ThinkPRM* (Khalifa et al., 2025): a generative verifier fine-tuned on ~1K process labels reported to beat discriminative PRMs trained on PRM800K. It spends test-time tokens instead of training labels — an efficiency *transfer*, and the exchange rate is not reported.
- *GenPRM* (Zhao et al., 2025): 23K synthetic samples, 1.5B model reported above GPT-4o on ProcessBench.

**Benchmark-number-only.** ProcessBench (Zheng et al., 2024) and PRMBench (Song et al., 2025) F1 scores are the field's default currency. They are single-number leaderboard results; no paper has shown that ProcessBench F1 predicts $U_{\text{RL}}$.

**Negative result.** DeepSeek-R1 (2025) reports abandoning PRMs for large-scale RL: reward hacking and retraining cost outweighed gains. This is the strongest evidence that $U_{\text{BoN}}$ and $U_{\text{RL}}$ come apart.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Outcome vs. process, matched final-answer supervision.** Uesato et al. (2022), 70B on GSM8K: final-answer error essentially equal between outcome- and process-supervised reward models (~12.7% vs 12.7%); trace error dropped from 14.0% to 3.4% under process supervision. Scale: 70B, GSM8K only.
- **MC-derived labels are systematically worse than judged labels.** Zhang et al. (2025), "The Lessons of Developing PRMs": MC-estimation data alone produces PRMs that score high on best-of-8 but poorly on step localization; consensus filtering between MC estimates and an LLM judge was required. Scale: Qwen2.5-Math 7B/72B.
- **ProcessBench spread is enormous.** Reported average F1: Math-Shepherd-PRM-7B ≈ 21, GPT-4o-0806 = 61.9, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B = 65.5, Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B = 73.5. A $3\times$ F1 gap between two MC-supervised 7B models means label *construction*, not label *count*, dominates.
- **Best-of-$n$ saturates and then inverts.** Beyond a few hundred samples, PRM-guided selection degrades as the verifier is over-optimized — visible in Lightman et al.'s own curves and in Snell et al. (2024) test-time-compute scaling.
- **Advantage-shaped process rewards buy sample efficiency.** Setlur et al. (ICLR 2025) report ~$5$–$6\times$ improvement in test-time search sample efficiency and ~$6\times$ in RL over outcome rewards, at 2B–9B scale on MATH.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open.** The curve $U(B)$ itself. No paper reports PRM quality at $B \in \{10^3, 10^4, 10^5, 10^6\}$ labels with everything else fixed. The experiment is runnable today for well under 10k GPU-hours; nobody has published it.
- **Empirically open.** Whether human labels are worth more than MC labels *per unit cost*. PRM800K and Math-Shepherd differ in generator, base model, domain and step segmentation simultaneously.
- **Methodologically blocked.** What "a process label" means. Under MC labeling a label is a noisy $\hat V_k$; under human labeling it is a correctness judgment; under LLM-judge labeling it is a critique. These are different random variables and are counted with the same integer.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Whether ProcessBench F1 is a valid proxy for RL utility. No published correlation between the two across ≥5 PRMs.
- **Theoretically open.** A sample-complexity separation. Standard RL theory gives horizon-dependent separations between dense and sparse reward, but nothing accounts for the LLM case where the sparse-reward signal is dense in *pretraining* prior. No lower bound rules out $\alpha$ being the same for outcome and process supervision.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**The specific obstruction is absent ground truth compounded by non-identifiability of the target.**

There is no reference "correct" step-value function to measure labels against. MC estimates are unbiased for $V^\pi$, but $V^\pi$ is provably the wrong objective for search (Setlur et al.): the useful signal is $A^\mu(s_t) = V^\mu(s_{\le t}) - V^\mu(s_{<t})$ under a *different* prover policy. So a labeling scheme can be more accurate about $V^\pi$ and less useful downstream. The label-efficiency question is therefore not "how many samples to estimate a fixed function" but "how many samples to estimate an unspecified one."

Secondary: the evaluation does not measure what it names. ProcessBench names step-error localization; PRMs are deployed for reranking and RL. And the confound is total: every published PRM varies base model, generator, step segmentation, and label source together, so no two points lie on the same curve.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Generative / chain-of-thought verifiers.** ThinkPRM, GenPRM, and successors trade training labels for verification-time tokens. Active at UMich, Tsinghua, and Qwen. The open question — the label-for-inference-token exchange rate — is unanswered *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Implicit PRMs from outcome labels.** Yuan et al.'s DPO-parameterized reward is the cleanest zero-process-label baseline; several groups now use it as the default control.
- **Advantage/prover-policy shaping.** CMU/Google (Setlur, Kumar) continuing on PAVs; the natural extension is measuring $\alpha$ under advantage targets.
- **PRM-free RL.** GRPO-style outcome-only RL (DeepSeek-R1 and descendants) is the dominant industrial practice, which makes the label-efficiency question sharper: PRMs must justify their labeling cost against a method that needs none.
- **Benchmark reform.** PRMBench adds fine-grained error taxonomies; validity against RL utility remains unestablished *(frontier — verify)*.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**The label-efficiency curve at matched labeling compute.**

- **Scale.** One base model (Qwen2.5-Math-7B), one generator, one fixed pool of 200K solution traces on MATH+GSM8K, one step segmentation rule.
- **Arms.** Label budgets $B \in \{10^3, 10^4, 10^5, 10^6\}$ step labels under four schemes: (a) MC with $k=8$; (b) MC with $k=64$ on $1/8$ as many steps (identical rollout tokens); (c) LLM-judge critiques; (d) human labels subsampled from PRM800K.
- **Control arm.** Implicit PRM from outcome labels only, trained on the same 200K traces — zero process labels, and hence the reservation price any process scheme must beat.
- **Evaluation.** For each of 16 PRMs: ProcessBench F1, best-of-64 accuracy, and $U_{\text{RL}}$ after a fixed 500-step GRPO run using the PRM as a dense shaping term.
- **The deciding number.** The Spearman correlation $\rho$ between ProcessBench F1 and $U_{\text{RL}}$ across the 16 models. If $\rho < 0.5$, the field's entire label-efficiency literature is optimizing a proxy that does not predict deployment utility, and every "$38\times$ fewer labels" claim is unfalsified rather than established. Secondary number: the fitted $\alpha$ per scheme — if arms (a) and (b) differ, label *count* is the wrong currency and rollout tokens is the right one.

Cost estimate: ~4k H100-hours for labeling, ~2k for training and RL. Within a single academic lab's annual budget.

## 10. Worked Example

Label 12,000 MATH training problems, $N=15$ solutions each, $T=8$ steps, $k=8$ rollouts per step, $L=300$ tokens.

$$B_{\text{tok}} = 12{,}000 \times 15 \times 8 \times 8 \times 300 \approx 3.5 \times 10^{9} \text{ tokens},$$

producing $12{,}000 \times 15 \times 8 = 1.44 \times 10^6$ step labels — about $2{,}400$ generated tokens per label.

Now the obstruction. Take a step whose true $V^\pi = 0.30$. With $k=8$, $\hat V_8$ has SD $0.162$. If the training label is the binarization $\mathbb{1}[\hat V_8 > 0]$, the step is labeled *positive* with probability $1 - 0.7^8 = 0.94$. A step with true $V^\pi = 0.05$ is labeled positive with probability $1 - 0.95^8 = 0.34$. The label carries roughly one bit and cannot separate a 30%-viable step from a 5%-viable one.

Spending the same $3.5\times10^9$ tokens at $k=64$ gives $1.8\times10^5$ labels — $8\times$ fewer — but SD $0.057$, enough to separate $0.30$ from $0.20$. Which arm produces the better PRM is *not known*. No published experiment holds $B_{\text{tok}}$ fixed and varies $k$.

Compare the control: the implicit PRM extracts step scores from the same 180,000 traces using only $1.8\times10^5$ outcome labels and $0$ extra rollout tokens — a $3.5\times10^9$-token saving. Its reported edge over Math-Shepherd is stated as "$1/38$ the data," where "data" means *trace count*, and the rollout tokens Math-Shepherd burned are not in the comparison at all. The efficiency claim and the efficiency question are denominated in different units. That mismatch, not any missing theorem, is what keeps the problem open.
