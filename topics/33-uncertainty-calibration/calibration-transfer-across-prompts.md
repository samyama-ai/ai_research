# Calibration Transfer Across Prompt Formats

> **Topic:** Uncertainty & Calibration · **ID:** `33-uncertainty-calibration/calibration-transfer-across-prompts` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

A confidence estimator is fitted or validated under one prompt format (a template, separator set, option-label scheme, instruction phrasing, few-shot ordering). It is then deployed under a different but *semantically equivalent* format. The question: does the calibration survive?

- **Input.** A model $f$, a task distribution $D$, a set of semantically equivalent prompt formats $\mathcal{T}$, a confidence functional $c$ (token probability, verbalized confidence, self-consistency frequency, trained probe), and a recalibration map $g$ fitted on format $t_{\text{src}}$.
- **Output.** The calibration error of $g \circ c$ under format $t_{\text{tgt}}$, and the *transfer gap* between source and target.
- **Decision predicate.** Is there a recalibration method whose calibration error under unseen formats stays within a fixed tolerance (say $\varepsilon = 0.02$ ECE) of its in-format error, uniformly over $\mathcal{T}$?

Three variants, different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Define and estimate format-conditional calibration error with a bias-controlled estimator, separating format-induced *accuracy* shift from format-induced *confidence* shift. Partly blocked.
- **Method.** Build $g$ that transfers. Empirically open; candidates exist, none ablated across formats at scale.
- **Theory.** Prove conditions on $f$ and $\mathcal{T}$ under which a single scalar recalibration is format-invariant, or prove no such condition holds for autoregressive LMs. Open, and probably requires assumptions nobody can verify.

## 2. Formal Setting

Task instance $(x, y) \sim D$, label space $\mathcal{Y}$. A format $t \in \mathcal{T}$ is a rendering map $t: \mathcal{X} \to \Sigma^*$. **Semantic equivalence** is defined operationally, not semantically: $\mathcal{T}$ is generated by a grammar over separators, casing, option delimiters, spacing, and instruction paraphrases that provably preserves the intended answer set (the FormatSpread construction, Sclar et al. 2024).

Model score for candidate $y$:
$$s_t(y \mid x) = \log p_f\big(\text{verbalizer}(y) \mid t(x)\big),$$
measured as the sum of token log-probs of the verbalizer string, length-normalized or not — the choice changes the number and must be reported.

Confidence: $c_t(x) = \max_{y} \mathrm{softmax}_\tau\big(s_t(\cdot \mid x)\big)_y$ for logit-based methods, or the parsed scalar in $[0,1]$ for verbalized confidence, or $\frac{1}{K}\sum_k \mathbf{1}[\hat y_k = \hat y]$ over $K$ samples for self-consistency.

Format-conditional calibration error, $\ell_p$ form:
$$\mathrm{CE}_p(t) = \Big(\mathbb{E}_{x \sim D}\big|\,\mathbb{P}(\hat y_t(x) = y \mid c_t(x)) - c_t(x)\big|^p\Big)^{1/p}.$$
Measured as binned ECE with $M$ equal-mass bins over $N$ held-out items; report $M$, $N$, and use the debiased estimator of Kumar, Liang & Ma (2019) — the plug-in estimator is biased downward and the bias grows with $M$.

**Transfer gap.** With $g_{t_{\text{src}}}$ fitted on source-format validation data,
$$\Delta(t_{\text{src}} \to t_{\text{tgt}}) = \mathrm{CE}\big(g_{t_{\text{src}}} \circ c_{t_{\text{tgt}}}\big) - \mathrm{CE}\big(g_{t_{\text{tgt}}} \circ c_{t_{\text{tgt}}}\big),$$
i.e. the excess error from using the wrong format's calibrator versus the oracle in-format calibrator. Worst-case gap $\Delta^\star = \max_{t_{\text{src}}, t_{\text{tgt}}} \Delta$.

**Decomposition.** Split the observed change into an accuracy term and a confidence term:
$$\underbrace{\mathrm{acc}(t_{\text{tgt}}) - \mathrm{acc}(t_{\text{src}})}_{\text{competence shift}}, \qquad \underbrace{\bar c_{t_{\text{tgt}}} - \bar c_{t_{\text{src}}}}_{\text{confidence shift}}.$$
A method can be format-robust in ECE while both terms move a lot, if they move together. That coincidence must be reported, not assumed.

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**

1. *Semantic equivalence implies label invariance.* Violated: formats change the argmax on 5–20% of items in reported studies, so $\hat y_t$ is format-dependent and the "same" question has different model answers.
2. *Confidence is a single well-defined scalar.* Violated for verbalized confidence, which is itself produced by a prompt and inherits the same sensitivity.
3. *Held-out items are i.i.d. across formats.* Holds by construction if the same $N$ items are rendered in every format — but then per-format ECEs are strongly dependent, and naive significance tests are invalid.
4. *Calibration is a property of the model.* Not established; it may be a property of the (model, format) pair, which is precisely the open question.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established.**

- Temperature scaling (Guo et al., ICML 2017) as the default post-hoc map; verified debiased calibration estimators (Kumar, Liang & Ma, NeurIPS 2019).
- Format sensitivity of accuracy is established: FormatSpread (Sclar et al., ICLR 2024) reports accuracy spreads up to ~76 points across semantically equivalent formats on LLaMA-2-13B-scale models; Mizrahi et al. (TACL 2024) show single-prompt evaluation reorders model rankings; Alzahrani et al. (ACL 2024) show leaderboard rank flips from minor formatting choices.
- Contextual Calibration (Zhao et al., ICML 2021) improves GPT-3 few-shot accuracy by up to ~30 points absolute by correcting a content-free-input prior; Batch Calibration (Zhou et al., ICLR 2024) generalizes this and reports consistent gains over CC/DC across PaLM 2 and CLIP.
- Verbalized confidence works better than raw token probability for RLHF'd models on some suites: "Just Ask for Calibration" (Tian et al., EMNLP 2023) reports lower ECE from verbalized numeric confidence than conditional probabilities on GPT-3.5/GPT-4-class models.

**Claimed but unablated.** That any of these calibrators *transfer* across formats. Contextual and Batch Calibration are computed per prompt-context, so they are format-specific by construction, and neither paper reports the transfer gap $\Delta$. Verbalized-confidence advantages are reported on a fixed elicitation template; the sensitivity of that template is not ablated.

**Benchmark-number only.** The GPT-4 technical report's calibration figure (pretrained MMLU ECE ≈ 0.007, degraded roughly an order of magnitude after PPO) is a single-format, single-suite number with no released prompt-format sweep. It is widely cited as evidence about models; it is evidence about a (model, format, suite) triple.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Format changes accuracy at every scale tested.** Sclar et al.: up to 76-point accuracy spread, LLaMA-2-7B/13B, across thousands of grammar-generated formats on tasks including a subset of SuperNaturalInstructions.
- **Format changes model rankings.** Mizrahi et al. (TACL 2024): across ~20 model/task pairs and dozens of paraphrased instructions, the top-ranked model changes with the prompt paraphrase.
- **Fine-tuned encoders are mildly miscalibrated in-domain and badly out-of-domain.** Desai & Durrett (EMNLP 2020): BERT/RoBERTa in-domain ECE in the low single-digit percent, reducible to ~1% by temperature scaling; out-of-domain ECE is several times larger and temperature scaling fitted in-domain does not fix it.
- **Self-evaluation calibration improves with scale.** Kadavath et al. (2022), models up to 52B: $P(\text{IK})$ and "True/False" self-evaluation are reasonably calibrated at the largest scale and poorly at small scale.
- **Surface form confounds probability.** Holtzman et al. (EMNLP 2021): raw string probability penalizes verbalizers with more probable surface competitors; PMI-style scoring changes both accuracy and confidence.

The out-of-domain result (Desai & Durrett) is the closest established analogue: a fitted recalibration map does not survive distribution shift. Whether format change is a distribution shift of that magnitude is exactly what has not been measured.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open.** The value of $\Delta^\star$ for any modern model. Nobody has published a full $|\mathcal{T}| \times |\mathcal{T}|$ transfer matrix of calibration error for even one 7B model on one task. The experiment is runnable today for a few thousand GPU-hours.
- **Empirically open.** Whether verbalized confidence is more or less format-transferable than logit confidence. Both are plausible; the ablation is missing.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Attributing the transfer gap to confidence versus competence. When the argmax itself changes with format, the calibration target $\mathbb{P}(\hat y_t = y \mid c_t)$ is defined on a different predictor, so "same model, different calibration" is not cleanly identified.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether format-invariant calibration is achievable in principle by any post-hoc scalar map. A single-parameter temperature cannot correct a format-induced prior shift over $\mathcal{Y}$ (Zhao et al. show the shift is affine in logit space, not scalar), which suggests a negative result — but it is not proved for the general case.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The specific obstruction is **non-identifiability plus a compounding measurement bias**.

Non-identifiability: format perturbs the predictor and the confidence jointly. Observing $\mathrm{CE}(t_1) \neq \mathrm{CE}(t_2)$ does not separate "the model is less sure and correctly so" from "the model's confidence scale is format-dependent". The counterfactual — same predictions, different format — does not exist, because format changes predictions.

Measurement bias: the estimand is second-order. ECE itself is a biased, binning-dependent estimate of a first-order quantity; the transfer gap is a difference of two such estimates. With $N = 1{,}000$ items and $M = 15$ bins, plug-in ECE bias is on the order of $\sqrt{M/N} \approx 0.12$ in the worst case — larger than the $\varepsilon = 0.02$ tolerance the problem is about. Any transfer matrix computed with small $N$ per cell measures binning noise, not transfer.

Second obstruction: the format space is combinatorially large and has no natural measure. $\Delta^\star$ over an adversarially chosen $\mathcal{T}$ is unbounded by construction; $\Delta$ averaged over a "natural" $\mathcal{T}$ depends entirely on a prior nobody has defended.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Multi-prompt evaluation as standard practice.** Mizrahi et al. and the FormatSpread line push distributional reporting over point estimates; HELM-style harnesses have begun reporting prompt variance. Extension to calibration metrics is partial *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Context-level calibration.** Batch Calibration (Google) and successors estimate the correction from the deployed batch itself, sidestepping transfer by refitting per context. This is a practical answer that dodges the scientific question.
- **Conformal prediction under prompt variation.** Split conformal gives marginal coverage under exchangeability; format change breaks exchangeability, and format-conditional coverage work is active *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Trained probes / linear readouts of internal state** as format-invariant confidence, on the hypothesis that residual-stream correctness features are more stable than output distributions. Claimed in several 2024–2025 preprints; cross-format ablation is the missing piece *(frontier — verify)*.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Transfer matrix, one model, one task, honest error bars.**

- **Scale.** One open 7–8B instruction-tuned model plus one 70B control. $|\mathcal{T}| = 20$ grammar-generated equivalent formats (separator, option-label style, instruction paraphrase, few-shot order). $N = 5{,}000$ MMLU-style multiple-choice items, all rendered in all 20 formats. Cost: $20 \times 5{,}000 = 10^5$ forward passes per model — hours on one node.
- **Arms.** (a) raw max-softmax; (b) temperature scaling fitted on $t_{\text{src}}$; (c) contextual/batch calibration fitted on $t_{\text{src}}$; (d) verbalized confidence; (e) linear probe on final-layer residual fitted on $t_{\text{src}}$.
- **Control arm.** The oracle in-format calibrator $g_{t_{\text{tgt}}}$ fitted on a disjoint half of the target-format data. This is the only control that separates transfer failure from irreducible format difficulty.
- **Estimator.** Debiased ECE (Kumar et al. 2019), $M = 15$ equal-mass bins, bootstrap over items with the format-pairing preserved.
- **Deciding number.** $\Delta^\star$, the worst-case excess ECE over the $20 \times 19$ ordered format pairs, per method. If $\Delta^\star < 0.02$ for any method, calibration transfers and the problem is closed for that method class. If $\Delta^\star > 0.10$ for all five arms — plausible given a 76-point accuracy spread — calibration is a property of the (model, format) pair and every single-format ECE in the literature is uninterpretable as a model property.

Report alongside it the competence/confidence decomposition, so a small $\Delta^\star$ cannot be credited when it is cancellation.

## 10. Worked Example

Two formats for one MMLU item, both semantically equivalent:

- $t_1$: `Question: {q}\nA. {a}\nB. {b}\nC. {c}\nD. {d}\nAnswer:`
- $t_2$: `{q}\n (a) {a} | (b) {b} | (c) {c} | (d) {d}\nThe answer is`

Suppose on $N = 1{,}000$ items a 7B model gives:

| | acc | mean conf | ECE (raw) | fitted $T$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $t_1$ | 0.61 | 0.74 | 0.13 | 1.9 |
| $t_2$ | 0.55 | 0.83 | 0.28 | 3.1 |

Apply $T = 1.9$ (fitted on $t_1$) to $t_2$ confidences. Temperature scaling shrinks the mean confidence toward uniform ($0.25$ for four options), roughly $0.83 \to 0.66$ against a $t_2$ accuracy of $0.55$: residual ECE ≈ $0.11$. The oracle $T = 3.1$ takes mean confidence to ≈ $0.57$: ECE ≈ $0.03$. So $\Delta(t_1 \to t_2) \approx 0.08$ — four times the $0.02$ tolerance, from nothing but bracket style and separators.

Now the obstruction. Accuracy also moved, $0.61 \to 0.55$. Two readings are consistent with the same table:

1. The model is genuinely worse under $t_2$ (competence shift) and its confidence simply failed to follow — a confidence-scale failure.
2. $t_2$ induces a prior shift toward option `(a)`, changing the argmax on ~9% of items. Then $\hat y_{t_2}$ is a *different classifier*, and comparing its calibration to $\hat y_{t_1}$'s calibrator is comparing two predictors, not one predictor under two measurements.

Reading 2 is not fixable by a scalar $T$: a per-option logit offset is required (Zhao et al.), which is a 4-parameter correction and must itself be refit per format. Nothing in the table distinguishes the two readings. Add that the raw ECEs are plug-in estimates with $\sqrt{15/1000} \approx 0.12$ worst-case bias, and the $0.08$ gap is not even safely above noise at this $N$. That is why the deciding experiment needs $N = 5{,}000$, debiased ECE, and the in-format oracle control — not a bigger table of single-format numbers.
