# Optimal Rollout Horizon in Dyna-Style Model-Based RL

> **Topic:** World Models & Planning · **ID:** `35-world-models/optimal-rollout-horizon-model-based-rl` · **Status:** empirically-open

## 1. Problem Statement

Dyna-style model-based RL trains a policy on synthetic transitions generated by rolling a learned dynamics model forward $k$ steps from states drawn from the real replay buffer. Longer rollouts give more synthetic data and propagate reward information further; they also compound model error, so the synthetic data drifts off the real state distribution. The problem: **choose $k$**.

Three variants, with different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given a run, define and estimate the *marginal value of the $k$-th imagined step* — the change in final real-environment return caused by extending rollouts from $k-1$ to $k$, holding the synthetic-to-real data ratio fixed. Currently this is confounded (§6) and arguably not well posed.
- **Method.** Produce a rule that sets $k$ (per state, per timestep, or per training epoch) from quantities observable during training — model ensemble disagreement, held-out one-step error, value-function TD error — and beats the best *constant* $k$ tuned by sweep, on a held-out task suite, at matched compute.
- **Theory.** Give a non-vacuous bound on the return gap of a policy trained on $k$-step branched rollouts, whose minimizing $k^*$ is (a) strictly positive and (b) predictive of the empirically optimal $k$ within a factor of ~2.

A solution to the method variant is an adaptive-$k$ rule that dominates constant-$k$ across environments with no per-environment tuning. A solution to the theory variant is a bound whose $k^*$ tracks the measured optimum rather than collapsing to $k^*=0$ or $k^*=\infty$.

## 2. Formal Setting

MDP $M=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},p,r,\gamma,\rho_0)$, discount $\gamma\in[0,1)$, returns bounded by $r_{\max}$. Learned model $\hat p_\theta$ (typically a Gaussian ensemble, $\hat p_\theta(s'\mid s,a)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta,\Sigma_\theta)$) fit by maximum likelihood on the real buffer $\mathcal{D}_{\text{env}}$.

**Branched rollout.** Sample $s\sim d^{\pi_{\text{old}}}_{\mathcal{D}_{\text{env}}}$ (empirical state distribution of the real buffer), then roll $\hat p_\theta$ forward $k$ steps under the current policy $\pi$, writing transitions into $\mathcal{D}_{\text{model}}$. The critic is trained on a mixture $\alpha \mathcal{D}_{\text{model}} + (1-\alpha)\mathcal{D}_{\text{env}}$; in MBPO $\alpha\approx0.95$.

**Quantities, as measured.**

- Model error, measured as total variation on held-out real transitions from the *current* policy:
 $$\epsilon_m \;=\; \max_t \; \mathbb{E}_{s\sim d^{\pi}_t}\big[ D_{TV}\big(p(\cdot\mid s,a)\,\|\,\hat p_\theta(\cdot\mid s,a)\big)\big].$$
 In practice $D_{TV}$ is not estimable from samples in continuous $\mathcal{S}$; implementations substitute held-out negative log-likelihood or normalized $\ell_2$ error $\|\mu_\theta(s,a)-s'\|_2/\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}$, which is a different quantity and not an upper bound on $D_{TV}$.
- Policy shift $\epsilon_\pi = \max_s D_{TV}(\pi(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_{\text{old}}(\cdot\mid s))$, measured as mean per-batch KL between current and buffer-time policy heads (again a proxy, and the max over $\mathcal{S}$ is replaced by an average over the buffer).
- Compounding error at horizon $k$: measured as $e(k)=\mathbb{E}\|\hat s_k - s_k\|_2/\sigma_{\mathcal{D}}$ over open-loop $k$-step rollouts from real states with real actions. Empirically $e(k)$ grows super-linearly; a Lipschitz model with constant $L$ gives $e(k)\le \epsilon_1 \frac{L^k-1}{L-1}$ (Asadi et al., ICML 2018), which is exponential whenever $L>1$, and $L>1$ for every contact-rich robotics domain.
- Real return $J(\pi)$, measured as the mean over $\ge 10$ evaluation episodes, $\ge 5$ seeds; per-seed standard deviation on MuJoCo locomotion at 200k–400k steps is routinely 10–30% of the mean.

**The reference bound** (Janner et al., NeurIPS 2019, Thm. 4.3):
$$J(\pi)\;\ge\; J^{\text{branch}}_k(\pi)\;-\;2r_{\max}\Big[\frac{\gamma^{k+1}\epsilon_\pi}{(1-\gamma)^2}+\frac{\gamma^{k}\epsilon_\pi}{1-\gamma}+\frac{k}{1-\gamma}\big(\epsilon_m+2\epsilon_\pi\big)\Big].$$
The first two terms decay in $k$; the third grows linearly. $k^*$ is interior only if $\epsilon_m$ is small relative to $\epsilon_\pi$-driven terms — and with $\epsilon_m$ held constant the derivative at $k=0$ is typically positive, so $k^*=0$.

**Assumptions known to be violated:** (i) $\epsilon_m$ independent of $k$ — false, error under the rollout's own drifted state distribution is much larger than under $d^\pi$; (ii) the model is used only for data generation, not for value bootstrapping — false in MVE/STEVE-style hybrids; (iii) branch states are distributed as $d^\pi$ — false, they are distributed as the buffer, which is a mixture over all past policies; (iv) bounded $r_{\max}$ with meaningful scale — MuJoCo rewards are unbounded in practice.

## 3. State of the Art

**Empirical SOTA (established).** MBPO (Janner et al., NeurIPS 2019) with *hand-tuned per-environment $k$ schedules* remains the reference. Published settings span a constant $k=1$ for several MuJoCo tasks up to linear ramps reaching $k=15$–$25$ over training for Hopper and Humanoid. That per-environment tuning is the result, not a side note: no published rule reproduces those schedules from observables.

**Latent-space imagination (established as a benchmark number, not as an ablation).** DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023) uses a *fixed* imagination horizon $H=15$ across >150 tasks spanning DMC, Atari, and Minecraft, with no per-domain tuning. This is strong evidence that in a learned latent space with a recurrent state-space model, horizon sensitivity is much flatter — but the paper does not ablate $H$ across that suite, so "$H=15$ is optimal everywhere" is claimed by construction and unablated.

**Adaptive-$k$ methods (claimed, thinly ablated).** M2AC (masked model-based actor-critic) and its relatives gate individual imagined transitions by ensemble variance; "Trust the Model Where It Trusts Itself" (Frauenknecht et al., ICLR 2024) sets rollout length per state from model uncertainty. Reported gains over constant-$k$ MBPO are typically within 1–2 seed-standard-deviations on 4–6 MuJoCo tasks — real, but not established at the level of an independently reproduced ablation.

**Theory SOTA.** The Janner bound above; Lipschitz-model compounding-error bounds (Asadi, Misra, Littman, ICML 2018); bidirectional rollouts halving effective compounding depth (Lai et al., ICML 2020). None yields a $k^*$ that matches measurement.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Constant $k=1$ is a strong baseline.** On MuJoCo Hopper/Walker2d/HalfCheetah at 100k–400k env steps, $k=1$ MBPO matches or beats SAC's 1M-step asymptote on several tasks. Scale: 4-layer, ~200-unit ensembles of 7 models, single GPU.
- **Horizon sensitivity is large and environment-specific.** Zhang et al. (AISTATS 2021), "On the Importance of Hyperparameter Optimization for Model-Based RL", found rollout length among the highest-sensitivity hyperparameters, with automated tuning changing final MuJoCo return by tens of percent — measured at MBPO scale (~10$^5$ env steps, ~10$^2$ GPU-hours per sweep).
- **Compounding error grows super-linearly.** Open-loop normalized $\ell_2$ error on MuJoCo dynamics ensembles typically rises by roughly an order of magnitude between $k=1$ and $k=20$; exact factors are implementation-dependent.
- **Value-expansion horizons behave differently.** MVE (Feinberg et al., 2018) at fixed $H$ degrades when $H$ exceeds model competence; STEVE (Buckman et al., NeurIPS 2018) fixes this by inverse-variance-weighting across horizons $0..H$, so no single $H$ must be chosen. This is the one *solved* sub-case — and it is solved for bootstrapping, not for data generation.
- **Latent models tolerate longer horizons.** $H=15$ works across DreamerV3's whole suite; pixel-space $k=15$ does not.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** Whether any bound on the branched-rollout return gap has an interior $k^*>0$ under assumptions that hold in practice. Every current bound either collapses to $k^*=0$ (error term linear in $k$, dominant) or requires the unmeasurable $\epsilon_m$ under the drifted distribution. Equally open: whether the optimal $k$ scales with $1/(1-\gamma)$, with the model's Lipschitz constant $L$, with dataset size, or with none of these.
- **Empirically open.** Nobody has run a clean, compute-matched sweep of $k\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$ across a $\ge$15-task suite with $\ge$10 seeds, holding *synthetic gradient steps per real step* fixed. The compute is roughly $6\times15\times10 = 900$ runs — order 10$^4$ GPU-hours, entirely feasible for a mid-sized lab. It has not been done.
- **Methodologically blocked.** "Marginal value of the $k$-th step" has no agreed estimator, because changing $k$ changes three things at once (§6).

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**The specific obstruction is confounded measurement.** Increasing $k$ simultaneously changes:

1. **Data volume.** With a fixed number of branch points, $k=8$ produces 8× the synthetic transitions of $k=1$. If gradient steps per real step are held fixed, the buffer turnover and effective sample reuse both change.
2. **State-distribution coverage.** Longer rollouts reach states the real buffer never contains — the intended benefit and the source of the error.
3. **Bias in the critic target.** Deeper synthetic data changes the critic's bootstrapping distribution, interacting with the well-documented overestimation dynamics of off-policy actor-critics.

A naive $k$ sweep therefore measures the sum of a data-quantity effect, a coverage effect, and a critic-bias effect. Published sweeps do not disentangle them; most do not even hold the model-to-real ratio $\alpha$ fixed. Compounding this: $\epsilon_m$ as it appears in theory is a total-variation distance under the rollout's own state distribution, and there is no consistent estimator for it in continuous state spaces from the samples a training run produces. The theory's controlling quantity is not measurable, and the empirical knob is not identifiable.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Per-state adaptive termination** — halt a rollout when ensemble disagreement crosses a threshold calibrated on held-out data. Active at ETH Zürich / MPI-IS and in the offline-RL uncertainty-penalty line (MOPO/MOReL descendants). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Horizon-agnostic aggregation** — extend STEVE's inverse-variance weighting from value expansion to data generation, so $k$ is marginalized rather than chosen.
- **Latent-space horizon scaling** — whether DreamerV3's $H=15$ insensitivity survives at 10$^9$-parameter world models and video-pretrained dynamics; TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., ICLR 2024) uses short planning horizons ($H=3$) with a learned terminal value, suggesting horizon and value-function quality trade off directly. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Model-error-aware scheduling** driven by predicted-vs-realized return gap rather than by dynamics error.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Does the optimal $k$ have an interior optimum once data volume is controlled?

**Scale.** 15 tasks (9 DMC + 6 Gym MuJoCo), $k\in\{1,2,4,8,16,32\}$, 10 seeds, 300k env steps each: 900 runs, ~10$^4$ GPU-hours on A100-class hardware.

**Control arm — this is the whole point.** Hold the number of synthetic transitions per real step *constant* across $k$ by scaling branch points as $n_{\text{branch}}(k) = N/k$. Hold $\alpha$, gradient steps per real step, ensemble size, and model retraining cadence fixed. Add a second control: $k$-step rollouts from the *true simulator* (oracle model), same $n_{\text{branch}}(k)$, which isolates the coverage effect from the model-error effect.

**Deciding number.** For each task, fit final normalized return $R(k)$ and report $\hat k^* = \arg\max_k R(k)$ with a bootstrap CI over seeds. The single decisive statistic: **the fraction of the 15 tasks whose bootstrap 95% CI for $\hat k^*$ excludes $k=1$.** If that fraction is $<0.3$, longer Dyna rollouts buy nothing once volume is controlled, and the field's $k$ schedules are compensating for data quantity — a deflationary but decisive result. If $>0.7$, an interior optimum is real and the theory's $k^*=0$ collapse is the bug. The oracle arm distinguishes the two failure modes: if oracle $\hat k^*$ is also 1, the limit is credit assignment, not model error.

## 10. Worked Example

Take Hopper-v2, MBPO configuration, $\gamma=0.99$, ensemble of 7 probabilistic models, 400 branch points per real step.

Plug measured quantities into the Janner bound. Suppose held-out one-step normalized error is $0.02$ and we optimistically read $\epsilon_m\approx0.02$; policy shift per epoch gives $\epsilon_\pi\approx0.01$; $r_{\max}\approx4$ (Hopper's alive bonus plus velocity term). Then with $1/(1-\gamma)=100$:

- $k=1$: decay terms $\approx \frac{0.99^2\cdot0.01}{10^{-4}} + \frac{0.99\cdot 0.01}{0.01} \approx 98 + 0.99 \approx 99$; growth term $= \frac{1}{0.01}(0.02+0.02)=4.0$. Total $\times 2r_{\max}=8$: $\approx 824$.
- $k=15$: decay terms $\approx 0.99^{16}\cdot 100 + 0.99^{15}\cdot 1 \approx 85.5$; growth term $=15\times 4.0=60$. Total $\approx 1164$.

So the bound says $k=1$ beats $k=15$ by ~340 return units — while Hopper's *published* schedule ramps to $k=15$ and that ramp is what makes MBPO reach ~3000 return in 120k steps. The bound is 8–10× larger than the entire achievable return, and it ranks the horizons backwards.

Now the empirical side. Run $k=1$ and $k=15$ with 400 branch points each. $k=15$ writes 6000 synthetic transitions per real step versus 400 — a 15× data increase. Rerun $k=15$ with 27 branch points (matched volume) and the advantage over $k=1$ in a typical MBPO reimplementation shrinks to within seed noise (per-seed SD on Hopper at 120k steps is roughly $\pm$400 return).

That is the obstruction in one instance: **the theory's ranking is inverted and vacuous, and the empirical advantage of long rollouts is confounded with a 15× increase in synthetic data.** Neither the bound nor the sweep, as currently run, answers whether the 15th imagined step is worth anything.
