# Learning Dynamics From Passive Video Without Action Labels

> **Topic:** World Models & Planning · **ID:** `35-world-models/action-free-dynamics-from-video` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement

**Input.** A corpus $\mathcal{D} = \{o_{1:T}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$ of video sequences with no action, reward, or agent-identity annotation. Frames may come from different embodiments, cameras, and controllers.

**Output.** A controllable dynamics model $\hat{T}(o_{t+1} \mid o_{\le t}, a_t)$ over *some* action space, plus a map from that space to a target agent's real actuators, learned with few or zero action labels.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Method.** Build a model whose latent control variable improves downstream control after $m \ll N$ labeled transitions. Partially solved: VPT, LAPO, LAPA, Genie all show gains.
- **Measurement.** Decide whether a learned latent $z_t$ *is* the action rather than a compressed frame-difference. No accepted metric. This is the binding constraint.
- **Theory.** State conditions on $\mathcal{D}$ under which the action variable is identifiable up to a bijection from passive observation alone. Open except under strong structural assumptions.

Solving it means: given action-free video only, produce a model that plans to a goal in a *held-out* environment with per-task label budget $m \le 10^2$ transitions, and does so measurably better than a video predictor with no latent-action bottleneck.

## 2. Formal Setting

Ground truth is a POMDP $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{O}, T, \Omega, \gamma\rangle$ with $o_t \sim \Omega(\cdot\mid s_t)$, $s_{t+1}\sim T(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$. The dataset is generated by an unobserved behavior policy $\pi_b(a_t \mid s_t, c)$ where $c$ is an unobserved context (skill level, task, embodiment).

**Latent action model.** An inverse model $q_\phi(z_t \mid o_{t-k:t+1})$ with $z_t$ quantized to a codebook $\mathcal{Z}$, $|\mathcal{Z}|=K$ (Genie uses $K=8$), and a forward model $p_\theta(o_{t+1}\mid o_{\le t}, z_t)$, trained on

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\big[-\log p_\theta(o_{t+1}\mid o_{\le t}, z_t)\big] + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{VQ}}(z_t),\qquad z_t\sim q_\phi.$$

The bottleneck $\log_2 K$ bits is the entire mechanism: it prevents $q_\phi$ from copying $o_{t+1}$ into $z_t$.

**Measured quantities.**

- *Controllability* $\Delta\text{PSNR} = \text{PSNR}(\hat o_{t+1}^{z_t}, o_{t+1}) - \mathbb{E}_{z'\sim \text{Unif}(\mathcal{Z})}\text{PSNR}(\hat o_{t+1}^{z'}, o_{t+1})$, in dB, on held-out video (Genie's metric).
- *Action alignment* $\text{NMI}(z_t; a_t)$ on a held-out slice that *does* have labels, in nats; equivalently top-1 accuracy of a linear probe $\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{A}$.
- *Label efficiency* $m^\star(\epsilon)$: labeled transitions needed to fine-tune to within $\epsilon$ of a behavior-cloning policy trained on the full labeled set.
- *Control return* $R$ on the actual task, which is the only number that is not a proxy.

**Assumptions, and their status in practice.**

1. $o_t$ is Markov-sufficient given a short window — **violated** (occlusion, off-screen agents, momentum beyond $k$ frames).
2. The only stochasticity between consecutive frames is the agent's action — **violated**: camera motion, other agents, video-encoder artifacts, and edited cuts all consume codebook capacity.
3. No unobserved confounder drives both $a_t$ and $o_{t+1}$ — **violated** whenever $\pi_b$ depends on state features the camera does not show (de Haan et al., 2019).
4. Single embodiment / fixed action semantics — **violated by construction** in internet-scale corpora, which is the reason to use them.

Under (1)–(3), $z_t$ is at best identifiable up to a bijection of $\mathcal{A}$; with an entangled $c$, not even that.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established (ablated, and the ablation matters).**

- **VPT** (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) sidesteps the problem: train an inverse dynamics model on 2k hours of *labeled* contractor Minecraft play, pseudo-label 70k hours of web video, behavior-clone. The IDM is non-causal (sees future frames), which is why 2k hours suffices. Ablations show the IDM route beats direct BC on contractor data alone. This is the strongest *result*, but it is semi-supervised, not action-free.
- **LAPO / "Learning to Act without Actions"** (Schmidt & Jiang, ICLR 2024) is the cleanest ablation of the pure form: on Procgen, a VQ latent-action model trained on observations only, then decoded with a small labeled set, recovers most of expert BC performance; removing the quantization bottleneck collapses the result. Scale: 16 Procgen games, small models.
- **Genie** (Bruce et al., ICML 2024, best paper) scaled it: 11B parameters, ~30k hours of filtered 2D-platformer video from a 244k-hour raw pool, $K=8$ discrete latent actions, spatiotemporal-transformer video tokenizer. Ablations of latent-action-model design and $\Delta\text{PSNR}$ are reported.

**Claimed but not independently ablated.**

- **LAPA** (Ye et al., 2024/ICLR 2025) — latent-action pretraining on Something-Something V2 plus Open X-Embodiment, then fine-tune to a VLA; reported to beat OpenVLA on several real-robot suites at far lower pretraining compute. Reported by one group; the cross-embodiment transfer claim has no external replication.
- **Genie 2 / Genie 3** (DeepMind, 2024/2025) — 3D, promptable, longer-horizon consistency. Blog-level artifacts with no paper, no held-out metric, no ablation. *(frontier — verify)*
- **AVDC** (Ko et al., ICLR 2024) and **ATM** (Wen et al., RSS 2024) route around latent actions entirely: predict video or point tracks, then extract actions by dense correspondence / track-conditioned policies. Benchmark numbers on Meta-World and small real-robot sets; success rates are the only reported evidence.

**Theory SOTA.** Weakest leg. **BISCUIT** (Lippe et al., UAI 2023) proves identifiability of causal variables *and* binary interaction variables from video without action labels, given one-to-one interaction structure. **Rybkin et al.** (ICLR 2019) formalize learning an action space from passive video as composable latent transformations. Neither covers continuous, confounded, multi-embodiment data.

## 4. What Is Known

- Non-causal IDMs are cheap and accurate: VPT's IDM, trained on 2k hours, labels 70k hours well enough that the resulting BC agent crafts a diamond pickaxe — a task requiring ~24k actions — at nonzero rate. Scale: 0.5B-parameter policy.
- The discrete bottleneck is load-bearing, not decorative. $K$ around 8 works for platformers (Genie); increasing $K$ lets $z_t$ absorb non-agent motion.
- Latent actions learned on one domain are *reusable*: Genie's platformer-trained latent action model applied to RT-1 robot video produces consistent, human-interpretable action semantics — evidence for embodiment-agnostic structure, reported qualitatively.
- Label efficiency is real but measured only at small scale: LAPO-style decoding needs orders of magnitude fewer labels than full BC on Procgen; nobody has published the equivalent $m^\star(\epsilon)$ curve on a real robot.
- $\Delta\text{PSNR}$ and control return are only loosely coupled. High controllability in dB is achievable by models whose latents encode camera pan.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked (the core gap).** There is no accepted measurement that separates "latent encodes the action" from "latent encodes the frame delta". $\Delta\text{PSNR}$ rewards any predictive side-channel; NMI requires the labels the setting says you do not have. Until a confounder-robust metric exists, SOTA claims are not comparable across papers.
- **Theoretically open.** Conditions on $\mathcal{D}$ under which $\mathcal{A}$ is identifiable up to bijection from passive video, with continuous actions, partial observability, and mixed behavior policies. No proof either way; the negative direction (a construction showing two POMDPs with different action spaces inducing identical observation distributions under their respective $\pi_b$) has not been published either, and would be cheap.
- **Empirically open.** Does action-free video pretraining beat a matched action-labeled-only baseline *at equal downstream label budget* on a real robot, at $\ge$10k-hour video scale? Runnable today; nobody has run the controlled version.
- **Empirically open.** Scaling law of $m^\star(\epsilon)$ in hours of unlabeled video. No published curve.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Non-identifiability under exogenous variation.** The training signal is next-frame prediction, which cannot distinguish "the agent pressed jump" from "the camera moved up" from "the video encoder allocated more bits". Both explain $o_{t+1}$. Codebook capacity is finite, so the latent spends bits on whichever factor most reduces reconstruction loss — usually the largest pixel-space motion, which in web video is camera motion, not agent action.

**Compounded by confounded measurement.** The metrics used to detect the failure ($\Delta$PSNR, FVD) are themselves reconstruction-based, so a model that has learned camera control instead of agent control scores *well*. The evaluation does not measure the thing it names. That is why the field's headline results are demo videos: the artifact humans judge (does it look controllable?) is more diagnostic than the published number.

Secondary: dataset filtering is a hidden variable. Genie's 244k$\to$30k-hour filter is a strong prior on "single agent, fixed camera, deterministic dynamics", and no paper ablates the filter.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **DeepMind** — Genie line, toward 3D promptable interactive environments and agent training inside them *(frontier — verify; no peer-reviewed artifact past Genie 1)*.
- **Robotics VLA groups** (KAIST/Berkeley/NVIDIA lineage around LAPA, OpenVLA) — latent-action pretraining as a cheap substitute for teleoperation data; the open question is whether latent actions survive cross-embodiment transfer.
- **Track/flow-as-action** (Berkeley, CMU: ATM, Track2Act) — replace the learned latent with an explicitly geometric intermediate (point tracks), buying identifiability at the cost of generality.
- **Causal representation learning** (Amsterdam/Mila: Lippe, Lachapelle) — identifiability theory for interaction variables; currently on synthetic and simulated data only.
- **Driving world models** (Wayve GAIA line, others) — action-conditioned at deployment but pretrained on unlabeled dashcam video; the closest thing to a natural large-scale testbed with real actions available for a held-out slice.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question.** Does the latent-action bottleneck buy anything beyond video pretraining, once the label budget is matched?

**Scale.** 1k–3k hours of unlabeled egocentric manipulation video (Ego4D subset + Something-Something V2). Model: ~300M-parameter spatiotemporal transformer, $K \in \{4, 8, 32, 256\}$. Downstream: 10 real or simulated manipulation tasks, label budget swept at $m \in \{10, 10^2, 10^3\}$ labeled transitions per task. Compute: ~2k A100-hours, i.e. an academic-lab experiment, not a frontier run.

**Arms.**
1. Latent-action pretraining (LAPO/Genie style) → decode → fine-tune.
2. **Control arm:** identical architecture and identical video pretraining, latent-action module deleted (unconditional video prediction), same fine-tuning budget.
3. Second control: no pretraining, BC from scratch on the same $m$.

**Deciding number.** Success rate at $m = 10^2$, arm 1 minus arm 2, averaged over 10 tasks and 5 seeds. If the gap is $< 5$ points, the bottleneck is not doing the work attributed to it and the field's story is wrong. Report alongside $\text{NMI}(z_t;a_t)$ on a held-out labeled slice; the informative case is a large control gap with *low* NMI, which would mean the latent helps for reasons other than recovering actions.

## 10. Worked Example

Take 100 hours of platformer video at 10 fps: $3.6\times10^6$ transitions. True action space: 6 buttons, empirical entropy $H(a) \approx 1.8$ bits (idle and right dominate). Codebook $K=8$, so capacity $\log_2 8 = 3$ bits/step — nominally enough.

Now split the observed frame delta into two sources. Sprite motion from the button press moves ~4 px/frame over ~500 px of sprite area. Camera scroll moves the entire ~$256\times256$ background 4 px/frame. Pixel-difference energy is roughly proportional to moving area:

$$\frac{E_{\text{camera}}}{E_{\text{agent}}} \approx \frac{65{,}536}{500} \approx 131.$$

Gradient descent on $-\log p_\theta(o_{t+1}\mid o_{\le t}, z_t)$ allocates the 3 bits to whichever variable most reduces loss. Camera scroll direction is ~2 bits ($H\approx 1.6$ for left/right/up/none) and explains 100× more pixel energy. The loss-minimizing solution spends nearly the whole codebook on scroll.

The observable consequence: $\Delta\text{PSNR}$ is *large* — say +3.5 dB — because knowing the scroll direction genuinely improves the prediction. The page looks like a success. But $\text{NMI}(z_t; a_t)$ measured against a 1k-frame labeled slice comes out near the mutual information between button and scroll, which in a side-scroller where "right" causes scroll is maybe 0.5 of 1.8 bits: the latent has recovered *one* action dimension (horizontal) and none of jump, duck, or attack. Fine-tune a policy from it and jump-dependent levels fail.

The obstruction is visible in the ratio 131, not in either metric alone. Two numbers reported together — $\Delta\text{PSNR}$ high, NMI low — diagnose it; the field currently reports only the first. Camera-stabilizing the video before training moves the ratio to ~1 and is the cheapest test of this whole account: if stabilization raises NMI without changing $\Delta$PSNR much, the bottleneck was being spent on camera motion all along.
