# Data-Constrained Frontier Beyond Web Text

> **Topic:** Scaling Laws & Compute Allocation · **ID:** `10-scaling-laws/data-constrained-frontier` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

Compute-optimal scaling laws assume unique training tokens are free to acquire. They are not. The stock of high-quality public human text is finite and, on current projections, is being consumed at a rate comparable to its growth. The problem: **given a fixed corpus of $U_D$ unique tokens and a compute budget $C$ that would, under Chinchilla-style allocation, call for $D \gg U_D$ tokens, what is the loss-minimizing allocation across (i) parameter count, (ii) epochs over the unique data, (iii) synthetic or model-generated data, (iv) non-web modalities (code, math, transcribed audio, video, scientific archives), and (v) trading data for compute at inference or via RL?**

Three variants, of very different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Does a fitted law $L(N, D, U_D, \text{mix})$ predict held-out loss for repetition counts and synthetic fractions outside the fitted range? Empirically open — runnable, not run at frontier scale.
- **Method.** Is there a curation, deduplication, or generation procedure that converts $U_D$ unique tokens into training signal worth $k U_D$ for $k > 16$? Open; no procedure has a validated $k$.
- **Theory.** Is there an information-theoretic ceiling on how much loss reduction a fixed sample of $U_D$ tokens from distribution $p$ can support, independent of model class and compute? Theoretically open.

Solving it means: a law whose extrapolation to $R_D \sim 10^2$ epochs and synthetic fractions $\ge 50\%$ is validated out-of-sample, plus a compute-allocation rule derived from it.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $p$ be the target text distribution. A corpus $\mathcal{U}$ holds $U_D$ **unique tokens**, measured after a stated deduplication procedure — exact-document hash, MinHash near-dup at Jaccard threshold $\tau$, or suffix-array substring dedup. The value of $U_D$ moves by 2–3× depending on which; it is not a property of the corpus alone.

Training consumes $D$ tokens total, so the repetition count is
$$R_D = \frac{D}{U_D} - 1 .$$
$N$ is non-embedding parameters. Compute is $C \approx 6ND$ FLOPs (forward+backward, dense transformer). Loss $L$ is mean cross-entropy in nats/token on a held-out split **drawn from a corpus disjoint from $\mathcal{U}$ at the document level** — otherwise repetition inflates apparent generalization.

The Chinchilla parametric law (Hoffmann et al., 2022):
$$L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^{\alpha}} + \frac{B}{D^{\beta}}, \quad (A, B, E, \alpha, \beta) = (406.4,\ 410.7,\ 1.69,\ 0.34,\ 0.28).$$

Muennighoff et al. (2023) extend it by replacing $N, D$ with **effective** counts that decay exponentially in repetition:
$$D' = U_D + U_D R_D^{*}\left(1 - e^{-R_D / R_D^{*}}\right), \qquad N' = U_N + U_N R_N^{*}\left(1 - e^{-R_N / R_N^{*}}\right),$$
with fitted $R_D^{*} \approx 15$, $R_N^{*} \approx 5$. Then $L = E + A/N'^{\alpha} + B/D'^{\beta}$.

Synthetic data enters as a mixture $p_\theta$ generated by a model $\theta$ itself trained on $\mathcal{U}$, at fraction $\lambda$:
$$\tilde p = (1-\lambda) \hat p_{\mathcal{U}} + \lambda\, p_\theta .$$

**Assumptions, and which are violated:**

1. *Effective-token decay is exponential.* Assumed, not derived. It forces a hard ceiling $D' \le U_D(1 + R_D^{*}) \approx 16 U_D$. Any law with polynomial decay has no ceiling. The data cannot currently distinguish them at large $R_D$.
2. *$E$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ are invariant to data source.* Violated. Fitted exponents differ across corpora and modalities; Goyal et al. (2024) show the data-quality term is compute-dependent.
3. *Held-out loss is the objective.* Violated in practice — downstream task accuracy and loss decouple, especially for repeated and synthetic data.
4. *Tokens are exchangeable.* Violated: curricula, ordering, and last-phase "annealing" mixes measurably change final loss.
5. *Dense-transformer $C = 6ND$.* Violated by MoE, and by any inference-time-compute regime where capability is bought with test-time FLOPs instead of training tokens.

## 3. State of the Art

**Empirical SOTA (established).** Muennighoff et al., *Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models* (NeurIPS 2023): up to 9B parameters and 900B total tokens, unique budgets from ~100M to ~100B tokens. Repeating data for up to **4 epochs** costs almost nothing relative to fresh tokens; value decays and is effectively nil past ~40 epochs. The fitted $R_D^{*} \approx 15$, $R_N^{*} \approx 5$ is the only widely used data-constrained law. Independently consistent with Xue et al. (NeurIPS 2023) on token-crisis repetition.

**Established, separate line.** Hernandez et al. (2022) show a small repeated fraction of an otherwise-fresh corpus causes a *double-descent-like* degradation with a loss spike at an intermediate repeat fraction — i.e. the harm is non-monotone, not a smooth discount.

**Claimed but unablated.** That synthetic data extends the frontier. Phi-series "textbook-quality" results (Gunasekar et al., 2023) and large synthetic corpora (Cosmopedia, Nemotron-CC) report strong benchmark numbers, but the comparison is typically against a *different* web baseline at a *different* token count, with no matched-compute, matched-unique-source arm. The benchmark gains are real numbers; the causal claim "synthetic tokens are worth $\ge$ web tokens at equal compute" is not ablated at frontier scale, and contamination from the generator's training set is rarely bounded.

**Theory SOTA.** Dohmatob et al. (ICML 2024) prove that recursive training on model output changes the *scaling exponent* — a finite-data law becomes a plateau — under Gaussian/linear-regression settings. Gerstgrasser et al. (COLM 2024) prove that *accumulating* real and synthetic data (rather than replacing) bounds test error, avoiding collapse. Both are model-class-restricted; neither predicts a transformer's loss curve.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Repetition budget.** At $\le 4$ epochs, loss is within ~0.1% of fresh-data training; measured up to 9B params / 900B tokens (Muennighoff et al., 2023).
- **Repetition ceiling.** Past ~40 epochs, additional passes gave no measurable loss improvement at that scale; excess compute was better spent elsewhere.
- **Optimal $N$ shrinks under data constraint.** With $D$ fixed by repetition, allocating extra compute to parameters saturates faster ($R_N^{*} \approx 5 < R_D^{*} \approx 15$).
- **Supply.** Villalobos et al. (ICML 2024) estimate the effective stock of public human-generated text at $\sim 3 \times 10^{14}$ tokens, with full utilization by frontier runs between 2026 and 2032 under trend extrapolation.
- **Pruning beats power laws — in vision.** Sorscher et al. (NeurIPS 2022) show that with a good difficulty ranking, error can fall exponentially rather than polynomially in dataset size (ImageNet/CIFAR scale). Not replicated for language pretraining loss.
- **Curation is compute-dependent.** Goyal et al. (CVPR 2024): the aggressiveness of the optimal filter decreases as repetitions increase — an aggressively filtered small set is optimal at low compute and harmful at high compute.
- **Knowledge capacity.** Allen-Zhu & Li (2024) measure ~2 bits of stored factual knowledge per parameter across scales in a synthetic-biography setting — a distinct, tighter ceiling than loss.
- **Collapse is a replacement phenomenon.** Shumailov et al. (Nature, 2024) show degeneration under full generation-over-generation replacement; accumulation avoids it (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024).

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Empirically open.** Whether $R_D^{*} \approx 15$ holds at $N \ge 70$B and $U_D \ge 10$T. The law was fit two orders of magnitude below the frontier; scaling-law constants are known to drift with fitting range.
- **Empirically open.** Whether a matched-compute, matched-source synthetic arm beats a repetition arm. Nobody has published $L(\lambda)$ at fixed $C$, fixed $U_D$, and disjoint eval.
- **Methodologically blocked.** "Unique token" has no source-independent definition. The dedup threshold that defines $U_D$ is a free parameter, so $R_D$ — and therefore every fitted constant — is not a measured quantity but a convention.
- **Methodologically blocked.** Multimodal token-equivalence: how many video or audio tokens substitute for one text token for text-loss purposes. No accepted exchange rate exists.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether effective-data decay is exponential (finite ceiling $16 U_D$) or polynomial (no ceiling). These differ by orders of magnitude in the regime that matters and are indistinguishable in current fits.
- **Theoretically open.** A lower bound on achievable loss given $U_D$ samples, over all learners, for a realistic language distribution.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

Three named obstructions.

1. **Non-identifiability of the tail.** Exponential and polynomial decay in $R_D$ fit existing data comparably in $0 \le R_D \le 40$ but diverge by $\ge 5\times$ in effective tokens at $R_D = 200$. Distinguishing them requires runs at $R_D$ where the loss differences are smaller than seed variance, so the discriminating experiment is the expensive one.

2. **Confounded measurement of synthetic data.** A generator trained on $\mathcal{U}$ produces text whose relationship to the eval set cannot be bounded by string matching. Reported synthetic-data gains conflate three effects — genuine augmentation, distillation from a stronger generator, and eval contamination — and no published protocol separates them at scale.

3. **Compute cost of the decisive arm.** The question is only interesting where $D/U_D \gg 1$ at frontier $N$. A 4-arm ablation at $N = 70$B, $D = 2$T is $\sim 8 \times 10^{23}$ FLOPs per arm — thousands of H100-months. Groups with that compute spend it on products, not on ablations they cannot publish.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Data-centric benchmarks.** DataComp-LM (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024 D&B) fixes compute and varies the corpus, making curation claims comparable. Extending it to the repetition axis is the natural next step *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Synthetic-data pipelines at scale.** NVIDIA (Nemotron-CC), HuggingFace (Cosmopedia/FineWeb), Microsoft (Phi). Rephrasing web text rather than free generation is the dominant safe variant.
- **Collapse theory.** Stanford/Constantine/Meta lines (Gerstgrasser, Dohmatob, Feng) — accumulation vs replacement, and collapse as an exponent change.
- **Token-value weighting.** Selective loss on high-value tokens (Rho-1-style) as a way to raise $k$ without new data.
- **RL and inference compute as data substitutes.** The claim that verifier-scored self-generated trajectories break the data constraint in verifiable domains (math, code) is now the main frontier argument *(frontier — verify)*; there is no published scaling law relating verifier-filtered synthetic tokens to $U_D$.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question:** is repetition or matched-compute synthetic augmentation the better use of a fixed unique corpus, and does $R_D^{*} \approx 15$ hold one order of magnitude up?

**Scale.** $N = 7$B dense transformer, $U_D = 40$B unique tokens (a fixed, MinHash-deduped FineWeb-Edu slice), $D = 800$B tokens per arm ($R_D = 19$), $C \approx 3.4 \times 10^{22}$ FLOPs per arm. Four arms, 3 seeds each: (A) **control** — 800B *fresh* tokens from the same source, never repeated; (B) repetition — 20 epochs over the 40B; (C) 40B unique + 760B rephrased-synthetic from a 7B generator trained only on $\mathcal{U}$; (D) 40B unique repeated 4× + 640B synthetic (accumulate, not replace).

**Eval.** Held-out loss on a temporally disjoint corpus (documents dated after $\mathcal{U}$'s cutoff), so neither repetition nor the generator can have seen it.

**The deciding number.** $\Delta = L_B - L_C$ in nats/token, against a seed-noise band. The law predicts $L_B - L_A \approx 0.013$ nats. If $|\Delta| < 0.005$ (within noise), synthetic augmentation buys nothing over plain repetition and the frontier is set by $U_D$ alone. If $\Delta > 0.02$, synthetic data raises the effective-token multiplier and the exponential ceiling at $16U_D$ is false. Secondary: refit $R_D^{*}$ on arm B alone; a value outside $[12, 18]$ falsifies the transfer of the published constant.

## 10. Worked Example

Fix $U_D = 100$B unique tokens, $N = 8$B parameters, and a compute budget permitting $D = 1$T tokens — ten passes, $R_D = 9$.

Effective tokens:
$$D' = 100\text{B} + 100\text{B}\times 15 \times \left(1 - e^{-9/15}\right) = 100\text{B} + 1500\text{B}\times 0.4512 = 777\text{B}.$$
So 1T repeated tokens are worth 777B fresh ones — a 22% discount.

Loss, with $N' \approx N$ (no parameter repetition penalty here):
- Repeated: $L = 1.69 + 406.4/(8\times10^9)^{0.34} + 410.7/(7.77\times10^{11})^{0.28} = 1.69 + 0.1746 + 0.1924 = 2.057$.
- Fresh 1T: $L = 1.69 + 0.1746 + 410.7/(10^{12})^{0.28} = 1.69 + 0.1746 + 0.1793 = 2.044$.

**The penalty for ten epochs is 0.013 nats/token.** That is the finding — and the obstruction. 0.013 nats is smaller than the loss gap between two reasonable web corpora, smaller than the effect of a tokenizer change, and comparable to seed-to-seed variation in a single 8B run. Any experiment in this regime measures a quantity swamped by nuisance factors.

Now push to the regime that matters: $R_D = 199$ (200 passes over 100B tokens, $D = 20$T). The exponential form gives $D' \to U_D(1+R_D^*) = 1.6$T — a hard ceiling, so $L \ge 1.69 + 0.1746 + 410.7/(1.6\times10^{12})^{0.28} = 2.030$, no matter how much compute is added. A polynomial-decay law fit to the same $R_D \le 40$ data instead predicts $D'$ continuing to grow past 4T, worth roughly another 0.02 nats. **Both forms fit the published measurements; they disagree about whether the frontier has a floor.** The experiment that separates them lives at 200 epochs and frontier $N$, where the signal is ~0.02 nats and the cost is $\sim10^{24}$ FLOPs. That gap — decisive signal below noise floor, at a compute cost only frontier labs can pay — is why the problem is open.
