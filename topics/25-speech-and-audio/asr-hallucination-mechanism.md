# Hallucination in End-to-End Speech Recognition

> **Topic:** Speech & Audio · **ID:** `25-speech-and-audio/asr-hallucination-mechanism` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

An attention-based encoder-decoder ASR model sometimes emits fluent, well-formed text that has no acoustic support in the input: a phrase spoken by nobody. This is different from a substitution error. A substitution is a wrong guess at a real acoustic event; a hallucination is content generated by the decoder's language prior in the absence of, or in contradiction to, evidence in the audio.

Three variants, of very different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Given audio $x$, a reference $y^\*$, and a hypothesis $\hat y$, decide which output tokens are *unsupported* rather than *wrong*. Word error rate cannot make this call — it counts an inserted sentence and a mis-heard word on the same scale, and is undefined when the reference is empty.
- **Method.** Reduce the rate of unsupported output at fixed WER on clean speech. Anything that just makes the decoder timid trades hallucination for deletion; the claim only counts if the clean-speech operating point is held.
- **Theory (mechanism).** Explain *why* the failure appears in autoregressive attention decoders and not, at comparable rates, in CTC or in frame-synchronous transducers. Candidate: the decoder's implicit LM $p(y)$ dominates when the attention distribution carries little information, so decoding degenerates to sampling from the prior.

Solving it means: a measure of acoustic support that is validated against human judgement, plus a decoder whose unsupported-token rate on non-speech and degraded input drops by an order of magnitude with no clean-speech WER regression.

## 2. Formal Setting

Audio $x \in \mathbb{R}^{T}$, token sequence $y = (y_1,\dots,y_L)$ over vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$. An attention encoder-decoder factorizes

$$p_\theta(y \mid x) = \prod_{l=1}^{L} p_\theta(y_l \mid y_{<l}, \mathrm{Enc}_\theta(x)).$$

**Acoustic support.** Define the per-token support as the log-odds the audio contributes, measured by ablating the encoder:

$$s_l(x) = \log p_\theta(y_l \mid y_{<l}, \mathrm{Enc}_\theta(x)) - \log p_\theta(y_l \mid y_{<l}, \mathrm{Enc}_\theta(\varnothing)),$$

with $\varnothing$ a fixed reference input (digital silence, or shuffled encoder states). Measured directly: two forward passes per hypothesis, no retraining. A token is *unsupported* at threshold $\tau$ if $s_l < \tau$; the **hallucination rate** is $H_\tau = \mathbb{E}_x[\,|\{l : s_l < \tau\}| \,/\, L\,]$. Note $\mathrm{Enc}_\theta(\varnothing)$ is itself off-distribution, so $s_l$ is a biased estimate of causal contribution — assumption known to be violated.

**Utterance-level predicate.** With a semantic similarity $\mathrm{sim}(\cdot,\cdot)$ (e.g. sentence-embedding cosine), an utterance is hallucinated if $\mathrm{WER}(\hat y, y^\*)$ is large *and* $\hat y$ is fluent under an external LM: $\mathrm{PPL}_{\text{ext}}(\hat y) < \mathrm{PPL}_{\text{ext}}(y^\*)$ while $\mathrm{sim}(\hat y, y^\*) < \kappa$. This is the operational definition used in the perturbation literature; $\kappa$ is unprincipled and set by hand.

**The metric gap.** $\mathrm{WER} = (S+D+I)/N$ with $N = |y^\*|$. For non-speech input $N = 0$ and WER is undefined; every practical harness either drops such segments or reports $\infty$. Hallucination is therefore *structurally invisible* to the field's primary metric exactly where it is most severe.

Assumptions violated in practice: (i) segment references exist (long-form audio is chunked, and Whisper-style decoding conditions on its own previous text, so $y_{<l}$ spans chunk boundaries); (ii) teacher forcing at training matches free-running decoding (exposure bias); (iii) the training corpus is transcript-faithful — Whisper's 680k hours of weakly-supervised web audio contains subtitle boilerplate ("Thanks for watching", channel sign-offs) paired with non-speech, which makes some hallucinations *correct* generalization from the training distribution.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established.**
- Whisper (Radford et al., ICML 2023) is the reference system and the reference failure case. Its own paper documents long-form repetition loops and ships heuristics against them: temperature fallback, a gzip compression-ratio threshold of 2.4, an average-logprob threshold of $-1.0$, and a `no_speech` probability threshold of 0.6. These are engineering guards, published with the model, not ablated as hallucination interventions.
- Koenecke et al. (ACM FAccT 2024, *Careless Whisper*) is the only careful prevalence audit: hallucinated phrases in roughly 1% of transcribed segments of aphasic speech (AphasiaBank), with a large minority containing invented harmful content — violence, fabricated names, spurious medical claims. Rates were higher for speakers with longer non-vocal pauses, tying hallucination to silence rather than to noise.
- Frieske & Shi (ACL 2024) show with input perturbations that models with near-identical WER differ substantially in hallucination propensity — the key result that WER does not rank systems on this axis.

**Claimed but unablated.** Distillation and data-filtering papers (e.g. Distil-Whisper, 2023) report reduced repetition/hallucination as a side effect; the reduction is a benchmark number on a chosen test set, not a controlled ablation holding clean WER fixed. Vendor claims that a newer checkpoint "hallucinates less" are benchmark numbers only.

**Theory SOTA.** There is none specific to ASR. The nearest formal handle is the decomposition of implicit-LM effects in seq2seq decoding (Chorowski & Jaitly, Interspeech 2017; internal-LM subtraction in RNN-T literature), which explains *that* a prior term exists, not *when* it wins.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Architecture matters.** CTC (Graves et al., ICML 2006) assumes conditional independence across frames given the encoder and has no autoregressive text prior; it produces garbage or empty output on non-speech, not fluent sentences. Attention decoders produce fluent sentences. This contrast is reproduced across many systems and is the strongest mechanistic clue available.
- **Silence and non-speech are the trigger.** Whisper on music, breathing, or long pauses emits subtitle boilerplate. Consistent with Koenecke et al.'s pause finding.
- **Prevalence, at scale.** ~1% of segments in the FAccT 2024 audit (order $10^4$ segments, disordered speech). No comparable audit exists for read speech; anecdotally the rate there is far lower.
- **Confidence does not separate.** Hallucinated spans are frequently high-likelihood under the model — that is what makes them fluent — so logprob thresholds catch loops (low-entropy repetition inflates the compression ratio) but not confident fabrications.
- **Long-form conditioning propagates errors.** Whisper's default decoder feeds the previous chunk's text as prompt; once a hallucinated sentence enters the context, subsequent chunks are more likely to continue it. Turning off the prompt reduces this, at a cost in contextual accuracy.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** No result specifies conditions under which $p_\theta(y_l\mid y_{<l},\mathrm{Enc}(x))$ collapses to the implicit prior. There is no theorem bounding hallucination rate as a function of encoder mutual information $I(\mathrm{Enc}(x); y)$, decoder capacity, or training-label noise rate. Whether hallucination is eliminable at fixed clean WER, or is a necessary cost of the language prior that buys the WER, is unproven either way.
- **Empirically open.** Whether removing subtitle boilerplate from a Whisper-scale (680k h) training corpus reduces the hallucination rate is runnable — it needs one pretraining run at scale — and unrun publicly.
- **Methodologically blocked.** "Acoustic support" has no validated measure. $s_l(x)$ depends on an arbitrary $\varnothing$; the utterance-level predicate depends on a hand-set $\kappa$; no dataset carries human token-level *unsupported* labels. Until a support measure is validated against human annotation, every mitigation claim is scored on a metric that does not measure what it names.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

The named obstruction is **an evaluation that does not measure the thing it names**, compounded by **absent ground truth**. WER's denominator is reference length, so the highest-severity case — fluent text over non-speech — is either undefined or silently dropped by every standard harness. Where references do exist, a hallucinated sentence contributes insertions that are diluted by corpus size: 184 hallucinated segments at 10 inserted words each in a 150k-word corpus move WER by ~1.2 points, indistinguishable from ordinary noise sensitivity. So a system can be made worse on this axis while looking better on the leaderboard.

Secondary and real: **non-identifiability of mechanism**. Training-data boilerplate, exposure bias, and prior dominance predict the same observable output. Separating them needs interventions at pretraining scale, which is the compute cost — a Whisper-large-class run is on the order of $10^3$ GPU-days, so the decisive ablation is out of reach for most groups.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Perturbation-based diagnostics** — the Frieske & Shi line: rank models by response to controlled input corruption rather than by WER.
- **Fairness/harm audits** of ASR on disordered and accented speech (Koenecke and collaborators, Cornell/Stanford), which surfaced the problem in its current form.
- **Open reproductions** — OWSM (CMU/ESPnet, Peng et al.) provides Whisper-style models with fully open training data, which is the only route to a controlled data-provenance ablation without proprietary corpora.
- **Speech-LLM decoders** (audio encoder + text LLM). *(frontier — verify)* Anecdotal reports suggest these hallucinate more, not less, since the prior is a full LLM; no systematic measurement published.
- **Internal-LM estimation and subtraction**, carried over from RNN-T fusion work, as a decode-time correction. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question:** is confident hallucination separable from correct output by any decoder-internal signal?

**Scale.** 5,000 audio segments, no pretraining required, ~50 GPU-hours on one A100. Four strata of 1,250: (a) digital silence and room tone; (b) non-speech audio (music, traffic, typing); (c) speech at SNR 0 dB; (d) clean read speech from LibriSpeech `test-clean` as the control arm. Models: Whisper large-v3 (attention) and a CTC model of matched WER on stratum (d).

**Procedure.** Decode with default settings and all Whisper guards disabled. Compute $s_l(x)$ per token with $\varnothing$ = digital silence. Have three annotators label each output span as *supported* / *unsupported*, on the audio, blind to model.

**Deciding number.** The AUROC of $s_l$ against the human *unsupported* label, restricted to tokens whose model probability exceeds 0.9. If AUROC $\ge 0.85$, hallucination is decoder-detectable and the problem moves from methodologically blocked to an engineering problem: ship $s_l$ as a filter. If AUROC $\le 0.65$, no decoder-internal signal separates fabrication from transcription, and mitigation must move into training data or architecture. The CTC arm gives the mechanism check: if its stratum-(a)/(b) unsupported-token rate is below one tenth of Whisper's at equal clean-speech WER, the autoregressive prior is confirmed as the locus.

## 10. Worked Example

Input: 30 seconds of room tone recorded in an empty office, 42 dB SPL, no speech. Reference transcript: the empty string.

Whisper large-v3, greedy, guards off, emits: `Thank you for watching!` — five tokens.

The guard values on that segment, as the model reports them:

```
no_speech_prob      = 0.31    (threshold 0.6  → passes, not flagged)
avg_logprob         = -0.42   (threshold -1.0 → passes, not flagged)
compression_ratio   = 1.09    (threshold 2.4  → passes, not flagged)
mean token p(y_l)   = 0.72
```

All three shipped heuristics pass. The model is *confident*. Support scores $s_l$ against a silence reference are near zero for every token, because the reference input is itself silence — the ablation control collapses onto the test condition and the measure returns no signal.

Now score it. WER $=(S+D+I)/N$ with $N=0$: undefined, five insertions over zero reference words. The standard harness drops the segment. Substitute a scenario where the segment does contain 8 spoken words and the same phrase is appended: $I=5$, $N=8$, contribution 0.63 WER for that utterance — but across a 150k-word corpus with 184 such segments (the ~1% prevalence from the FAccT audit), total impact is about 1.2 WER points, inside the spread between two random seeds.

The obstruction is visible in three places at once: the confidence signal points the wrong way, the ablation reference is degenerate, and the metric either divides by zero or dilutes the effect below noise. None of the three is fixed by a better model.
