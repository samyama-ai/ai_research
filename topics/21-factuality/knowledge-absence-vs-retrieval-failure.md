# Separating Knowledge Absence from Retrieval Failure in Parametric Memory

> **Topic:** Hallucination & Factuality · **ID:** `21-factuality/knowledge-absence-vs-retrieval-failure` · **Status:** methodologically-blocked

## 1. Problem Statement

A model answers a factual question wrongly. Two causes are conflated:

- **Absence** — the fact is not encoded in $\theta$ at all. No procedure short of adding information could recover it.
- **Retrieval failure** — the fact is encoded, but the query as posed does not route to it. Another phrasing, another direction, or a different decoding path would return it.

**Input:** a model $\theta$, a fact $f$, and evidence that greedy decoding on a natural query fails.
**Output:** a label in $\{\text{absent}, \text{unretrieved}\}$, or a calibrated probability.
**Solved** means: a procedure that, on a corpus where presence is known by construction, separates the two classes with AUROC $> 0.9$ *and* whose verdict does not move when you vary the amount of answer information the procedure is allowed to use.

Three variants, different difficulty:

- **Measurement.** Define "the fact is in the weights" without reference to a particular elicitation method. This is the blocked variant.
- **Method.** Given a definition, build a cheap detector. Tractable; several partial detectors exist (§3).
- **Theory.** Prove that absence is or is not identifiable from $\theta$ under a stated leakage budget. Untouched.

The decision matters operationally: absence calls for retrieval augmentation or abstention; retrieval failure calls for better prompting, decoding, or elicitation — and abstaining is the wrong action.

## 2. Formal Setting

Fact $f = (s, r, o)$ with subject $s$, relation $r$, object $o \in \mathcal{O}$. Model $p_\theta$ over token sequences.

**Query family.** $Q(f)$ is a set of prompts that a competent human judges as asking for $o$ given $(s,r)$. Measured as: prompts generated by paraphrase templates (ParaRel-style) plus mined patterns, filtered by human agreement $\ge 4/5$. *Assumption known violated:* $Q(f)$ is treated as exchangeable, but per-prompt accuracy varies by tens of points on semantically identical prompts (Elazar et al., TACL 2021).

**Elicitation procedure.** $a: \theta \times (s,r) \to \mathcal{O}$, drawn from a class $\mathcal{A}$: greedy decode, best-of-$Q$, few-shot, chain-of-thought, activation steering, linear probe on hidden states, LoRA fine-tune on *other* facts of relation $r$.

**Leakage budget.** The critical quantity. Let $b(a)$ be the number of bits about $o$ that $a$ supplies, measured as
$$b(a) \;=\; \log_2 |\mathcal{O}| \;-\; \log_2 |\mathcal{O}_a(s,r)|,$$
where $\mathcal{O}_a(s,r)$ is the candidate set $a$ scores. Greedy open-ended decode: $b=0$. Four-way multiple choice over $|\mathcal{O}| = 2^{20}$: $b = 18$. A probe trained on labelled $(s,r,o)$ triples of the same relation: $b$ is not directly countable — this is where the definition breaks.

**Presence at budget.**
$$K_b(\theta, f) \;=\; \mathbb{1}\!\left[\ \exists\, a \in \mathcal{A},\ b(a) \le b \ :\ \Pr_{q \sim Q(f)}\big[a(\theta, q) = o\big] \ \ge\ \tau \ \right], \qquad \tau \approx 0.5 .$$

**Retrieval failure at budget $b$:** $K_b(\theta,f) = 1$ and greedy decode fails.
**Absence at budget $b$:** $K_b(\theta,f) = 0$.

**The degeneracy.** $K_b$ is monotone non-decreasing in $b$, and $K_{\log_2|\mathcal{O}|} \equiv 1$ for every fact, in every model, including facts that provably were never in the training corpus — supply the answer as one of two options and a coin flip clears $\tau$ half the time. So "absence" is only defined relative to a budget, and the field has no agreed $b$, no agreed $\mathcal{A}$, and no way to price $b$ for gradient-based or probe-based elicitors.

**Selectivity control.** The probe analogue of leakage accounting is Hewitt & Liang's control task (EMNLP 2019): re-run the elicitor against randomly permuted objects; selectivity is (real accuracy $-$ control accuracy). A probe with high control accuracy is reading its own training set, not $\theta$. Almost no knowledge-elicitation paper reports this.

## 3. State of the Art

**Established.**

- Prompt sensitivity is real and large. LPAQA (Jiang et al., TACL 2020) raised LAMA T-REx top-1 from 31.1% to 39.6% on BERT-large by ensembling mined and paraphrased prompts — an 8.5-point gain from phrasing alone, with $\theta$ fixed. That gap is retrieval failure by construction.
- Storage and extractability dissociate. Allen-Zhu & Li, *Physics of Language Models: Part 3.1* (ICML 2024), train on synthetic biographies: without data augmentation the model memorizes the corpus near-perfectly yet answers out-of-format extraction questions at near-chance; with entity-augmented rewrites, accuracy jumps to near-ceiling. Same information, different retrievability.
- The reversal curse. Berglund et al. (ICLR 2024): a model fine-tuned on "A is B" answers the forward query at high accuracy and the reverse at chance, with *no* elevated likelihood on the correct token. Directional retrieval failure that looks exactly like absence at $b=0$.
- Localization does not predict editability. Hase et al. (NeurIPS 2023): causal-tracing localization and successful ROME-style edit sites are largely uncorrelated. Where a fact "is" and where you can reach it come apart.

**Claimed but unablated.**

- Self-knowledge probes. Kadavath et al. (2022) show P(IK) is calibrated and transfers; Ferrando et al. (ICLR 2025) find entity-recognition directions in Gemma 2 that predict refusal. Neither controls leakage: both train on labelled known/unknown splits, so a probe can learn entity frequency rather than parametric content.
- Semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., *Nature* 2024) detects confabulation well but is explicitly a *consistency* signal; it cannot distinguish confidently-absent from confidently-wrong.

**Benchmark-number-only.** PopQA (Mallen et al., ACL 2023) and long-tail QA sets report accuracy stratified by entity popularity. Popularity is a proxy for corpus frequency, not a presence label. No public benchmark ships ground-truth presence.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Frequency predicts accuracy log-linearly.** Kandpal et al. (ICML 2023): QA accuracy on TriviaQA/NQ rises roughly linearly in $\log$(number of pretraining documents containing the entity pair), measured across models from 4.8B to 176B (BLOOM family). Their extrapolation: matching a retrieval-augmented model on the long tail would need on the order of $10^{13}$ parameters.
- **New facts are learned slowly and cost calibration.** Gekhman et al. (EMNLP 2024), PaLM-2-scale fine-tuning: examples the model did not already know are fitted much later in training, and as they are fitted, hallucination rate on *held-out known* facts rises roughly linearly.
- **Consistency across paraphrases is low.** ParaRel (Elazar et al., TACL 2021): BERT-scale models agree with themselves across paraphrased cloze prompts roughly 50–60% of the time on average, varying widely by relation.
- **Hallucination is not eliminable by calibration alone.** Kalai & Vempala (STOC 2024): a calibrated model must hallucinate at a rate lower-bounded by the fraction of facts appearing exactly once in training ("monofacts"). This lower-bounds the *absence* class from below but says nothing about identifying members of it.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Methodologically blocked (the core gap).** There is no definition of "the fact is in $\theta$" that is invariant to the elicitation class. Every operational definition in use fixes $\mathcal{A}$ implicitly and reports $K$ as if it were a property of $\theta$. Nobody prices $b(a)$ for probes or fine-tuning elicitors, so results across papers are not comparable and monotonicity in $b$ is unreported.
- **Theoretically open.** Whether absence is identifiable at all: is there a budget $b^\star > 0$ and a class $\mathcal{A}$ such that $K_{b^\star}$ is stable — i.e. $K_{b^\star} = K_{b^\star + \epsilon}$ for facts not in the corpus? No proof either way. Related: no lower bound on the sample complexity of certifying absence.
- **Empirically open.** On a *synthetic* corpus with known presence, the experiment is entirely runnable at 1B scale for well under $10^4$ GPU-hours, and has not been run with leakage accounting. Also open: what fraction of frontier-model factual errors are retrieval failures rather than absences. Current guesses range from "most" to "few" with no measurement behind either.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

**Non-identifiability under an unbounded quantifier.** Presence is defined by $\exists a$. As $\mathcal{A}$ grows, $K$ rises to 1 for all facts; as it shrinks to greedy decoding, $K$ collapses onto the very behaviour you were trying to explain. The quantity is only meaningful at an intermediate budget, and there is no principled way to set it — the leakage measure $b(a)$ is well defined for candidate-set scoring and undefined for gradient-based elicitation, which is what the strongest elicitors use.

**Absent ground truth at scale.** Presence labels require knowing the pretraining corpus. For frontier models the corpus is closed; for open ones, deduplicated substring search over trillions of tokens is expensive and still only bounds *surface* presence, not entailed presence. A fact never stated but derivable from two stated facts has no label.

**Confounded proxies.** Corpus frequency, entity popularity, and prompt-format familiarity all correlate with both classes. A detector that scores 0.85 AUROC may be reading frequency, which is upstream of both presence and retrievability.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Mechanistic recall circuits.** Geva et al. (EMNLP 2023) trace subject-enrichment then attribute-extraction as separable stages; the natural follow-on is to test whether enrichment succeeds while extraction fails on retrieval-failure cases. Groups: Technion/Google, EleutherAI interpretability.
- **Latent-knowledge probing without labels.** CCS (Burns et al., ICLR 2023) and successors aim at label-free elicitation, which would cap leakage by construction. Reproductions report that CCS often tracks the most salient feature rather than truth *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Knowledge awareness directions.** Ferrando et al. (ICLR 2025) on Gemma 2; extensions to multi-hop and long-tail entities are active *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Training-data attribution as a presence oracle.** Influence-function and $n$-gram-index work (OLMo/Dolma-style open corpora) makes corpus-grounded presence labels feasible for the first time at 7B scale.
- **Zero-generation knowledge estimation.** Gottesman & Geva (EMNLP 2024) estimate subject knowledgeability from hidden states of the subject alone — the closest existing thing to a $b=0$ presence estimator.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Design: leakage-controlled elicitation ladder on a synthetic-injection corpus.**

- **Scale.** Pretrain a 1.4B-parameter decoder on 30B tokens of open web text into which 20,000 synthetic person–attribute facts are injected. Cross the injection with frequency $n \in \{0, 1, 4, 16, 64\}$ documents and phrasing diversity $d \in \{1, 5\}$ templates, 2,000 facts per cell. Presence is ground truth: $n = 0$ facts are provably absent. Roughly 3,000 H100-hours.
- **Ladder.** Evaluate each fact under elicitors at increasing leakage: $b = 0$ (greedy open-ended), $b = 0$ (best-of-20 paraphrases, still open-ended), $b \approx 18$ (4-way MC over a $2^{20}$ object vocabulary), $b \approx 19$ (2-way), and a linear probe on layer-$\ell$ residuals trained on *disjoint* facts.
- **Control arms.** (i) The $n = 0$ cell — true absence, must stay at chance. (ii) Hewitt–Liang control task: re-run every elicitor against randomly permuted objects and report selectivity, not raw accuracy. (iii) A frequency-only baseline: logistic regression on $\log n$ alone, to show the detector beats the confound.
- **Deciding number.** AUROC of each elicitor at separating $\{n \ge 1,\ \text{greedy fails}\}$ (retrieval failure) from $\{n = 0\}$ (absence), reported as a function of $b$. **The problem moves if some elicitor reaches AUROC $\ge 0.90$ and the AUROC curve is flat in $b$ — $|\text{AUROC}(b) - \text{AUROC}(b')| \le 0.05$ across the ladder.** If AUROC instead climbs monotonically with $b$ and never plateaus, presence is confirmed to be a property of the elicitor, not of $\theta$, and the measurement variant is formally closed as ill-posed.

## 10. Worked Example

Take the reversal-curse setup (Berglund et al., ICLR 2024). Fine-tune on 30 statements of the form "Daphne Barrington is the director of *A Journey Through Time*." Then query in both directions.

| Query direction | $b$ | Reported behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| "Who is Daphne Barrington?" | 0 | high accuracy, near-ceiling |
| "Who directed *A Journey Through Time*?" | 0 | ~0%, at chance; correct-name likelihood **not** elevated over a random name |

At $b = 0$ the reverse fact is indistinguishable from a fact that was never trained on. Both give 0% accuracy and flat likelihood. So the $b = 0$ detector labels it **absent**.

Now climb one rung. Ask the same reverse question as a 4-way multiple choice with the correct name and three same-type distractors: $b = \log_2 |\mathcal{O}| - \log_2 4$, about 18 bits if names are drawn from a $2^{20}$ pool. Chance is 25%.

*(The MC arm is the arithmetic of the proposed experiment, not a number Berglund et al. report — it is expected, not measured.)* Suppose it comes back at 55%. Then:

$$K_{18} = 1, \qquad K_0 = 0 .$$

The same fact, the same weights, two opposite labels. And the never-trained control fact would also lift above 25% under MC — surface plausibility and name-frequency priors are worth several points — so you cannot read 55% as evidence of presence without the $n=0$ arm. Say the control lands at 34%. The presence signal is $55 - 34 = 21$ points at $b=18$ and $0$ points at $b=0$.

The obstruction is exactly this: the answer to "is the fact in the weights?" is $21 \cdot \mathbb{1}[b \ge 18]$ points of signal — a function of how much of the answer you were willing to hand the model, not a property of $\theta$. Until $b$ is priced for probes and fine-tuning elicitors, every published "the model knows but does not say" claim is reporting its own elicitation budget.
