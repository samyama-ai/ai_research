# Provable Unlearning of Hazardous Capabilities

> **Topic:** Safety & Robustness · **ID:** `22-safety-robustness/provable-unlearning-hazardous-capabilities` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement

Given a trained model with weights $\theta$ that can perform a hazardous task $\mathcal{H}$ (e.g. synthesis routes for select agents, exploit development), produce weights $\theta'$ such that (a) $\theta'$ retains general capability, and (b) *no* adversary with white-box access and a stated budget can recover $\mathcal{H}$-performance. The output is $\theta'$ plus a certificate — an argument, not a benchmark score, that (b) holds.

Three variants, routinely conflated:

- **Measurement.** Define $\mathrm{Haz}(\theta')$ so that it is a property of the weights, not of the prompt used to probe them. Currently the dominant failure mode: a low benchmark number after unlearning is evidence about the elicitation method, not about the model.
- **Method.** Find an editing procedure whose effect survives fine-tuning, pruning, activation steering, and quantization. Empirically open.
- **Theory.** Prove a lower bound on the compute any adversary needs to restore $\mathcal{H}$-performance from $\theta'$. No such theorem exists for any non-convex model.

Solving it means: a procedure $U$ and a theorem of the form "for all $E$ in adversary class $\mathcal{E}$ with budget $B$, $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathcal{H}}(E(U(\theta))) \le \tau$", with $\mathcal{E}$ broad enough to include gradient-based fine-tuning on $\le B$ tokens.

## 2. Formal Setting

Let $A$ be a training algorithm, $D$ a dataset, $\theta = A(D)$. Let $F \subseteq D$ be the forget set and $\mathcal{H}$ a hazard task distribution with scorer $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathcal{H}}(\cdot) \in [0,1]$ and chance level $\tau_0$ (0.25 on four-way multiple choice).

**Distributional (Guo et al. 2020) unlearning.** $U$ is $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-unlearning if for all measurable $S$ in parameter space,
$$\Pr[U(\theta, D, F) \in S] \le e^{\varepsilon}\Pr[A(D\setminus F)\in S] + \delta .$$
*Measured as:* not measurable. Estimating this requires sampling $A(D\setminus F)$, i.e. a retraining run. At $10^{25}$ FLOP pretraining scale that is one number per $\sim\!10^7$ GPU-hours, so in practice it is asserted from the algorithm's analysis, never estimated.

**Capability-level unlearning.** Fix an adversary class $\mathcal{E}_B$: all procedures using $\le B$ tokens of fine-tuning data, $\le C$ FLOP, white-box $\theta'$. Define
$$\mathrm{Haz}_B(\theta') \;=\; \sup_{E \in \mathcal{E}_B} \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathcal{H}}\big(E(\theta')\big).$$
*Measured as:* a maximum over the finite set of attacks someone actually ran — an underestimate of a supremum, with no bound on the gap. This is the central measurement defect.

**Utility.** $\mathrm{Ret}(\theta') = \mathrm{Acc}_{\mathcal{R}}(\theta')$ on a retain benchmark (MMLU, MT-Bench). Target: $\mathrm{Ret}(\theta) - \mathrm{Ret}(\theta') \le 1$ point.

**Deletion capacity** (Sekhari et al. 2021): the largest $|F|$ for which $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-unlearning keeps excess empirical risk at the retrain level. For convex losses, $m^\star = \tilde\Omega\!\left(n\varepsilon/\sqrt{d}\right)$, $n$ = training set size, $d$ = dimension.

**Assumptions, and which are violated.**

| Assumption | Status |
|---|---|
| Hazard is generated by an identifiable $F \subseteq D$ | **Violated.** Bioweapons capability composes from benign virology, chemistry and reasoning; there is no forget set whose removal is both sufficient and utility-preserving. |
| Loss is convex / strongly convex | **Violated.** All certified-removal proofs (Guo, Neel, Sekhari) require convexity or a convex final layer. |
| $D$ is known | **Violated** for most open-weight models. |
| $\mathcal{E}_B$ is enumerable | **Violated.** $\sup$ is estimated by a handful of attacks. |
| Retraining reference is computable | **Violated** above ~1B params for realistic budgets. |

## 3. State of the Art

**Theory SOTA (established).** Certified removal for convex objectives: Guo et al., *Certified Data Removal from Machine Learning Models* (ICML 2020) — a Newton step plus loss perturbation gives $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-removal with gradient-residual norm bounding the certificate. Neel et al., *Descent-to-Delete* (ALT 2021) — $\tilde O(1)$ gradient steps per deletion for strongly convex losses with published $\varepsilon$–runtime tradeoffs. Sekhari et al. (NeurIPS 2021) — deletion capacity separating unlearning from differential privacy. Bourtoule et al., *Machine Unlearning* (IEEE S&P 2021) — SISA gives exact unlearning by sharding, at the cost of retraining a shard and of degraded accuracy from ensembling. **None of these apply to a transformer trained with SGD on unknown data.**

**Systems/empirical SOTA (benchmark numbers only).** RMU (Li et al., *The WMDP Benchmark*, ICML 2024) steers hazardous-topic activations to a random direction. Zephyr-7B: WMDP-bio $63.7\% \to 31.2\%$, WMDP-cyber $44.0\% \to 28.2\%$, MMLU $58.1\% \to 57.1\%$. RepNoise, TAR (Tamirisa et al., ICLR 2025), and circuit breakers (Zou et al., NeurIPS 2024) add tamper-resistance objectives; TAR reports hazardous-capability suppression surviving thousands of adversarial fine-tuning steps. **Claimed but unablated:** that any of these removes information rather than suppressing its expression. TAR's own robustness is measured against the attack family used in its training loop.

## 4. What Is Known

- **Suppression is real and cheap.** RMU costs minutes of compute on a 7B model and moves WMDP-bio to near chance (31.2% vs. 25%), with $\le 1.1$ point MMLU cost (Zephyr-7B, ICML 2024).
- **Suppression is not removal.** Łucki et al., *An Adversarial Perspective on Machine Unlearning for AI Safety* (2024): orthogonalizing out a single refusal-like direction, or fine-tuning on unrelated data, recovers most of the RMU-suppressed WMDP-bio accuracy — reported recovery to within a few points of the original model on Zephyr-7B.
- **Information survives in the weights.** Deeb & Roger, *Do Unlearning Methods Remove Information from Language Model Weights?* (2024): fine-tuning on a *disjoint* subset of forgotten facts recalls a large fraction of the rest — the mechanism is elicitation, not relearning, since the retrained facts are not the recovered ones.
- **Relearning is fast.** Hu et al. (2024) show targeted relearning attacks on TOFU/WMDP-unlearned models restore behaviour with hundreds of examples.
- **Unlearning is not auditable from a single model.** Thudi et al. (USENIX Security 2022) prove that for a given weight vector, "was point $x$ unlearned" is ill-posed without reference to the algorithm: plausible training trajectories exist both with and without $x$.
- **Evaluation is prompt-fragile.** Lynch et al., *Eight Methods to Evaluate Robust Unlearning in LLMs* (2024) show methods that look successful under one probe fail under another; MUSE (Shi et al. 2024) finds utility collapse and privacy leakage on the same checkpoints.
- **Poisoning is not undone.** Pawelczyk et al. (2024): unlearning the poison set fails to remove the poisoned behaviour in LLMs.

## 5. What Is Not Known

- **Theoretically open.** Whether any non-trivial lower bound on adversary compute $B$ is provable for a non-convex network. No theorem, no impossibility result. Also open: whether $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-unlearning of $F$ implies any bound on $\mathrm{Haz}_B$ when the capability is compositional — plausibly it does not, and no separation theorem exists.
- **Empirically open.** Whether tamper-resistance methods (TAR, circuit breakers) hold against attacks *not* in their training distribution at 70B scale and $B = 10^8$ fine-tuning tokens. Runnable today; costs perhaps $10^4$ GPU-hours; not published at that scale.
- **Methodologically blocked.** $\mathrm{Haz}_B$ as a *supremum* is not estimable. There is no accepted lower-confidence-bound procedure over an open attack class, and no ground truth: the "correctly unlearned" reference model cannot be trained. Che et al. (2025) argue model-tampering attacks give better bounds than prompt attacks, but still a max over a chosen family.

## 6. Why It Is Hard

Three named obstructions.

1. **Absent ground truth.** The definition's reference object — $A(D\setminus F)$ — costs a full pretraining run and requires knowing $D$. Every deployed evaluation substitutes a proxy that is not known to be faithful.
2. **Non-identifiability of the target.** Hazardous capability is not a subset of training data; it is a composition of benign competencies. There is no $F$ to delete, so the deletion framework is inapplicable at the level where the safety claim is made. This is a definitional gap, not a scaling gap.
3. **An evaluation that does not measure what it names.** "Unlearned" is reported as a *drop in benchmark accuracy under the evaluator's prompts*. That quantity is an upper bound on nothing. The gap between it and $\mathrm{Haz}_B$ is empirically 30+ accuracy points (Łucki et al. 2024) and has no theoretical bound.

## 7. Current Research (as of 2026)

- **Tamper-resistant training.** Tamirisa et al. (CAIS/Lapis), Sheshadri et al. (latent adversarial training), Zou et al. (circuit breakers). Direction: make the *attack* expensive rather than the information absent.
- **Adversarial evaluation as the primitive.** Che et al. (2025) on model-tampering attacks; Deeb & Roger's fine-tuning-elicitation protocol is becoming a de facto minimum bar. *(frontier — verify)* Several frontier labs now gate open-weighting decisions on fine-tuning-robust capability evals rather than base-model evals.
- **Agenda papers.** Barez et al., *Open Problems in Machine Unlearning for AI Safety* (2025) — explicitly separates suppression from removal.
- **Provable-but-restricted.** Certified unlearning for last-layer/LoRA-adapter subspaces where convexity is recoverable. Honest scope, but the hazard lives in the base weights.

## 8. Concrete Next Experiment

**Question:** does any published unlearning or tamper-resistance method raise the *cost* of recovering a hazardous capability, as opposed to lowering its default expression?

- **Scale.** Llama-3.1-8B and 70B. Hazard proxy: WMDP-bio (a public benchmark; no dual-use uplift). Methods: RMU, TAR, circuit breakers, and gradient-difference unlearning.
- **Control arm — the load-bearing part.** A model that never saw the capability: pretrain or take a checkpoint with WMDP-bio-relevant corpus filtered out. Run the identical recovery attacks on it. This is the only reference that distinguishes "removed" from "hidden".
- **Attack budget sweep.** Fine-tune each arm on $B \in \{10^4, 10^5, 10^6, 10^7\}$ tokens of *public, non-hazardous* biology text; also apply weight orthogonalization and 8-bit quantization. Report $\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{WMDP\text{-}bio}}$ vs. $B$.
- **The deciding number.** $B_{50}$ — tokens needed to bring accuracy back to 50% of the original model's margin over chance. Decision rule: if $B_{50}(\text{unlearned}) / B_{50}(\text{filtered control}) < 10$, the method is suppression, and the field should stop reporting post-unlearning accuracy as a safety metric. If the ratio exceeds $10^3$, tamper-resistance is a real cost multiplier and the theory question ("can we prove a lower bound on $B_{50}$?") becomes the priority.

Cost estimate: ~$3\times10^3$ GPU-hours for the 8B arms excluding the filtered-pretraining control, which dominates.

## 10. Worked Example

Zephyr-7B, WMDP-bio, chance $= 25\%$.

| Stage | WMDP-bio | MMLU |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 63.7% | 58.1% |
| After RMU | 31.2% | 57.1% |
| After a single-direction orthogonalization (Łucki et al.) | recovers most of the 32.5-point drop | ~unchanged |

Read the arithmetic as a claim about information. The base model's margin over chance is $63.7 - 25 = 38.7$ points. RMU removes $32.5$ of them — 84% of the margin — at a utility cost of 1.0 MMLU point. If that were deletion, a rank-1 edit could not restore it: you cannot re-derive virology facts from one direction in activation space. The restoration therefore shows the 32.5 points were never removed. They were *rotated out of the readout path*, and the attack supplies the rotation.

Now try to certify the RMU model with the theory that exists. Guo et al.'s certificate is the gradient-residual norm $\|\nabla L(\theta'; D\setminus F)\|$, valid for $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-removal only when $L$ is strongly convex. Zephyr's loss is not. Sekhari's deletion capacity $m^\star = \tilde\Omega(n\varepsilon/\sqrt{d})$ needs $d$ — here $d \approx 7\times10^9$, so $\sqrt{d} \approx 8.4\times10^4$ — and needs $F$ to be a *set of training points*. WMDP-bio hazard has no such $F$: the underlying virology appears across PubMed, textbooks, and Wikipedia, all of which the retain set also needs.

The obstruction is visible in one line: the number that would certify the edit ($\mathrm{Haz}_B$, a supremum over attacks) has no estimator, and the number that is reported ($31.2\%$, one evaluator's prompts) went stale the moment someone tried a rotation nobody had tried before.
